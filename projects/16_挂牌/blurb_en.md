Retired fertilizer‑plant worker Zhou Jianguo watches the value of his family's sole asset — a 120‑square‑meter apartment he bought for 720,000 yuan — plummet to a 390,000‑yuan offer after eleven months on the market. At fifty‑eight, his 3,100‑yuan pension barely covers daily expenses, yet the apartment represents a lifetime of savings, renovations, and the years his son Zhou Yuan spent in the town before moving to the booming city of Jiangzhou. Now Yuan’s marriage hinges on an 800,000‑yuan down payment for a Jiangzhou flat, a sum that can only be raised by selling the county home. Market data shows first‑tier prices climbing while second‑ and third‑tier cities keep sliding, and the agent warns that even the current bid may vanish. Caught between preserving the family’s roots and securing his son’s future, Jianguo must decide whether to let go of the house that holds their history.
