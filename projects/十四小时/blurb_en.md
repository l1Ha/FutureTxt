In the megacity of 2126, every scarce public benefit — housing vouchers, cross‑district medical quotas, school enrollment slots — is funneled through the Sequence Hall, a blockchain‑backed queue that assigns each citizen a digital rank. The wealthy hire “stand‑ins” to hold their place for hours, while the underclass survives on day‑labor gigs that barely cover a night’s bunk in a cramped capsule dorm. Lin Xiu, forty‑eight, shoulders a chronic neck injury and spends fourteen hours a day standing in line for strangers, earning just enough for a mattress. Her twelve‑year‑old daughter, Xiao Yu, holds a temporary residency number that expires next Wednesday; the law demands a guardian appear in person at the service window, or the slot is lost and the girl must wait another year, missing the legal school‑entry age. Lin can afford to queue for anyone else, yet she cannot afford to queue for herself.
