At Minghu University’s "Smart Classrooms," ceiling-mounted hemispheres track four metrics—attendance, eyes-up rate, front-row occupancy, and interaction—reducing pedagogy to a real-time dashboard. Professor Zhou Jingxiu’s *Song Lyrics Seminar* languishes at a humiliating 38% eyes-up rate, second-to-last department-wide, earning him a disciplinary summons. His benchmark: Lin Yiming, a former student moved to tears by Zhou’s lecture on Li Qingzhao three decades ago, now a rising star whose metrics are flawless. Caught in the algorithmic crossfire is Jiang Xiaoman, a towering sophomore hunched in the back row, feigning notes while looping a bootleg recording of Zhou’s voice—"Ten years, dead and alive, both vast and vague"—on her phone. She needs 0.3 more points to secure the scholarship paying next semester’s tuition; Zhou needs the 8,000-yuan performance bonus tied to his scores for his wife’s targeted therapy drugs. As the midterm audit locks in grades, no one dares open rebellion. The Vice Dean needs quotas met; Lin needs to survive probation; Jiang needs tuition; and Zhou must find a way to inscribe a single, uncounted line of dignity for those with heads bowed inside the system’s exported "qualified" list.
