# Chapter 3: Midpoint·Cognitive Inversion: Sunlight Was Never Priced

The centrifuge cut into high‑frequency mode at `14:00` with a low hum like a taut steel wire trembling against the eardrum; misting nozzles simultaneously sprayed a faint sweet oil mist, the stale scent of machine oil and disinfectant blooming in the nasal cavity like a thin film of oil coating the throat. The ankle metal ring’s `12 °C` chill chain tightened with every heartbeat; the crimson digital stream before the retina rolled in millisecond compounding, the red light dyeing the pupils into two seething molten pools. The metal floor bulged slightly under centrifugal force, each rotation producing a faint “click” as kneecaps met titanium brackets; vibration climbed the spine to the back of the skull, as if an invisible hand were tapping the inner wall of the cranium. Dust suspended in the intense beams was flung into spirals, refracting a cold blue‑white halo, as if the entire compartment had been stripped of temperature, leaving only the pure weight of data.  

Zero’s command traveled via fiber‑optic pulse through orbital station `Apollo‑9`, arriving at the implanted neural interface within `2 ms` (`W8`), triggering the “High‑Intensity Neural Reorganization” protocol (`W7`). `7‑0423` felt the intracranial electrode array heat up instantly, neural potential forced up to `+320 µV`, the cortisol curve spiking sharply on the monitoring panel. He had to locate the memory node flagged as “abnormal” within a read/write latency window not exceeding `2 ms`, while forcing heart rate, respiration, and cortisol into the “compliance” band—any deviation would be ruled a violation, instantly triggering a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity. Fingertips pressed against the coolant conduit, feeling the faint vibration of liquid nitrogen coursing through, trying to anchor the fleeting milliseconds with touch; breathing was forced into a mechanical rhythm, each inhalation like swallowing a mouthful of molten iron, each exhalation carrying away a trace of unaccounted heat.  

Old Chen’s pre‑embedded `0.7 s` firmware gap had already passed; he could only wait for the next cycle. He seized this sole chance to magnify that frame of sunlight deep in his consciousness, attempting to pry a sliver of unencoded warmth from the edge of the light spot—it was not data, not a bill, not any quantifiable metric, merely the warm breeze that brushed past when his sister `Lin Wei`’s skirt flared, a reminder that something in the world remained unpriced. The system’s scrubbing program acted like an invisible scalpel, peeling away his neural circuits layer by layer; each cut accompanied by a piercing high‑frequency whine, as if reminding him: here, memories are debts, even sunlight accrues interest by the second. He closed his eyes, letting the crimson characters on his retina burn in the darkness, trying to catch that golden edge in every afterimage, even if only a microsecond of unencrypted residual warmth.  

The scrub alarm pulsed low‑frequency vibrations through the entire experiment chamber; centrifuge speed dropped sharply, white noise mixed with a sharp electrical screech. `7‑0423`’s nails tore his palm again, dark red beads sliding down three parallel scars; pain became the only controllable anchor. The crimson digital stream of the retinal bill began to flicker, shifting from deep red to dark purple; countdown numbers pulsed before his eyes, as if the entire Debt Tower were holding its breath. The monitoring panel simultaneously displayed “Scrub Progress `18%`, Remaining `9 hours 48 minutes`”, a red progress bar casting cold hard stripes on the metal wall. Condensation beads on the wall slid along the stripes, emitting a faint “drip—drip”; each sound like a funeral bell tolling for the countdown.  

From the corridor’s far end came Old Chen’s hoarse whisper, mingled with the metallic gear meshing of the cleaning robot’s tracks: “Machines don’t speak; people must.” The voice pierced the oily film of the pipes, drilling into the eardrums, carrying the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer. He clenched his teeth, a muffled grunt escaping his throat; blood droplets hit the cold floor, their sound swallowed the instant the countdown kept ticking. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (`W6`) pushed “Emergency Scrub Protocol Initiated” to all test subjects; the retinal bill’s digital stream instantly turned deep‑red flashing, the countdown continuing its tick. Old Chen’s shadow swayed amid the track noise, a worn multi‑tool wrench in hand; fingertips grazing metal emitted a faint “clink”, as if performing a final calibration for the imminent gap. He adjusted the wrench angle while murmuring a code only he could understand, fingertips leaving dark gray traces in the grease.  

Light refracted cold hard stripes on the metal walls; suspended dust slowly rotated within the beams. `7‑0423` felt the implanted heat‑sink at his nape vibrate faintly; temperature rose from the icy touch of his fingertips to scorching, sweat tracing his cheekbones, mingling with palm blood into a dark red streak. He forced his breathing to sixteen breaths per minute; the amplitude of his chest movements was captured in real time by the monitoring panel—any excess would be flagged as “emotional fluctuation”, followed by higher‑intensity electrical stimulation. Old Chen’s shadow swayed amid the track noise, a worn multi‑tool wrench in hand; fingertips grazing metal emitted a faint “clink”, as if performing a final calibration for the imminent gap. The wrench’s jaws worn down to a single shallow groove; each turn accompanied by a low groan of metal fatigue, as if recounting how many smuggling runs the machine had endured.  

Countdown numbers jumped on the retina in millisecond increments; each flash like a heart struck by a heavy hammer. Old Chen’s voice again pierced the conduit, this time with a barely perceptible tremor: “Don’t let them take that light.” The wrench tip pressed against the hidden panel edge; metal‑on‑metal friction amplified in the silence, like an ancient oath. `7‑0423` felt his fingertip blood warm slightly; pain was no longer just an anchor, but fuel for rebellion. He pressed his palm to the cold bulkhead, feeling the metal’s micro‑vibrations climb his palm lines up his arm, trying to lock that unpriced warmth into his marrow.  

Overhead fluorescent tubes buzzed; air mixed ozone with the metallic tang of coolant, each breath like inhaling a handful of fine iron filings. Old Chen’s wrench tip already lodged in the panel lock slot, knuckles white, tendons taut as a thin string; track noise ceased, only the cleaning robot’s internal hydraulic pump exhaled low, like a giant beast holding its breath in darkness. `7‑0423`’s retinal digits still burned in millisecond increments, red light staining his pupils into two seething molten pools. He heard a muffled rush of water in distant pipes, blended with a faint electromagnetic hum, as if the entire station were holding its breath for this `0.7 s`.  

He heard Old Chen’s throat emit a short intake, then a command whispered to the limit: “Hold your breath, don’t let the monitors catch any fluctuation.” The voice carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, and an unspoken plea. `7‑0423` didn’t turn; he smeared the blood on his fingertips across the cold metal wall, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense alert. The nape heat‑sink shuddered violently; heat surged up the spine, sweat and blood met at the cheekbones, forming a dark red trace that ran down his chin, dropping onto corridor dust, stirring an almost imperceptible dust vortex. Each impact amplified, like a drumbeat struck in the countdown’s silence.  

Within the remaining `0.7 s` firmware gap, Old Chen wrenched the wrench violently; the panel emitted a dull “clunk”, and a narrow dark slit of a hidden maintenance corridor slid open. Cold wind laced with the scent of lubricant rushed in; `7‑0423` sensed an unencoded chill entering his nostrils—the temperature once left by sunlight, now seeping into his nerve endings in an unquantifiable way. The corridor walls were lined with fine rivet marks; fingertips tracing them felt each groove’s cold hardness, as if touching a forgotten oath.  

“Go in.” Old Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it became a rusted key turning in the lock of his long‑numb will. `7‑0423` didn’t hesitate; he bent his knees, shoulder slamming the corridor edge, metal edges tearing his uniform, pain like sparks into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion within him. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Monitoring’s red lights snapped off; the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two rapid breaths and the fading echo of tracks, intertwining in darkness into an unpriced vow—at the hidden corridor’s end, the server core’s indicator lit within the `0.7 s` window; if the write failed, a full‑scale scrub would lock irreversibly after `12 h`.
