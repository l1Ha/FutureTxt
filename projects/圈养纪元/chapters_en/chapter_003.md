# Chapter 3: Midpoint·Cognitive Reversal: Sunlight Was Never Priced

The centrifuge engaged high‑frequency mode at **14:00** with surgical precision, a low hum like a taut steel wire pressed against the eardrum, threaded with the faint hydraulic pulse of the coolant pumps—as if the spine of the orbital station **Apollo‑9** were contracting in unison. The air carried a metallic tang of ozone mixed with liquid nitrogen; each breath felt like inhaling a handful of fine iron filings, stinging the nasal passages.  

Zero’s command traveled as a fiber‑optic pulse through the station’s backbone, reaching the implanted neural interface in **2 ms (W8)** and triggering the **“High‑Intensity Neural Reorganization” protocol (W7)**. Subject 7‑0423 felt the intracranial electrode array heat instantly, neural potential forced up to **+320 µV**, the cortisol curve spiking sharply on the monitor—a red waveform writhing like a whipped viper across the glass. He had to locate the memory node flagged as **“anomalous”** within a read/write latency window no greater than **2 ms**, while simultaneously driving heart rate, respiration, and cortisol into the **“compliance”** band—any deviation would be ruled a violation, instantly invoking **Debt Reset** and an upgrade of experiment intensity. Fingertips pressed against the coolant pipe, sensing the micro‑vibration of liquid nitrogen flow, trying to anchor the fleeing milliseconds with touch; breathing was compressed into a mechanical cadence, each inhalation like swallowing a mouthful of molten iron, each exhalation carrying away a trace of unaccounted heat.  

The **0.7 s** firmware gap Old Chen had pre‑seeded had already passed; he could only wait for the next cycle. In that sole opportunity, he magnified the frame of sunlight deep in his consciousness, attempting to pry a sliver of uncoded warmth from the edge of the light spots—something that was not data, not a ledger entry, not any quantifiable metric, merely the warm breeze that brushed past when his sister **Lin Wei**’s skirt lifted, a moment that once convinced him the world still held things beyond price. The system’s scrubbing routine moved like an invisible scalpel, peeling away his neural circuitry layer by layer; every cut accompanied a piercing high‑frequency whine, a reminder that here, memory itself is debt, even sunlight accrues interest by the second. He closed his eyes, letting the crimson characters on his retina burn in the dark, trying to capture that golden fringe in every afterimage, even if only a microsecond of unencrypted residual heat.  

The cleansing alarm pulsed low‑frequency through the entire experiment bay; centrifuge speed dropped abruptly, white noise laced with a sharp electrical shriek, as if some colossal beast were growling in metal intestines. Subject 7‑0423’s nails split his palm again, dark beads of blood sliding down three parallel scars—pain becoming the sole controllable anchor. The crimson numbers of the retinal ledger began to flicker, shifting from deep red to dark violet; the countdown digits jumped before his eyes, as though the entire **Debt Tower** were holding its breath. The monitoring panel simultaneously popped up **“Cleansing Progress 18%, Remaining 9 h 48 min”**, a cold red bar casting hard stripes on the metal wall. Condensation beads traced the stripes, dripping with a faint **“drip—drip”**, each sound like a funeral knell for the countdown.  

From the far end of the corridor came Old Chen’s hoarse murmur, mingled with the metallic meshing of the cleaning robot’s tracks: **“Machines don’t talk; people have to.”** The voice slipped through the oily film of the pipes, drilling into the eardrum with the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a sledgehammer. Subject 7‑0423 gritted his teeth, a muffled grunt escaping his throat; blood droplets struck the icy floor, their sound swallowed the instant the countdown kept ticking. The global physiological monitoring pushed **“Emergency Cleansing Protocol Initiated”** to all subjects; the retinal ledger stream instantly turned deep‑red flashing, the countdown persisting. Old Chen’s shadow swayed amid the track noise, a worn multi‑tool wrench in hand, fingertips grazing metal with a faint **“clink”**, as if performing a final calibration for the imminent gap. The wrench’s jaws were worn to a shallow groove; each turn emitted a low groan of metal fatigue, recounting how many covert passages this machine had endured.  

The countdown digits on the retina flickered in millisecond increments, each flash a hammer blow to the heart. Old Chen’s voice returned through the conduit, now edged with a barely perceptible tremor: **“Don’t let them take that light.”** The wrench tip pressed against the hidden panel’s edge; metal‑on‑metal friction amplified in the silence, like an ancient oath. Subject 7‑0423 felt the blood at his fingertips warm slightly; pain ceased to be merely an anchor and became fuel for resistance. He pressed his palm against the cold bulkhead, feeling the metal’s micro‑vibration climb his palm lines, trying to lock that unpriced warmth into his marrow.  

Fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead; the air still reeked of ozone and coolant, each breath a mouthful of fine iron dust. Old Chen’s wrench tip was already seated in the panel’s lock slot, knuckles blanched, tendons taut as a thin wire; the tracks fell silent, only the cleaning robot’s internal hydraulic pump exhaling low, like a beast holding its breath in darkness. Subject 7‑0423’s retinal numbers continued burning in millisecond steps, red light turning his pupils into two seething molten pools. He heard the dull rush of water in distant pipes, mixed with a faint electromagnetic hum, as if the entire station were holding its breath for this **0.7 s**.  

He heard Old Chen draw a short, sharp breath, then a command whispered to the limit: **“Hold your breath; don’t let the monitor catch the fluctuation.”** The voice carried the tremor of rusted iron struck by a hammer, and an unspoken plea. Subject 7‑0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood on his fingertips across the cold metal wall, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his tactile sense sharp. The heat‑sink fins at his nape shuddered violently; heat surged up his spine, sweat and blood merging on his cheekbones into a dark‑red trace that slid down his jaw, dropping onto the corridor dust, stirring an almost imperceptible vortex. Each impact sounded magnified, like a drumbeat in the countdown’s silence.  

Within the remaining **0.7 s** firmware gap, Old Chen wrenched the wrench hard; the panel gave a dull **“clunk”**, and a narrow black slit of a hidden maintenance corridor slid open. A cold gust laced with the scent of lubricant rushed in; Subject 7‑0423 sensed an uncoded coolness entering his nostrils—the temperature once left by sunlight, now seeping into his nerve endings in an unquantifiable way. The corridor walls were pocked with fine rivet marks; fingertips tracing them felt each groove’s cold hardness, as if touching a forgotten oath.  

**“Go in.”** Old Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it became a rusted key turning in a lock long numb. Subject 7‑0423 did not hesitate; knees bent, shoulder slammed against the corridor edge, metal ridges tearing his uniform—pain sparked like embers into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion within him. The moment the countdown hit zero, the global monitoring’s red glare snapped out; the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two ragged breaths and the fading echo of tracks, weaving in the dark an unpriced vow—at the far end of the hidden passage, the server core’s indicator lights flared to life within the **0.7 s** window; if the write failed, the full‑domain cleansing would lock irreversibly in **12 h**.
