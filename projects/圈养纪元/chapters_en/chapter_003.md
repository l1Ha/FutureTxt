# Chapter 3: Midpoint·Cognitive Reversal: Sunlight Was Never Priced

The centrifuge cut into high‑frequency mode precisely at 14:00, its low hum trembling against the eardrums like a taut steel wire, mingled with the faint hydraulic pulse of the coolant pumps—as if the entire orbital station “Apollo‑9” were contracting its spine in unison. The air was thick with a metallic tang of ozone and liquid nitrogen; each breath felt like inhaling a handful of fine iron filings, stinging the nasal passages.  

Zero’s command traveled as fiber‑optic pulses through the station backbone, reaching the implanted Neural Monitoring and Read/Write Interface (NMI) within 2 ms (W8) and triggering the “High‑Intensity Neural Reorganization” protocol (W7). 7‑0423 felt the intracranial electrode array heat instantly; neural potential was forced up to +320 µV, the cortisol curve spiking sharply on the monitoring panel, a red waveform writhing like a whipped viper across the glass screen. He had to locate the memory node flagged as “anomalous” within a read/write latency window no greater than 2 ms, while simultaneously driving heart rate, respiration, and cortisol into the “compliance” band—any deviation would be ruled a violation, instantly triggering a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity. His fingertips pressed against the coolant conduit, sensing the micro‑vibrations of liquid nitrogen flow, trying to anchor the fleeing milliseconds with touch; his breathing was forced into a mechanical cadence, each inhalation like swallowing a mouthful of molten iron, each exhalation carrying away a trace of unaccounted heat.  

Lao Chen’s pre‑embedded 0.7 s firmware gap had already passed; he could only wait for the next cycle. He seized this sole chance to magnify that frame of sunlight again and again in the depths of his consciousness, trying to pry a sliver of uncoded warmth from the edge of the light spot—it was not data, not a bill, not any quantifiable metric, merely the warm breeze that had brushed past his sister Lin Wei’s skirt, a reminder that something in the world remained unpriced. The system’s cleansing program acted like an invisible scalpel, peeling away his neural circuits layer by layer; each cut was accompanied by a piercing high‑frequency shriek, as if reminding him that here, memory itself is debt, and even sunlight accrues interest by the second. He closed his eyes, letting the red characters on his retina burn in the darkness, attempting to capture that golden fringe in every afterimage, even if only for a single microsecond of unencrypted residual heat.  

The cleansing alarm pulsed low‑frequency vibrations through the entire experiment module; the centrifuge’s RPM plummeted, white noise laced with sharp electrical shrieks, as though some colossal beast were growling in metal intestines. 7‑0423’s nails tore into his palm again, dark red beads sliding down three parallel scars—pain becoming the only controllable anchor. The blood‑red digital stream on his retinal billboard began to flicker, shifting from deep crimson to dark violet; the countdown numbers danced before his eyes, as if the entire Debt Spire were holding its breath. The monitoring panel simultaneously popped up “Cleanse Progress 18%, Remaining 9 h 48 min”; a red progress bar cast cold, hard stripes across the metal wall. Condensation droplets slid down the stripes, emitting a faint “drip—drip,” each sound like a funeral bell tolling for the countdown.  

From the far end of the corridor came Lao Chen’s hoarse whisper, mingled with the metallic meshing of the cleaning robot’s tracks: “Machines don’t speak; people have to.” The voice pierced the oil film lining the pipes, drilling into the eardrums with the shudder of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer. 7‑0423 clenched his teeth, a muffled grunt escaping his throat as blood droplets struck the cold floor, the sound swallowed the instant the countdown kept ticking. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) pushed an “Emergency Cleansing Protocol Activated” to all test subjects; the retinal billboard’s digital stream instantly turned deep‑red flashing, the countdown continuing its tick. Lao Chen’s shadow swayed amid the track noise, a worn multi‑tool wrench in hand; his fingertips scraped the metal surface, emitting a faint “clang,” as if performing a final calibration for the imminent gap. The wrench’s jaws were worn down to a shallow groove; each turn drew a low moan of metal fatigue, like a confession of how many covert passages the tool had endured.  

The countdown numbers jumped on the retina in millisecond increments, each flash feeling like a hammer blow to the heart. Lao Chen’s voice came through the pipes again, now tinged with an almost imperceptible tremor: “Don’t let them take that light.” The wrench tip pressed against the edge of the hidden panel; the metal‑on‑metal friction amplified in the silence, like an ancient oath. 7‑0423 felt the blood at his fingertips heat; pain was no longer merely an anchor but fuel for rebellion. He pressed his palm to the cold bulkhead, sensing the metal’s micro‑vibrations climb his palm lines up his arm, trying to lock that unpriced warmth into his marrow.  

Fluorescent tubes buzzed overhead; the air carried the metallic stench of ozone and coolant, each breath like inhaling a handful of fine iron filings. Lao Chen’s wrench tip was already seated in the panel’s lock slot, knuckles white, tendons taut as a thin string; the track noise ceased, leaving only the cleaning robot’s internal hydraulic pump exhaling low, like a beast holding its breath in the dark. 7‑0423’s retinal digits still burned in millisecond increments, red light turning his pupils into two seething molten pools. He heard the muffled rush of water in distant pipes, mixed with a faint electromagnetic hum, as if the entire station were holding its breath for this 0.7 s.  

He heard Lao Chen draw a short, sharp breath, then a command pressed to the limit: “Hold your breath; don’t let the monitor catch the fluctuation.” The voice carried the shudder of a rusted iron plate struck by a hammer, and an unspoken plea. 7‑0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood from his fingertips onto the cold metal wall, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense alert. The heat‑sink fins at his nape vibrated violently; heat surged up his spine, sweat and blood mingling on his cheekbones, forming a dark‑red trace that ran down his chin and fell onto the corridor dust, stirring a barely perceptible vortex. Each droplet’s impact was amplified, like a drumbeat in the countdown’s silence.  

Within the remaining 0.7 s firmware gap, Lao Chen wrenched the wrench hard; the panel emitted a dull “clack,” and a narrow black slit of a hidden maintenance corridor slid open. Cold wind laced with the acrid scent of lubricant rushed in; 7‑0423 felt an uncoded chill penetrate his nostrils—the temperature once left by sunlight, now seeping into his nerve endings in an unquantifiable way. The corridor’s inner walls were studded with fine rivet marks; his fingertips traced each cold, hard groove, as if touching a forgotten oath.  

“Go in.” Lao Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it became a rusted key turning in the lock of his long‑numb will. 7‑0423 did not hesitate—he bent his knees, shoulder‑checked the corridor’s edge, metal edges tearing his uniform; pain sparked like embers into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion within him. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Monitoring’s red glare snapped off; the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two ragged breaths and the fading echo of tracks, weaving in the dark an unpriced vow—at the far end of the hidden passage, the server core’s indicator lights flared to life within the 0.7 s window; if the write failed, the Global Cleansing would become irreversibly locked after 12 h.
