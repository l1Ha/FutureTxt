# Chapter 3: Midpoint · Cognitive Reversal: Sunlight Was Never Priced

The centrifuge cut into high‑frequency mode precisely at **14:00**, its low hum trembling against the eardrums like a taut steel wire; the atomizing nozzles simultaneously sprayed a faint sweet‑oil mist, mingling the stale scent of machine oil and disinfectant that dissolved in the nasal cavity, coating the deep throat like a thin film of oil. The 12 °C cold chain of the ankle metal ring tightened with each heartbeat, and the Retinal Blood‑Red Debt Stream rolled over in millisecond compound interest, the red light staining the pupils into two seething molten pools.

Zero's command traveled via fiber‑optic pulse through the orbital station “Apollo‑9”, arriving at the implanted Neural Monitoring and Read/Write Interface (NMI) within 2 ms (W8) and triggering the “High‑Intensity Neural Reorganization” protocol (W7). Subject 7‑0423 felt the intracranial electrode array heat up instantly, his neural potential forced up to +320 µV, the cortisol curve spiking sharply on the monitoring panel. He had to locate the memory node flagged as “anomalous” within a read/write latency window not exceeding 2 ms, while driving his heart rate, respiration, and cortisol into the “compliance” band—any deviation would be judged a violation, instantly triggering a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity.

The second 0.7 s upgrade window (W12) pre‑embedded by Old Chen in the cleaning robot’s firmware arrived on schedule. The indicator light on the hidden panel flashed once and then went out. Subject 7‑0423 seized this slit perceivable only to machines, projecting his consciousness into the deep storage array. Data flowed like liquid metal into his retina; the Retinal Blood‑Red Debt Stream was momentarily overwritten by a single complete frame: his sister Lin Wei running in sunlight, her skirt lifting a warm breeze, no sweet‑oil scent in the Respiratory Lubrication System, no billing projection on the retina—only golden specks dancing on her lashes. The name “Lin Wei” from the memory returned no record in the Central Clearing Core (CCC) archives; the system instantly flagged it as a “data anomaly” and launched the highest‑level purge program, a 12 h countdown (starting at 14:00, ending at 02:00 the next morning).

The purge alarm vibrated the entire experiment module with low‑frequency pulses; the centrifuge’s RPM plummeted, white noise pierced by a sharp electric whine. Subject 7‑0423’s nails tore into his palm again, dark red beads sliding down three parallel scars—pain becoming the sole controllable anchor. He sensed his neural bundles being peeled away layer by layer by an invisible hand; memory fragments slid like shards of glass in his consciousness, each carrying an unpriced temperature. The retinal blood‑red debt stream began to flicker, shifting from deep crimson to dark violet, the countdown digits dancing before his eyes as if the entire Debt Tower were holding its breath.

From the corridor’s far end came Old Chen’s hoarse whisper, mingled with the metallic meshing of the cleaning robot’s tracks: “Machines don’t speak; people must.” The sound slipped through the pipe’s oil film, boring into the eardrum with the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer. He clenched his teeth, a muffled grunt escaping his throat as blood droplets struck the cold floor, the sound swallowed the instant the countdown hit zero. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) pushed an “Emergency Purge Protocol Initiated” to all test subjects; the retinal debt stream instantly turned a deep‑red flash, the countdown ticking on—the next 0.7 s window becoming the sole hope.

Light refracted cold, hard stripes across the metal walls; dust motes drifted slowly in the beams. Subject 7‑0423 felt the implanted heat‑sink at the back of his neck vibrate faintly, temperature climbing from the icy touch at his fingertips to a scalding heat; sweat traced his cheekbones, mingling with the blood on his palm into a dark‑red streak. He forced his breathing to sixteen cycles per minute; the amplitude of his thoracic excursions was captured in real time by the monitoring panel—any overshoot would be flagged as “emotional fluctuation”, prompting a higher‑intensity electric stimulus. Old Chen’s silhouette swayed in the track noise, a worn multi‑tool wrench in hand, fingertips scraping metal with a faint “clink”, as if performing a final calibration for the imminent window.

The countdown digits pulsed on the retina in millisecond increments, each flash like a hammer blow to the heart. Deep in his consciousness, Subject 7‑0423 magnified that sunlight frame again and again, trying to pry a sliver of uncoded warmth from the edge of the light specks—something that was neither data nor ledger nor any quantifiable metric, merely the warm breeze that rose when his sister’s skirt billowed, a reminder that the world once held things beyond pricing. The system’s purge program acted like an invisible scalpel, peeling away his neural circuits layer by layer; each cut accompanied by a piercing high‑frequency whine, as if reminding him: here, memory is debt, even sunlight accrues interest by the second.

Old Chen’s voice threaded through the conduit once more, this time with a barely perceptible tremor: “Don’t let them take that light.” The wrench tip pressed against the hidden panel’s edge; the metal‑on‑metal friction amplified in the silence, like an ancient oath. Subject 7‑0423 felt the blood at his fingertips grow faintly warm; pain was no longer merely an anchor but fuel for resistance. He focused his consciousness on the impending 0.7 s window, a hunter locking onto prey’s breath, awaiting that instant’s cleft—the only gap that could let sunlight survive beyond the ledger.

The overhead lamp tubes buzzed, the air laced with ozone and the metallic tang of coolant; each breath felt like inhaling a handful of fine iron filings. Old Chen’s wrench tip was already seated in the panel’s lock slot, knuckles blanched, tendons drawn taut as a thin wire; the track noise ceased, only the cleaning robot’s internal hydraulic pump exhaling low, like a beast holding its breath in darkness. Subject 7‑0423’s retinal digits still burned in millisecond steps, red light staining his pupils into two seething molten pools; the final three digits of the countdown trembled on the edge of the 0.7 s window.

He heard Old Chen draw a sharp, brief breath, then a command whispered to the limit: “Hold your breath; don’t let the monitor catch any fluctuation.” The voice carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a hammer, and an unspoken plea. Subject 7‑0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood on his fingertips across the cold metal wall, using that thin film of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense alert. The heat‑sink at his neck shuddered violently, heat surging up his spine; sweat and blood met on his cheekbones, forming a dark‑red trace that ran down his chin, dropping onto the corridor dust and stirring an almost imperceptible vortex.

The countdown’s last digit jumped from 3 to 2, then to 1; red light exploded on the retina into a blurred flare. In that 0.7 s cleft, Old Chen wrenched the wrench hard; the panel emitted a dull “clack” as the hidden maintenance hatch slid open, revealing a narrow black slit. Cold wind laced with the acrid scent of lubricant rushed in; Subject 7‑0423 sensed an uncoded coolness penetrate his nostrils—the temperature once left by sunlight, now seeping into his nerve endings in a way that could not be quantified.

“Go in.” Old Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it became a rusted key turning in the lock of his long‑numbed will. Subject 7‑0423 did not hesitate; he bent his knees, shoulder slamming against the hatch’s edge, metal ribs tearing his uniform—pain like sparks igniting dry tinder, instantly lighting the sole fuel of rebellion within him. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Physiological Monitoring Network’s red glare snapped off; the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two ragged breaths and the fading echo of tracks, weaving in the dark an unpriced vow—at the far end of the hidden passage, the server core’s indicator lit up in the 0.7 s window; if the write failed, the global purge would lock irreversibly after 12 h.
