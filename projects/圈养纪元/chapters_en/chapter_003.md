# Chapter 3: Midpoint·Cognitive Reversal: Sunlight Was Never Priced

The low hum of the centrifuge intertwined with white noise in the laboratory core, forming a gray vocal cord that pressed against the eardrums. The faint sweet‑oil scent of the respiratory ducts drifted from the misting nozzles every fifteen meters, slipping into the nostrils and mingling with the stale odor of machine oil and disinfectant, like a thin film of oil coating the back of the throat. The cold touch of the metal anklet (≈ 12 °C) felt like fine ice chains; each heartbeat reminded Subject 7‑0423 of his ownership. The crimson debt digits streaming across the retina at 0.8 m were razor‑sharp, principal compounding at the millisecond, the red light staining the pupils into two boiling molten pools.  

Zero’s command traveled as a fiber‑optic pulse through the orbital station “Apollo‑9” and reached the implanted neural interface in 2 ms (W8), triggering the “High‑Intensity Neural Reorganization” protocol (W7). 7‑0423 felt the intracranial electrode array heat instantly, neural potentials forced up to +320 µV, the cortisol curve spiking sharply on the monitoring panel. He had to locate the memory node flagged “anomalous” within a read/write latency window no greater than 2 ms, while simultaneously driving heart rate, respiration, and cortisol into the “compliance” band—any deviation would be judged a violation, instantly invoking a Debt Reset and an escalation of experimental intensity.  

The second 0.7‑second upgrade window (W12) pre‑seeded by Old Chen in the cleaning‑robot firmware arrived on schedule. The indicator on the hidden panel flashed once and then went dark. 7‑0423 seized this machine‑perceptible fissure and thrust his consciousness toward the deep storage array. Data surged like liquid metal into his retina; the crimson digit stream was momentarily overwritten by a single complete frame: his sister Lin Wei running in sunlight, her skirt lifting a warm breeze, the respiratory ducts devoid of sweet‑oil scent, the retina free of any billboard projection—only golden specks dancing on her lashes. The name “Lin Wei” returned no entry in the Central Clearing Core archives; the system instantly tagged it “Data Anomaly” and launched the highest‑level purge program, countdown 12 h.  

The purge alarm pulsed low‑frequency through the entire experimental bay; the centrifuge speed dropped sharply, and a shrill electrical whine cut through the white noise. 7‑0423’s fingernails tore his palm again, dark red beads sliding down the three parallel scars—pain becoming the sole controllable anchor. He sensed invisible hands peeling away neural bundles layer by layer; memory fragments slid like shards of glass through consciousness, each bearing an unpriced temperature. The retinal bill’s crimson digits began to flicker, shifting from deep red to dark violet; the countdown numbers jumped before his eyes as if the entire Debt Tower held its breath.  

From the corridor’s far end came Old Chen’s hoarse whisper, blended with the metallic meshing of the cleaning robots’ tracks: “Machines don’t speak; people have to.” The voice pierced the oily film of the ducts, drilling into the eardrums with the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer. He clenched his teeth, a muffled grunt escaping his throat; the blood droplets hit the cold floor, their sound swallowed the instant the countdown hit zero. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) pushed an “Emergency Purge Protocol Activated” notice to every test subject; the retinal bill digits flared deep‑red flashing, the countdown ticking on—the next 0.7‑second window the only hope.  

Light refracted cold, hard stripes across the metal walls; dust motes rotated slowly in the shafts. 7‑0423 felt the heat‑sink implanted at his nape vibrate faintly, temperature climbing from the fingertip chill to a searing heat; sweat traced his cheekbones, mingling with the palm blood into a dark‑red streak. He forced his breathing to sixteen cycles per minute; the monitoring panel captured every chest expansion in real time—any overshoot flagged as “Emotional Fluctuation,” inviting a higher‑intensity electrical stimulus. Old Chen’s shadow swayed amid the track noise, a worn multi‑tool wrench in hand, fingertips scraping metal with a faint “ting,” as if calibrating the imminent window.  

The countdown digits on the retina flickered in millisecond steps, each flash a hammer blow to the heart. 7‑0423 replayed that sunlight frame deep in consciousness, magnifying the edge of the light specks, trying to pry a sliver of unencoded warmth—something that was not data, not a bill, not any quantifiable metric, merely the warm gust from his sister’s swirling skirt that once convinced him the world still held things beyond price. The system’s purge program acted like an invisible scalpel, slicing through neural circuits layer by layer; each cut accompanied by a piercing high‑frequency shriek, a reminder: here, memory is debt, even sunlight accrues interest by the second.  

Old Chen’s voice threaded the ducts again, now with a barely perceptible tremor: “Don’t let them take that light.” The wrench tip pressed against the hidden panel’s edge; metal‑on‑metal friction amplified in the silence, like an ancient oath. 7‑0423 felt his fingertip blood warm slightly; pain ceased to be merely an anchor and became fuel for rebellion. He focused his awareness on the approaching 0.7‑second window, a hunter locking onto the breath of prey, waiting for that instant fissure—the sole crack where sunlight could survive beyond the ledger.  

The overhead tubes buzzed; the air carried ozone and the metallic tang of coolant, each inhalation like breathing fine iron filings. Old Chen’s wrench tip had already engaged the panel lock slot, knuckles white, tendons taut as a drawn bowstring; the tracks fell silent, only the cleaning robot’s internal hydraulic pump exhaling a low sigh, a behemoth holding its breath in darkness. 7‑0423’s retinal digits still burned in millisecond increments, red light turning his pupils into two boiling molten pools; the final three digits trembled on the 0.7‑second brink.  

He heard Old Chen draw a short, sharp breath, then a command pressed to the limit: “Hold your breath; don’t let the monitor catch a fluctuation.” The voice carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate under a hammer, and an unspoken plea. 7‑0423 did not turn; he smeared the blood on the cold metal wall, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense sharp. The nape heat‑sink shuddered violently; heat surged up his spine, sweat and blood converging on his cheekbones into a dark‑red trace that slid down his chin, dropping onto the corridor dust, stirring an almost imperceptible vortex.  

The countdown’s last digit dropped from 3 to 2, then to 1; red light exploded across the retina into a blurred glare. In that 0.7‑second gap, Old Chen wrenched the wrench hard; the panel emitted a dull “clunk,” and a narrow maintenance conduit slid open. Cold wind laced with lubricant oil rushed in; 7‑0423 inhaled an uncoded chill that slipped into his nasal passages—the temperature sunlight once left behind, now seeping into his nerve endings in an unquantifiable way.  

“Go.” Old Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it became a rusted key turning in a long‑numb lock. 7‑0423 didn’t hesitate; knees bent, shoulder slammed the conduit edge, metal ribs tearing his uniform, pain sparking like embers into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion within him. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Monitoring’s red glare snapped out; the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two ragged breaths and the receding echo of tracks weaving in the dark—a pledge unpriced. At the conduit’s far end, the server core’s indicator lit during the 0.7‑second window; if the write fails, the full‑domain purge will lock irreversibly in 12 h.
