# Chapter 4: Darkest Hour: Purge Countdown

A sudden burst of ozone odor erupted from the ventilation duct, mingling with the acrid scent of alloy melting under a laser cutter, riding the corridor's slight negative pressure straight into the nostrils. Emergency lights on the walls flickered a ghastly white strobe, slicing Lao Chen's silhouette behind a hidden panel into fragmented, intermittent shadows; light spots jumped across the metal bulkhead like a swarm of out‑of‑focus fireflies struggling in the darkness. The ankle metal ring still seeped a 12 °C chill into his veins.  

7‑0423 crouched at the junction of the habitation module and the corridor. Three parallel scars on his palm oozed a dark red; his fingernails had scored a shallow groove in the metal wall, metal filings lodged like fine sand beneath the nail beds, and every breath carried a metallic rust taste. He used the peak of pain to force his heart rate, cortisol, and neural potential into the “compliance” band; any deviation would be flagged as a violation, instantly triggering a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) uploaded each breath and micro‑tremor in real time; red and green indicator lights blinked at the edge of his retina, like countless microscopic judges delivering silent verdicts.  

The data port behind Lao Chen's left ear pulsed with a faint glow, its oxidized layer catching a dull amber under the emergency lights, like a copper plaque corroded by time. He tapped the panel in a low voice: “The window’s still open—don’t let the monitors catch any fluctuation.” His voice trembled like a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, echoing through the metal duct with a barely perceptible rasp. 7‑0423 didn’t turn; he merely smeared the blood from his fingertips onto the cold metal wall, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile awareness sharp. The heat‑sink fins at the nape of his neck vibrated violently; a surge of heat climbed his spine, sweat and blood mingling on his cheekbones, forming dark‑red trails that dripped from his chin, stirring barely visible dust vortices that glittered like miniature nebulae in the dim light.  

In the mere 0.7 seconds of the firmware upgrade window, he wrote his sister Lin Wei’s running memory into the core redundancy zone.  

At that instant, Lao Chen wrenched the wrench; the panel gave a dull “clack,” and a narrow black slit of a hidden maintenance passage slid open. A cold gust laden with the scent of lubricant hit their faces; 7‑0423 felt an uncoded chill slip into his nostrils—a temperature once left by sunlight, now seeping into his nerve endings in an unquantifiable way, like a slender optical fiber laying new pathways inside him.  

“Go in.” Lao Chen’s voice no longer trembled, like a rusted key turning in a long‑numb lock of will. 7‑0423 didn’t hesitate; he bent his knees, shoulder‑checked the passage edge, metal edges tearing his uniform, pain flaring like sparks into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion within him. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Monitoring’s red lights snapped out, the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two men’s ragged breaths and the fading echo of tracks, weaving an unpriced vow in the darkness that reverberated through every weld seam of the duct.  

The passage walls were coated with a thin layer of condensation; fingertips made a faint “tap” on contact, droplets bursting into microscopic cold mist that instantly crystallized into fine ice needles at the tips. 7‑0423’s knee struck a protruding pipe brace; bone met metal with a dull thud, pain shooting up the femur to his brain, yet it sharpened his awareness of every muscle contraction, as if each impact rewrote the ownership of his body. Lao Chen led the way, his tool‑pack swaying; wrench, fiber‑optic splicer, worn insulating tape clattered in the tight space, producing a sporadic metal percussion that bounced back and forth in the duct like a wordless elegy.  

“Easy, there’s a pressure valve ahead,” Lao Chen glanced back, the reflective strip on his mask flashing under the weak emergency light, its edge tracing a thin blue line. “Turn it open; the pressure will push us into the next maintenance shaft.” His tone was steady, yet each syllable carried an unmistakable command.  

7‑0423 nodded, his throat rasping a reply, vocal cords grinding a gritty texture in the dry air. His fingertips found the cold threads; the ridges of his fingertip skin etched shallow marks on the metal, a faint vibration traveling up his nerves to his arm. He forced the valve open; it shrieked a long hiss as high‑pressure gas blasted out, swirling white mist that instantly dropped the passage temperature to near zero. The cold pierced like countless fine needles; pores snapped shut, breathing grew harder, his lungs whistling faintly in the freeze, yet he didn’t stop, stumbling forward through the mist, boots squeaking faintly on the thin frost, each step claiming another inch of freedom.  

Ahead, the maintenance shaft entrance was sealed by a heavy alloy grille; its weld points showed dark‑red rust stains, like old wounds bleeding in the dark. Lao Chen drew the laser cutter; a blue‑violet beam traced a slender path through the darkness, its hiss resembling nocturnal insects gnawing wood. The ozone scent from the cutting head instantly filled the cramped space, sharp enough to tighten the throat. High heat melted the grille edges; molten metal droplets fell with a crisp “ding,” then were swept away by the airflow, turning into tiny gleaming specks flickering in the mist.  

“Hurry, time’s running out.” Lao Chen’s voice was shredded by the airflow, yet it pierced the eardrums; each word struck like a hammer on metal.  

7‑0423 surged into the shaft; his body compressed like a spring in the narrow vertical well, limbs scraping with a screech, the grooves in the metal walls leaving pale white marks on his elbows and knees, as if each squeeze recorded the price of escape. Sensors on the shaft walls blinked a faint green, logging each passage; the green light formed a faint halo at the edge of his retina, like a ghost’s fingerprint confirming identity in the night. Each spinal impact emitted a soft “click,” reminding him this body still belonged to him; every tremor affirmed the reality of his existence.  

The hatch at the bottom of the shaft slid open at the last moment, revealing a dim blue glow and rows of silent server racks. Fans rotated low, emitting a mix of warm air and ozone; heat condensed into a thin sheen of sweat on his palms, and a faint current tingled when his fingertips touched the metal surface. The instant 7‑0423 landed, his knees struck the metal deck again, pain surging like electricity through his whole body; his gaze locked onto the console ahead, indicator lights pulsing at a near‑heartbeat rhythm, red and green alternating as if timing the final decision.  

The holo‑screen flashed blood‑red characters: “Purge Progress 65%, Remaining 4 hours 12 minutes”; the numbers jittered at the screen’s edge like an EKG trembling at the limit. Lao Chen’s fingers flew, keystrokes faster than a heartbeat; each press accompanied a faint electrical click, the mechanical shafts giving a crisp “click” under his fingertips, leaving brief luminous trails on the keys.  

“Upload complete,” Lao Chen barked, breath ragged, a barely perceptible curve lifting the corner of his mouth; a flash of unspoken relief crossed his eyes. “The data package has been written to the core redundancy zone; the monitors cannot roll it back.”  

7‑0423 felt the crimson digital stream on his retina suddenly freeze; the debt ledger’s compound interest halted its climb, the interest‑rate needle locked as if in amber. He drew a deep breath of ozone‑mixed cold air; his chest expanded for the first time in ages, as though a gas named “freedom” were slowly filling his lobes, carrying a faint metallic sweetness that spread through his throat.  

On the distant monitoring wall, a blue flash pulsed in the iris‑projection ring of Vira’s right eye—she had pressed the delayed‑report key. The blue light then died, like a dead star marking this unrecorded gap, leaving a silent blind spot in the endless data flood.
