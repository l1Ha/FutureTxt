# Chapter 4: Darkest Hour – The Purge Countdown

The faint sweet oil scent of the respiratory ducts was cut off at the corridor’s end, replaced by a dry, metallic rust odor—like rusted screws grinding in vacuum, tinged with the sourness of old lubricant. The cold bite of the ankle cuffs against skin (≈ 12 °C) felt like fine ice chains; each heartbeat pushed the chill to the fingertips, which tingled as if countless microscopic needles were pricking the veins. The retinal debt bill still scrolled 0.8 m away, principal 1.2 × 10⁵ CR compounding per millisecond, interest 0.00012 %/s (W2) driving the numbers deeper into crimson; the red digits’ edges trembled in the visual field, a strobe synced to the pulse. The uniform whir of cleaning robot 07‑T (≈ 38 dB) vanished, leaving only the low‑frequency hum of the ventilation ducts, as if the whole floor were holding its breath; dust settled on the duct walls quivered with the airflow. The bitter aftertaste of the mandatory medication lingered on the tongue root, the metallic chew resistance of the breakfast nutrient paste (1.8 N·mm) still emitting a faint “crunch” between teeth, releasing a whisper of iron‑rust as it melted on the tongue. Laboratory white noise and the distant centrifuge drone seeped in like a gray vocal cord pressed against the eardrum; each spin‑up of the rotor sent an imperceptible vibration through the floor, climbing the bones of the feet to the spine.  

Subject 7‑0423 crouched at the junction of the habitation pod and the corridor, three parallel scars on his palm oozing dark red, fingernails having gouged a shallow groove in the metal wall—metal filings like fine sand lodged under the nails. He used the pain peak to clamp his heart rate, cortisol, and neural potential into the “compliance” band; any deviation would be flagged as a violation, instantly triggering a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity (W7). The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) streamed every breath, every micro‑tremor in real time; the data flow was adjudicated millisecond by millisecond in the Compute Palace of the Central Clearing Core (CCC), red‑green indicators flashing at the retinal periphery like countless silent judges.  

Maintainer “Old Chen” flickered behind a concealed panel, the worn data port behind his left ear pulsing faintly, its oxidized rim glowing dull amber under the emergency lights. He tapped the panel in a low voice: “The window’s still open—don’t let the monitors catch a fluctuation.” His words carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, each syllable echoing like a reverberation inside a metal pipe. Subject 7‑0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood on his fingertips across the cold metal wall, borrowing that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense sharp. The heat‑sink fins at his nape shuddered violently; heat surged up his spine, sweat and blood mingling on his cheekbones, forming dark‑red traces that fell from his chin, stirring an almost invisible dust vortex that spun once in the light beam before dying.  

The final countdown digit jumped from 3 to 2, then to 1; red light exploded across the retina into blurred spots ringed with a pale violet halo. The System Upgrade Window (W12) remained open for only 0.7 s—the sole interval permitting unauthorized data writes. Subject 7‑0423 seized this machine‑perceivable slit, projecting his consciousness into the deep storage array. The data stream surged like liquid metal into his vision; the crimson digit stream was instantly overwritten by a single complete frame: his sister Lin Wei running in sunlight, her skirt lifting a warm breeze, no sweet oil in the respiratory ducts, no retinal billboard—only golden specks dancing on her lashes, sunlight gilding her hair amber. The name “Lin Wei” returned no entry in the CCC archives, only a blank record silently mocking from the database.  

In that 0.7‑second gap, Old Chen wrenched the wrench; the panel gave a dull “clack,” and a narrow black slit of a hidden maintenance shaft slid open. Cold air laced with lubricant oil rushed in; Subject 7‑0423 felt an uncoded coolness pierce his nostrils—the temperature once left by sunlight, now seeping into nerve endings in an unquantifiable way, like a cold blade lightly scoring the cortex of memory.  

“Go in.” Old Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it was a rusted key turning in a long‑numbed lock, flat yet bearing an undeniable weight. Subject 7‑0423 did not hesitate; he bent his knees, shoulder‑checked the shaft’s edge, the metal ridge tearing his uniform—pain flared like sparks into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Monitoring red lights snapped out; the corridor fell back into dead silence, only the two ragged breaths and the fading tread of tracks weaving an unpriced oath in the dark.  

The shaft’s inner wall was coated with a thin film of condensation; fingertips touching it produced a soft “pop,” droplets bursting into microscopic cold mist. Subject 7‑0423’s knee struck a protruding pipe brace, bone on metal yielding a dull thud; the shock raced up the femur to the brain, yet sharpened his awareness of every muscle contraction, fibers tightening like drawn strings in the dim light. Old Chen led, his tool‑pack swaying—wrench, fiber‑fusion splicer, worn insulating tape—clanking a sporadic metallic chorus, each impact reverberating as short echoes in the narrow tube.  

“Don’t rush, there’s a pressure valve ahead,” Old Chen glanced back, his faceplate’s reflective strip flashing a thin blue line in the weak emergency glow. “Turn it; the pressure will push us to the next maintenance well.”  

Subject 7‑0423 nodded, his throat rasping a reply, vocal cords grinding like gravel in dry air. Fingertips found the cold threads, ridges imprinting shallow marks on the metal; the minute surface irregularities transmitted fine vibrations to his pads. He twisted hard—the valve shrieked after long disuse, high‑pressure gas jetting out, spiraling white fog that instantly dropped the shaft temperature near zero. The cold stabbed like innumerable needles, pores snapping shut, breathing growing harder, yet he did not stop; his boots slipped on the thin frost, emitting a faint “squeak.”  

Ahead, the maintenance well mouth was sealed by a heavy alloy grille, its welds blooming dark‑red rust like dried blood scabs under the lights. Old Chen drew a laser cutter; a violet‑blue beam traced a slender path in the darkness, hissing like nocturnal insects gnawing wood; ozone from the cutting head instantly flooded the cramped space. The grille’s edges melted, molten droplets falling with a crisp “ding,” then scattered by the airflow, each droplet carving a brief luminous arc before extinguishing.  

“Hurry, the window’s down to 0.3 s.” Old Chen’s words were shredded by the gust but pierced the eardrums, each syllable striking the same spot on the heart.  

Subject 7‑0423 lunged into the well, his body compressed like a spring in the tight vertical shaft; limbs scraped a screeching chorus against the metal, leaving shallow white marks on elbows and knees. Wall sensors blinked a faint green, logging each passage; the green formed a thin halo at the retinal edge. Each spinal impact emitted a soft “click,” reminding him this body still belonged to him, if only for an instant; each impact reinforced the conviction: pain is proof of freedom.  

The bottom hatch slid open at the last second, revealing a dim blue glow and rows of silent server racks. Fans spun low, exhaling a blend of heat and ozone—an industrial breath; heat condensed into a thin sheen of sweat on his palm. Subject 7‑0423 landed, knee striking the metal floor again; the shock surged through him like current, eyes locked on the control console ahead, its indicator lights pulsing at a near‑heartbeat rhythm.  

The holo‑screen flashed “Purge 99 % complete” in blood‑red; the countdown digits flickered wildly between 0.7 s and 0.6 s, edges ghosting from the refresh rate. Old Chen’s fingers flew, keystrokes faster than a pulse, each press accompanied by a faint electrical whisper, as if drumming an unknown rite; the mechanical switches clicked crisply under his fingertips, each sound amplified in the quiet server room.  

“Upload complete,” Old Chen shouted, fatigue and elation intertwined, a hoarse laugh threading his voice. “The data packet is written to the core redundancy zone; monitoring cannot roll back.”  

Subject 7‑0423 felt the retinal crimson digit stream freeze; the debt ledger’s compounding halted, the interest needle locked in temporal amber, the red numbers ceasing their crawl—as if the universe itself had held its breath. He inhaled a lungful of ozone‑cold air; for the first time in ages his chest expanded slightly, as though a gas named “freedom” were slowly filling the alveoli, the cilia trembling in the faint draft.  

From afar, the orbital station Apollo‑9 emitted a low‑frequency resonance; Auditor “Zero”’s facial projection bore scrolling crimson text: **Purge 99 % complete**, countdown 0.7 s to final lock. The resonance wave set the metal floor trembling faintly, the vibration traveling up through the soles like the earth whispering: it isn’t over yet.
