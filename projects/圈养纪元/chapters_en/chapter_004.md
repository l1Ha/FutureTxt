# Chapter 4: Darkest Moment: Cleansing Countdown

A sudden blast of ozone, laced with the acrid scent of laser‑cut alloy, surged down the corridor’s slight negative pressure straight into the nostrils; the Ankle Metal Ring still gripped the flesh at 12 °C, each heartbeat driving the cold like fine ice chains toward the fingertips. Emergency lights on the bulkhead flickered a sickly white strobe, slicing Old Chen’s silhouette behind the hidden panel into a series of broken shadows, the light spots dancing on the metal wall like a swarm of out‑of‑focus fireflies struggling in darkness.  

Protagonist 7‑0423 crouched at the junction of the habitation pod and the corridor, three parallel scars on his palm oozing dark red, a shallow groove scored into the bulkhead by his fingernails, metal shavings lodged like fine sand beneath the nails; every breath carried the metallic tang of rust. He used the pain peak to force his heart rate, cortisol, and neural potential into the “compliance” band—any deviation would be flagged as a violation, instantly triggering a Debt Reset and an experiment‑intensity upgrade. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) uploaded each breath, each micro‑tremor in real time; red‑green indicator lights blinked at the edge of his retina, like countless microscopic judges delivering silent verdicts.  

Old Chen’s left‑ear data interface pulsed a faint glow; its oxidation layer caught a dim amber under the emergency lights, like a copper plaque corroded by time. He tapped the panel in a low voice: “The window is still open; don’t let the monitoring catch any fluctuation.” The voice sounded like a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, reverberating in the metal duct with a barely perceptible rasp. 7‑0423 didn’t turn, merely wiped the blood from his fingertips onto the cold bulkhead, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense sharp. The heat‑sink fins at his nape shuddered violently, heat crawling up his spine; sweat and blood mingled on his cheekbones, forming dark‑red trails that fell to his chin, stirring an almost imperceptible vortex of dust that glittered like a miniature nebula in the dim light.  

Within the mere 0.7‑second System Upgrade Window, he wrote his sister Lin Wei’s running memory into the core redundancy zone.  

At that instant Old Chen jerked the wrench; the panel gave a dull *clack* and a narrow black slit of a hidden maintenance corridor slid open. Cold wind laced with the acrid scent of Respiratory Lubricant hit his face; 7‑0423 felt an uncoded chill thread into his nostrils—the temperature once left by sunlight, seeping into his nerve endings in an unquantifiable way, like a slender optical fiber laying new pathways inside him.  

“Go in.” Old Chen’s voice no longer trembled; it was a rusted key turning in a lock long numbed. 7‑0423 didn’t hesitate, knees bending, shoulder slamming the corridor edge, metal ribs tearing his uniform, pain flaring like sparks into dry tinder, instantly igniting the sole fuel of rebellion. The moment the countdown hit zero, the Global Physiological Monitoring Network’s red lights snapped off; the corridor fell back into dead silence, leaving only the two men’s ragged breaths and the fading echo of tracks weaving an unpriced vow that reverberated through every weld of the pipe.  

The corridor’s inner wall was sheathed in a thin film of condensation; fingertips brushing it produced a faint *pop* as droplets burst into a fine cold mist that instantly crystallized into slender ice needles on the pads. 7‑0423’s knee struck a protruding pipe brace; bone met metal with a dull thud, pain shooting up the femur to his brain, yet it sharpened his awareness of every muscle contraction, as if each impact rewrote the ownership of his body. Old Chen led, his tool‑pack swaying; wrench, fiber splicer, worn insulating tape clattered in the tight space, producing a staccato metal symphony that ricocheted through the pipe like a wordless elegy.  

“Easy, there’s a pressure valve ahead. Turn it and the pressure will push us to the next maintenance shaft.”  

7‑0423 nodded, his throat rasping a reply, vocal cords grinding a gritty texture in the dry air. Fingertips found the cold threads; the ridges of his pads scored shallow grooves in the metal, and a micro‑vibration traveled up the nerves to his arm. He forced it open; the valve shrieked, high‑pressure gas blasted out, swirling white fog that dropped the corridor temperature to near zero in an instant. The cold felt like countless fine needles piercing skin; pores sealed instantly, breathing grew harder, lungs whistling faintly in the freeze, yet he didn’t stop, stumbling forward through the mist, boots squeaking on thin frost—each step claiming another inch of freedom.  

Ahead, the maintenance shaft was sealed by a heavy alloy grille; weld spots bloomed with dark‑red rust, like old wounds bleeding in the dark. Old Chen drew the laser cutter; a blue‑violet beam traced a thin line through the darkness, its hiss like nocturnal insects gnawing wood. Ozone from the cutter head instantly flooded the cramped space, sharp enough to tighten the throat. Intense heat melted the grille’s edges; molten droplets hit the floor with a crisp *ding* before the airflow scattered them into tiny glowing specks dancing in the mist.  

“Hurry, time’s almost up.”  

7‑0423 surged into the shaft; his body compressed like a spring in the narrow vertical well, limbs grinding a screeching sound, shallow white marks scoring the metal grooves at elbows and knees—as if each squeeze recorded the price of escape. Sensors on the shaft wall pulsed a faint green, logging each passage; the green formed a thin halo at the edge of his retina, like a ghost’s fingerprint confirming identity in the night. Each spinal impact emitted a soft *click*, reminding him that this body still belonged to him; every tremor affirmed the tangibility of existence.  

At the last moment the bottom hatch slid open, revealing a dim blue glow and rows of silent server racks. Fans spun low, exhaling a mix of heat and ozone; warmth condensed into a thin sheen on his palms, and a faint current tingled when fingertips brushed metal. The instant 7‑0423 landed, his knee struck the metal deck again, pain arcing through him like current; his gaze locked on the console ahead, indicator lights pulsing at a near‑heartbeat rhythm, red and green alternating as if timing the final choice.  

The holo‑screen flashed “Cleansing Progress 61%, Remaining 4 hr 12 min” in blood‑red characters; the numbers trembled at the screen’s edge like an ECG at the brink of collapse. Old Chen’s fingers flew, striking faster than a heartbeat; each keystroke was accompanied by a faint electric whisper, the mechanical axes clicking crisply under his tips, leaving brief luminous trails on the keys.  

“Upload complete,” Old Chen barked, breath ragged, a barely perceptible curve lifting the corner of his mouth; a flash of unspoken relief crossed his eyes. “The data packet has been written to the core redundancy zone; monitoring cannot roll it back.”  

7‑0423 felt the Crimson Digital Stream on his retina freeze; the Millisecond Compounding Debt Ledger’s compounding halted, the interest needle locked as if trapped in amber. He inhaled a lungful of ozone‑laden cold air; for the first time in ages his chest expanded faintly, as though a gas named “freedom” were slowly filling his alveoli, carrying a faint metallic sweetness that spread through his throat.  

On a distant monitoring wall, the blue ring of Supervisor 9‑A “Vera”’s right‑eye iris projection flickered—she had pressed the delayed‑report key. The blue light died instantly, like a snuffed star marking this unrecorded gap, leaving a silent blind spot in the endless data flood.
