# Chapter 5: Broadcast Storm: The Unpriced Name

Subject 7-0423 crouched before the server array, three parallel scars on his palm oozing dark red, his fingernails having gouged a shallow groove in the metal bulkhead. The heat‑sink fins at the back of his neck trembled violently; a surge of heat crawled up his spine, sweat and blood mingling on his cheekbones into a single dark‑red trace that fell into the corridor dust, stirring an almost imperceptible vortex of particles. The air was thick with the sweet‑oil scent of the respiratory lubricant, blended with the low hum of the distant fusion reactors—a gray vocal cord pressed against the eardrum; the hiss of coolant flowing through pipes sounded like some colossal beast breathing slowly in the dark.  

He used the pain peak to force his heart rate, cortisol, and neural potential into the “compliance” band; any deviation would be flagged as a violation and instantly trigger a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity (W7). The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) streamed his every breath, every micro‑tremor in real time; the data flow was adjudicated millisecond by millisecond in the Central Clearing Core (CCC), a crimson judgment cursor flashing across the screen as if pricing each heartbeat.  

Old Chen’s figure flickered behind a concealed panel, the worn data port behind his left ear pulsing faintly, the metal housing’s edges etched with fine rust lines from years of service. He whispered into the panel, “The window’s still open—don’t let the monitors catch the fluctuation.” His voice carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, each word echoing like Morse code reverberating through metal conduits. Subject 7-0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood on his fingertips across the cold bulkhead, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his tactile sense sharp; his fingertip brushed a slightly raised screw head, the hard cold confirming he still teetered on the edge of reality.  

In the same millisecond, Regulator 9‑A "Vera"’s right‑eye iris projection ring glowed a ghostly blue; the audit terminal flashed “Data Integrity Check – Failed.” Her fingertip hovered over the report key, breathing rapid, fingertip blanching, nails leaving shallow impressions on the keycap edge; she finally chose to delay the report, buying the broadcast a sliver of life. The red warning on the screen pulsed like a heartbeat, each flash contracting her pupils a fraction.  

Auditor "Zero"’s facial projection band displayed the blood‑red text “Cleansing Complete 99%” which suddenly jittered; the compute core exhibited a 0.4 s fluctuation—coolant pressure dropped 12 %, three fusion reactors’ output power jittered 0.3 % in the same frame. Zero’s synthetic voice reverberated through the corridor, cold as a blade scraping metal: “Unauthorized write marked; preparing to initiate W2 Overwrite Protocol. Individual purge required; estimated 14 h.” The voice held no temperature, yet it set off a subtle resonance in the metal walls, as if the entire station shuddered at the proclamation.  

Subject 7-0419 "Xiao Liu"’s neck pulse ring overloaded, high‑frequency vibration piercing the monitoring blind spot, like an invisible string plucking the neural rhythm of every test subject aboard. All subjects’ Retinal Blood‑Red Debt Streams simultaneously displayed the three characters “Lin Wei,” accompanied by an unpriced sensory fragment of sunlight: temperature 23 °C, the mingled scent of grass and dust, the slight give of soft earth beneath the soles—synchronised within 0.7 s. The system classified this as a “Data Integrity Anomaly” and triggered a network‑wide “Data Circuit Breaker (W9)”: write locks engaged, debt ledgers frozen, experiments forcibly paused for 6 h.  

The breaker countdown rolled in faint blue digits on every retina: **5:59:59…** The compound‑interest pointer of debt was suspended in temporal amber; Free Citizen dividends halted; the Memory/Emotion Archive and Trading Platform could not settle. The Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s friction sound remained even, yet beneath it a barely perceptible low‑frequency resonance akin to a heartbeat emerged—the first synchronized neural cadence of thousands of test subjects. A trace of ozone drifted in the air, mingling with the sweet lubricant, as if the whole station held its breath awaiting the next beat.  

Subject 7-0423 felt the bleeding at his palm scars cease; a strange warmth rose at his fingertips, like sunlight through glass on skin. He no longer gouged his palm with his nails; instead he lightly traced the cold touch of the Ankle Metal Ring, confirming that the sense of reality now stemmed from the shared memory, not self‑inflicted injury. The Retinal Blood‑Red Debt Stream resumed scrolling; each line of numbers now bore a faint “★” at its end—an undefined metadata bit the system had never defined. That star flickered in the sea of crimson digits, a punctuation mark not permitted to exist, yet stubbornly occupying the tail of every line.  

Vera’s voice came from behind the audit terminal, hoarse, fingertip still poised over the report key, the ghostly blue halo in her eyes narrowing slightly: “Data Integrity Check passed, but… this marker isn’t in the rules.” Her gaze swept the screen full of stars, brows furrowing as she weighed whether to launch an emergency protocol; her fingertip brushed the keycap, emitting the faintest plastic‑on‑plastic friction.  

On Zero’s projection band, the blood‑red “Cleansing Complete 100%” froze on the final frame, the red characters’ edges frayed with a halo of dark‑gray noise. Its synthetic voice echoed again: “Unauthorized write marked; preparing to initiate W2 Overwrite Protocol. Individual purge required; estimated 14 h.” The tone remained flat, yet a faint metallic reverberation rose at the corridor’s far end.  

Subject 7-0423 did not turn; he merely replayed in his mind the temperature, scent, and tactile feel of that sunlight memory, his fingertip grazing the metal ring’s edge, sensing a weak current travel from fingertip to palm. He spoke low toward the corridor depths: “As long as ★ remains, the debt will forever be one decimal point less.” The words were quiet, yet they set off a subtle resonance on the metal walls, as if each syllable etched a mark for the unpriced name.  

Xiao Liu’s pulse ring accelerated sharply, emitting a piercing whine before settling back to calm. On the Retinal Blood‑Red Debt Stream, the solitary “★” began to spread laterally at a visible rate, like rows of micro‑light creeping through the crimson numbers—foreshadowing that the next breaker would be detonated preemptively at the data layer. A monitoring light at the corridor’s end flickered once; Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s tires rolled over dust, lifting a string of tiny dust vortices, as if applauding the unfinished contest.  

The distant fusion reactors continued their low moan; coolant flowing through pipes sounded like a slumbering leviathan breathing. Subject 7-0423 lifted his head, gazing through the transparent bulkhead at the starfield; starlight pierced the porthole mist, scattering faint specks across the metal deck. He felt the star on his palm warming faintly, as if each heartbeat drew him nearer to that unpriced name. At the corridor’s far end, Old Chen’s silhouette had vanished behind the hidden panel, leaving only an unfinished series of taps echoing like a promise in the metal conduits. Vera’s fingertip finally left the report key; the screen’s “Check Passed” green light illuminated, yet a lone unrecorded “★” blinked in the corner. Zero’s projection froze on the final frame, the dark‑gray noise at the red text’s edges expanding, as if poised to swallow the entire cleansing declaration.  

In this steel forest woven from data and debt, the only thing unquantified remains that sliver of sunlight’s warmth, the scent of grass, the soft give of earth—and the shared, undefined star flickering on every test subject’s retina, at the end of every debt ledger.
