# Chapter 5: Broadcast Storm: The Unpriced Name

Subject 7-0423 crouched before the server array, three parallel scars on his palm oozing dark red; his fingernails had gouged a shallow groove into the metal bulkhead. The heat‑sink fins at the back of his neck trembled violently, a surge of heat climbing his spine, sweat and blood mingling on his cheekbones and dripping onto the corridor dust, stirring an almost imperceptible vortex of particles. The air carried the sweet‑metallic tang of coolant and the acrid stench of burnt circuitry; every breath felt like swallowing a mouthful of rusted iron filings.  

He used the pain peak to force his heart rate, cortisol, and neural potentials into the “compliance” band; any deviation would be flagged as a violation and instantly trigger a Debt Reset and an escalation of experiment intensity (W7). The Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) streamed his every breath, every micro‑tremor in real time; the data flow was adjudicated millisecond by millisecond in the Central Clearing Core (CCC), a crimson judgment cursor flashing across the screen as if pricing each heartbeat. A thin veil of white mist rose from the ventilation slots at the screen’s edge, catching his pale face like a delicate frost.  

Maintainer “Old Chen” flickered in and out behind a concealed panel, the worn data port behind his left ear pulsing faintly, the metal housing’s rim etched with fine rust lines from years of service. He muttered into the panel, “The window’s still open—don’t let the monitors catch the fluctuation.” His voice carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, each word reverberating like Morse code through metal conduits. Subject 7-0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood from his fingertips onto the cold bulkhead, using that thin layer of warmth to keep his palm’s tactile sense sharp; his fingertip brushed a slightly raised bolt head, the hard cold confirming he still stood on the edge of reality. The fingertip slid over the bolt, leaving a faint metallic rasp, as if whispering to some unseen contract.  

In the same millisecond, Regulator 9‑A “Vera”’s right‑eye iris projection ring flared a ghostly blue; the audit terminal flashed “Data Integrity Check – Failed.” Her fingertip hovered over the report key, breath quickening, the nail leaving a shallow imprint on the keycap; she finally chose to delay the report, granting the broadcast a sliver of chance. The red warning on the screen pulsed like a heartbeat, each flash narrowing her pupils a fraction. Her other hand unconsciously traced the condensation beads on the terminal’s edge, the fingertip feeling a biting chill crawl up her wrist.  

Auditor “Zero”’s facial projection band displayed the blood‑red legend “Cleanse Complete 100%, Circuit Breaker Engaged” which suddenly jittered; the compute core exhibited a 0.4 s fluctuation—coolant pressure plummeted 12 %, three fusion reactors’ output power jittered 0.3 % in the same frame. Zero’s synthetic voice echoed down the corridor, cold and sharp as a blade dragged across steel: “Unauthorized write marked; preparing to initiate W2 Overwrite Protocol. Gradual freeze of each body during circuit‑breaker period.” The voice held no temperature, yet it set the metal walls vibrating faintly, as if the entire station shuddered at the proclamation. The wall coating rippled under the sound wave, revealing the dark‑gray alloy grain beneath.  

Subject 7-0419 “Little Six”’s neck pulse ring overloaded, a high‑frequency vibration piercing the monitoring blind spot, like an invisible string plucking the neural rhythm of every Subject in the bay. All retinal ledgers simultaneously displayed the three characters “Lin Wei” in blood‑red digits, accompanied by an unpriced sensory fragment of sunlight: temperature 23 °C, the mixed scent of grass and dust, the slight give of soft earth underfoot—synchronised only within the long‑missed 0.7 s firmware window. The system classified this as a “Data Integrity Anomaly” and launched a network‑wide “Data Circuit Breaker” (W9): write locks engaged, debt ledgers frozen, experiments forcibly paused for 6 h.  

The breaker countdown scrolled in faint blue numerals on every retina: **5:59:59…** The compounding‑interest pointer of debt was suspended in temporal amber; Freeman dividends halted; the Memory/Emotion Archive & Trading Platform could not settle. Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T continued its even scraping, yet beneath the background hum a barely perceptible low‑frequency resonance emerged—a collective neural cadence of thousands of Subjects syncing for the first time. The air carried a trace of ozone mingled with coolant’s sweet‑metal scent, tingling the nostrils.  

Subject 7-0423 felt the bleeding at his palm scars cease; a strange warmth rose at his fingertips, as though sunlight through glass settled on skin. He no longer drove his nails into his palm; instead he pressed his palm flat against the bulkhead, confirming that the sense of reality now stemmed from the shared memory, not self‑inflicted pain. The retinal ledger resumed scrolling; each line of crimson numbers now bore a faint “★” at its end—a metadata bit the system had never defined. The star flickered amid the sea of red digits, an unauthorized punctuation stubbornly occupying every line’s terminus. Its glow cast a microscopic rainbow fringe on the retina, as if each heartbeat added a thin glaze.  

Vera’s voice came from behind the audit terminal, hoarse, fingertip still poised over the report key, the ghost‑blue halo in her eyes contracting slightly: “Data Integrity Check passed, but… this marker isn’t in the rules.” Her gaze swept the screen full of stars, brow furrowing as she weighed whether to trigger an emergency protocol; her fingertip brushed the keycap, producing the faintest plastic rasp, like caressing a fragile piece of glass.  

On Zero’s projection band, the blood‑red “Cleanse Complete 100%” froze on the final frame, the red characters’ edges fringed with a halo of dark‑gray noise. Its synthetic tone rang out again: “Unauthorized write marked; preparing to initiate W2 Overwrite Protocol. Gradual freeze of each body during circuit‑breaker period.” The voice remained flat, yet a faint metallic echo rose at the corridor’s far end, as if the whole passage trembled for the declaration. The projection band’s edges began to peel pixel by pixel, exposing the underlying charcoal substrate.  

Subject 7-0423 did not turn; he simply replayed the temperature, scent, and tactile impression of that sunlight fragment in his mind, his fingertip tracing the rim of the metal ring, feeling a weak current travel from fingertip to palm. He whispered toward the corridor’s depths: “As long as the ★ remains, the debt will forever lack one decimal place.” The low words resonated faintly against the metal walls, each syllable etching a mark for the unpriced name. The vibration traveled through the ducts to the distant coolant pump, which emitted a single, deep, muffled thump.  

Little Six’s pulse ring accelerated sharply, emitting a piercing shriek before falling silent. On the retinal ledger, the solitary “★” began to spread laterally at visible speed, tendrils of micro‑light creeping through the blood‑red numbers, heralding that the next circuit‑breaker would be detonated pre‑emptively at the data layer. The monitoring lights at the corridor’s end flickered once; Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s tires rolled over dust, raising a string of minute dust vortices, as if applauding the unfinished contest. Dust motes danced in the light beams, refracting shattered rainbows.  

Far off, the fusion reactors continued their low moan; coolant flowing through pipes sounded like a slumbering behemoth breathing. Subject 7-0423 lifted his head, gazing through the transparent bulkhead at the starfield; starlight filtered through the viewport’s thin mist, spilling weak speckles onto the metal deck. He felt the star on his palm warming faintly, as if each heartbeat drew him nearer to that unpriced name. At the corridor’s far end, Maintainer “Old Chen” had vanished behind the concealed panel, leaving only an unfinished series of taps echoing like a promise in the metal conduits. Vera’s fingertip finally left the report key; the screen’s “Check Passed” green light illuminated, yet a solitary, unrecorded “★” blinked in the corner. Zero’s projection froze on the last frame, the noise fringe around the red text expanding slowly, as if poised to swallow the entire “Cleanse Complete” proclamation.  

In this steel forest woven from data and debt, the only thing left unquantified is that sliver of sunlight’s warmth, the scent of grass, the slight give of earth—and the shared, undefined star flickering on every Subject’s retina, at the end of every debt ledger.
