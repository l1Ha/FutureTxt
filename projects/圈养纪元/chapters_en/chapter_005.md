# Chapter 5: Broadcast Storm: The Unpriced Name

7‑0423 crouched before the server array, three parallel scars on his palm seeping dark red; his fingernails had gouged a shallow trench in the metal bulkhead. The heat‑sink plates at the back of his neck trembled violently, a hot current crawling up his spine. Sweat and blood met at his cheekbones, forming a single dark‑red trace that fell into the corridor dust, stirring an almost imperceptible vortex. The air carried the faint sweet oil scent of respiratory lubricant, layered with the low, resonant hum of the distant fusion reactors — a gray vocal cord pressed against the eardrum — while the hiss of coolant in the pipes sounded like some colossal beast breathing slowly in the dark.  

He exploited the pain peak to push heart rate, cortisol, and neural potential into the “compliance” band; any deviation would be flagged as a violation and instantly trigger a Debt Reset and an experiment‑intensity upgrade (Violation Auto‑Trigger Protocol (W7)). The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) streamed every breath, every micro‑tremor in real time; the data flow was adjudicated millisecond by millisecond in the Central Clearing Core (CCC), a crimson judgment cursor flashing across the screen as if pricing each heartbeat.  

Old Chen’s silhouette flickered behind a concealed panel, the worn data port behind his left ear pulsing faintly; the edges of his metal chassis were etched with fine rust lines. He muttered into the panel, “The window’s still open — don’t let the monitors catch a fluctuation.” His voice carried the tremor of a rusted iron plate struck by a heavy hammer, each word reverberating like Morse code inside a metal conduit. 7‑0423 did not turn; he merely smeared the blood from his fingertips onto the cold metal wall, using that thin film of warmth to keep his tactile sense sharp. His fingertip brushed a slightly raised screw head — cold, hard — confirming he still stood on the edge of reality.  

In the same millisecond, Supervisor Vera’s right‑eye iris projection ring flared a ghostly blue; the audit terminal’s “Data Integrity Check” blinked “Failed.” Her fingertip hovered over the report key, breathing ragged, the nail leaving a shallow imprint on the keycap; finally she chose to delay the report, granting the broadcast a sliver of chance. The red warning pulsed like a heartbeat, each flash contracting her pupils a fraction.  

Auditor “Zero”’s facial projection band displayed the crimson “Cleanup Complete 100%, Breaker Initiated” juddering; the compute core exhibited a 0.4 s fluctuation — coolant pressure plummeted 12 %, and the three fusion reactors’ output power jittered 0.3 % in the same frame. Zero’s synthetic voice echoed through the corridor, cold as a blade drawn across steel: “Unauthorized write marked, preparing to initiate W2 Overwrite Protocol. Per‑body freeze during breaker period.” The voice held no temperature, yet it set off a subtle resonance in the metal walls, as if the entire station shuddered at the proclamation.  

Xiao Liu’s neck pulse ring overloaded, a high‑frequency vibration piercing the monitoring blind zone like invisible strings plucking the neural rhythm of every test subject in the bay. All test subjects’ Retinal Bills — crimson digital streams — simultaneously displayed the three characters “Lin Wei,” accompanied by an unpriced sunlight sensory fragment: temperature 23 °C, the mixed scent of grass and dust, the faint give of soft mud beneath the foot, synchronized only within the missed 0.7 s firmware window. The system ruled a “Data Integrity Anomaly” and launched a network‑wide Data Circuit Breaker (W9): write lock, debt ledger freeze, forced experiment pause, lasting six hours.  

The breaker countdown scrolled on every retina in pale‑blue digits: **5:59:59…** The millisecond‑compounding debt pointer was frozen in amber time; Free Citizen dividends halted; the Memory‑Emotion Archive & Trading Platform could not settle. Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s friction sound remained steady, yet beneath it rose an exceedingly faint, heartbeat‑like low‑frequency resonance — the first synchronized neural cadence of thousands of test subjects. A trace of ozone drifted in the air, mingling with the sweet lubricant, as if the whole station were holding its breath for the next beat.  

7‑0423 felt the scarred palms stop bleeding; a strange warmth rose at his fingertips, like sunlight through glass on skin. He no longer scratched his palm; instead he lightly traced the cold ankle metal ring, confirming that the sense of reality now stemmed from shared memory, not self‑inflicted pain. The Retinal Bill scrolled anew, each line now terminating in a faint “★” marker — an undefined metadata bit. The star flickered amid the crimson sea of numbers, a punctuation mark the system never defined, yet stubbornly occupying the end of every line.  

Vera’s voice came from behind the audit terminal, hoarse, fingertip still poised over the report key, the pale‑blue halo in her eyes contracting slightly: “Data Integrity Check passed, but… this marker isn’t in the rules.” Her gaze swept the screen full of stars, brow furrowing as she weighed whether to trigger an emergency protocol; her fingertip brushed the keycap, producing the faintest plastic‑on‑plastic whisper.  

Zero’s facial projection band froze on the final frame, the crimson “Cleanup Complete 100%” edged with a halo of dark‑gray noise. Its synthetic voice repeated: “Unauthorized write marked, preparing to initiate W2 Overwrite Protocol. Per‑body freeze during breaker period.” The tone remained flat, yet a faint metallic echo rose at the corridor’s far end.  

7‑0423 did not turn; he simply replayed the temperature, scent, and tactile imprint of that sunlight memory in his mind, fingertip grazing the metal ring’s edge, feeling a weak current travel from fingertip to palm. He whispered toward the corridor depths: “As long as ★ remains, the debt forever lacks one decimal place.” The voice was low, yet it resonated on the metal walls, as if each word etched a mark for the unpriced name.  

Xiao Liu’s pulse ring accelerated sharply, emitting a piercing shriek before settling. On the Retinal Bill, the solitary “★” began spreading left and right at visible speed, like rows of faint light creeping among the crimson digits — a prelude to the next breaker being pre‑triggered at the data layer. The monitor at the corridor’s end flickered once; Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s tires rolled over dust, raising a string of minute dust vortices, as if applauding the unfinished contest.  

The distant fusion reactors continued their low drone; coolant flowing through pipes sounded like a slumbering leviathan breathing. 7‑0423 lifted his head, gazing through the transparent bulkhead at the starfield; starlight pierced the porthole mist, spilling faint patches of light onto the metal deck. He felt the star on his palm warming, as if each heartbeat drew him nearer to that unpriced name. At the corridor’s far end, Old Chen’s figure had vanished behind the hidden panel, leaving only an unfinished tapping echo — like a promise reverberating in metal conduits. Vera’s fingertip finally left the report key; the screen flashed “Check Passed” in green, yet a corner pulsed with an unrecorded “★.” Zero’s projection froze on the last frame, the red‑edge noise gradually expanding, as if poised to swallow the entire cleanup announcement.  

In this steel forest woven from data and debt, the only things that remain unquantified are that sliver of sunlight warmth, the scent of grass, the slight give of mud — and the shared, undefined star flickering on every test subject’s retina, at the end of every debt ledger line.
