# Chapter 2: The Friction of Fragment Exchange

The corridor‑end Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T rolled its tracks across the alloy floor, emitting a steady low‑frequency friction sound, like a taut steel wire being plucked repeatedly in the silence. Every fifteen meters a misting nozzle sprayed a faint sweet oil scent, mingling the stale odor of machine oil and disinfectant that slipped into the nostrils, coating the throat with a thin film of oil that slid down the airway to the lung lobes, leaving a faint metallic tang. The ankle metal ring rested against the skin, its constant 12 °C chill traveling with each heartbeat to the fingertips, reminding subject 7‑0423 of his ownership. On the retina a crimson digital stream of debt scrolled at 0.8 m, the principal 1.2 × 10⁵ CR swelling with millisecond compounding, the red fluid devouring the last reserves of stamina. The tongue still bore the bitter aftertaste of the forced medication; the breakfast nutrient paste’s metallic chew resistance produced a faint “crunch” between the teeth, as if chewing an unnamed contract. The distant centrifuge’s low hum intertwined with the laboratory’s white noise, forming a gray membrane that pressed against the eardrums, the very air vibrating at a weak frequency that made every breath feel like a count against an invisible metronome.  

Old Chen pushed the maintenance cart; its wheels groaned a duller “clack” on the same rail, metal bearings sighing with microscopic fatigue in the cold alloy gaps. The data port behind his left ear flickered weakly; his fingertips tapped the handle twice — the pre‑arranged “window open” signal for 7‑0423, calibrated so the port sensed a minute capacitance shift without tripping an alarm. The System Upgrade Window opened only 0.7 seconds every 30 days (W12), the sole interval permitting unauthorized writes; Old Chen had seeded this gap in the cleaning robot’s firmware, masking the extra command behind the rule “execute preset checklist only,” so the firmware log would show merely a routine “environment scan complete.” He glanced at the hidden panel beneath the cart, its green indicator blinking, fingertips brushing the cold edge and feeling a faint current crawl up the back of his hand, then he rolled the cart forward, leaving a string of shallow impressions on the rail.  

7‑0423 moved along the wall, the ankle ring tightening with each step, the metal clasp leaving faint pressure marks on his skin — each contraction a reminder that his body no longer belonged to him. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed dull red in the dim light; a bead of blood at the nail edge trembled on the verge of a neural spasm, sliding through his fingers with a chill. The retinal bill’s crimson digital stream rolled across the centre of his pupil, the interest increment swelling visibly each second, the red fluid seemingly about to rupture the retinal membrane. He dared not look up at Supervisor Vera’s projection ring; the micro‑ring implanted in her right iris glowed blue in the gloom, auditing data integrity in real time (W8) — any read/write latency exceeding 2 milliseconds would trigger a Data Integrity Check and lock the subject for 24 hours. Wall lamps cast long, thin shadows like invisible hands measuring his every step; the shadows’ edges trembled on the metal floor, as if ready to close at any moment.  

Xiao Liu (7‑0419) leaned against the corner railing, his neck pulse ring flashing with his heart rate, a weak blue light refracting into shattered prisms on the metal surface; the light spots danced on the wall like a fragmented cipher. His language center had atrophied; he could only tap a rhythm with his ankle ring: two short, one long, pause, three short — the agreed “Memory Fragment Exchange” syntax for the surveillance blind spot, each beat corresponding to an encrypted memory index. The taps echoed in the corridor, mingling with the cleaning robot’s low growl. Old Chen pressed the confirm key on the hidden panel beneath the cart; the screen flashed green text: **“Firmware window 0.7 s ready.”** The characters stood out starkly against the dim background, like a key turning in a rusted lock, the lock cylinder emitting a faint “click.”  

0.7 seconds passed like a single heartbeat. The nano‑carbon‑tube electrode array inside 7‑0423’s skull completed the write; the unpriced memory of his sister Lin Wei’s sunlight laughter was encapsulated as a pointer and written to the edge block of the test‑subject public cache. At the instant of writing, the palm scars bled again; a blood bead slipped down the fingers, forming a dark red dot on the metal floor that gave an almost inaudible “drip,” amplified by the corridor’s reverberation into a ritualistic knock, as if each drop sealed a contract. The retina flashed red: **“Unauthorized write attempt – pending review.”** Vera’s projection ring flared blue; her fingertip typed “Mark 7‑0423 as high‑risk subject,” and the screen popped up: **“Approval requires Auditor ‘Zero’ signature, countdown 48 hours.”** The numbers flickered in the air like an hourglass suspended above, grains falling one by one into the abyss.  

Cleaning Robot 07‑T hesitated, its tracks emitting a deeper, more ominous friction tone; the metal‑on‑metal hesitation reverberated down the narrow corridor, as if the entire hallway’s air had been drawn taut. Old Chen glanced at the robot’s log; a new entry appeared: **“Environmental interference – physical obstruction.”** It would be automatically reported to the Central Clearing Core, the timestamp precise to microseconds, blinking cold blue. He muttered to 7‑0423, “Machines don’t speak; people have to.” His tone was flat, yet in the echo it weighed heavy, each word seeming to add weight to the impending judgment, the sound refracting off the metal walls with a subtle tremor.  

7‑0423 did not turn back; he merely drove his nails deeper into his palm, the blood‑drop sound stark in the silence — the only rhythm he could hear that belonged to him, each drop striking a rusted iron plate. The automatic door at the corridor’s end slid open, cold air carrying the faint sweet oil scent rushing toward him; the retinal bill’s crimson digital stream kept scrolling, interest devouring every second without mercy. He knew the next 0.7‑second window would not arrive for another 30 days, and before then Vera’s review, Zero’s signature, and the ever‑possible Data Circuit Breaker protocol all ticked down in countdown. A distant cough from another test subject rose again, mixed with a low laugh; the laugh stretched by the ductwork into a metallic warble: “Don’t think about running; once the upgrade window closes, even breathing is billed by frequency.” The voice was hoarse, permanently scarred by years of inhaling oil and disinfectant, each syllable striking a rusted iron plate, lingering long in the corridor.  

7‑0423’s pupils contracted; a new prompt floated on his retina: **“Memory correction program initiation countdown 3.2 seconds”** (the forced rewrite routine automatically launched when the system detects memory integrity FAIL). A whiff of burnt insulation varnish rode the cold air into his nasal passages, scraping the trachea like a rusted file; each inhalation brought a fine stab of pain. The nano‑array in his skull released a weak current the moment the countdown lit, crawling along cranial sutures with a tingling sting, as if countless tiny ants gnawed at the meninges. He clenched his teeth, a low muffled grunt escaping his throat; another blood bead fell, striking the icy floor, its sound swallowed the instant the countdown hit zero, leaving only a faint echo trembling on the metal walls.  

Vera’s fingertip hovered over the “Delay report” key; the blue light of her projection ring refracted into two slender blades within her pupils. At the corridor’s far end a red alarm lamp flared, a shrill siren slicing through the tracks’ low growl; the whine of an external inspection drone’s rotors pressed against the metal wall. She did not press confirm; instead she slowly withdrew her hand, the screen’s **“Temporary authorization – pending supervisory review”** still pulsing weakly, like a pass that could be torn up at any moment. Old Chen rolled the cart backward, the axle’s “clack” fading toward the corridor’s end, leaving only the cleaning robot’s low growl and the intermittent coughs of distant test subjects — an ominous cadence that kept hammering against 7‑0423’s heart.
