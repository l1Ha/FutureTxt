# Chapter 2: The Friction of Fragment Exchange

The treads of cleaning robot 07‑T rolled across the alloy floor, emitting a steady hiss (≈ 38 dB), like a thin metal string plucked repeatedly in the morning stillness. Every fifteen meters the misting nozzles released a faint sweet oil scent from the respiratory lubrication system; it drifted through the ventilation ducts into the nostrils, a cloying blend of industrial synthetic fat, machine oil, and aged disinfectant, as if a thin film of grease clung to the back of the throat. The cold bite of the ankle cuff against skin (≈ 12 °C) felt like a delicate ice chain, each heartbeat reminding Subject 7‑0423 of his ownership. The retinal bill crimson digit stream hovered 0.8 m away, crystal clear: principal 1.2 × 10⁵ CR compounding each millisecond at 0.00012 %/s (W2), the numbers sliding deeper into red with every tick. The bitter aftertaste of the mandatory medication lingered on the tongue; the breakfast nutrient paste’s metallic chew resistance (1.8 N·mm) produced a faint “crunch” between the teeth, as though he were mastication some unspeakable contract. The laboratory’s white noise and the centrifuge’s low hum seeped through the vents, a perpetual gray film wrapping the mid‑levels of the Debt Tower, the very air vibrating at a barely perceptible frequency.  

Maintainer “Old Chen” pushed a maintenance cart; its wheels groaned on the same track with a duller rumble than the cleaning robot, metal spokes striking microscopic grooves in a staccato “clack‑clack.” The worn data interface behind his left ear flickered weakly; his fingertips tapped the handle twice — the pre‑arranged signal with 7‑0423 meaning “window open.” Per regulation (W12), the system upgrade window opens only 0.7 s every 30 days, the sole interval permitting unauthorized data writes; Old Chen had seeded the gap in the cleaning robot’s firmware, masking the extra command behind the “execute preset checklist only” rule. He glanced at the hidden panel beneath the cart, a green indicator blinking, a faint sourness rising in his chest: each smuggling run traded a slice of his own lifespan for another’s breath.  

Subject 7‑0423 moved along the corridor, hugging the wall; the ankle cuff’s chill tightened with each step, the metal clasp leaving a faint pressure ring on his skin. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed dull crimson in the dim light; beads of blood at his nail beds trembled on the verge of a neural spasm, as if an invisible force might pull them away at any moment. The retinal bill’s crimson digits scrolled across his pupil, interest swelling visibly each second, the red fluid devouring his last reserves of stamina. He dared not glance at Supervisor 9‑A “Vera”’s projection ring — the micro‑projector implanted in her right iris glowed blue in the gloom, performing real‑time data integrity verification (W8); any read/write latency exceeding 2 ms would trigger a Data Integrity Check and lock the body for 24 h. Cold white wall lamps flickered, casting long, thin shadows like invisible hands measuring his every step.  

Subject 7‑0419 “Xiao Liu” leaned against the metal railing at the corridor bend; his neck pulse ring flashed in time with his heart, emitting a weak blue pulse that fractured into prism shards on the metal surface. His language centers had atrophied; he could only tap rhythm with the cold touch of his ankle cuff: two short, one long, pause, three short. This was the “Data Fragment Exchange” syntax they had devised in the surveillance blind spot, each beat mapping to an encrypted memory index. Old Chen pressed the confirm key on the cart’s hidden panel; the screen flashed a line of green text: **“Firmware window 0.7 s ready”**, the characters stark against the dim backlight, like a key turning in a rusted lock.  

The 0.7 s elapsed like a single heartbeat. The nano‑carbon‑tube electrode array inside 7‑0423’s skull completed the write; the unpriced laughter of his sister Lin Wei was encapsulated as a pointer and stored in the edge block of the test‑subject public cache. At the moment of writing, the palm scars bled again; a droplet slid through his fingers, landing on the metal deck with a soft “drip,” amplified by the corridor’s echo into a ritualistic tap. A red line flared on his retina: **“Unauthorized write attempt – pending review.”** Vera’s projection ring blazed blue; her fingers typed “Flag 7‑0423 as high‑risk body,” and a dialog appeared: **“Approval requires Auditor ‘Zero’ signature, countdown 48 h.”** The countdown numbers hovered in the air like an hourglass suspended above his head.  

Cleaning robot 07‑T hesitated, its treads emitting a lower, deeper hiss, as if a massive gear meshed with reluctance; the metal‑on‑metal friction reverberated down the narrow corridor with an ominous resonance. Old Chen glanced at the robot log; a new entry read **“Environmental interference – physical obstruction.”** It would be auto‑reported to the Central Clearing Core (CCC). He muttered to 7‑0423, “Machines don’t talk; people have to.” His tone was flat, yet in the corridor’s echo it weighed heavy, each word adding to the impending judgment.  

7‑0423 did not turn; he simply drove his nails deeper into his palm, the blood drops’ rhythm stark in the silence — the only beat he could claim as his own. The automatic door at the corridor’s end slid open; cold air, laced with the sweet oil scent, rushed over him. The retinal bill’s crimson stream kept scrolling, interest devouring every second without mercy. He knew the next 0.7 s window would not arrive for another 30 days, and before then Vera’s review, Zero’s signature, and the ever‑present threat of a Data Circuit Breaker Protocol all ticked down. A distant cough from another test subject rose again, this time mixed with a low, metallic laugh; the laughter stretched through the ducts into a vibrating timbre. “Don’t think about running. Once the upgrade window closes, even breathing is billed by the beat.” The voice was raspy, permanently hoarse from years of inhaling machine oil and disinfectant; each syllable struck like a hammer on rusted iron.  

7‑0423’s pupils contracted slightly; a new prompt floated on the retinal bill: **“Memory correction program initiation countdown 3.2 s”** (the automatic forced rewrite triggered when memory integrity FAILs, countdown configurable). He did not unclench his fist; the nails bit again into the old scar, a fresh bead of blood falling, a crisp “drip” on the cold floor, drawn out by the corridor’s echo into a monotonous tapping rhythm.  

A whiff of burnt insulation varnish rode the chill air into his lungs, a rusted rasp dragging across his trachea. At the instant the “Memory correction program initiation countdown 3.2 s” lit up, a faint current crawled along the cranial sutures, delivering a tingling sting as if countless tiny ants gnawed at the meninges. He kept his fist clenched; the scar split a hairline wider, fresh blood tracing his fingers, striking the metal deck with a clear “drip,” stretched by the echo into a single, steady beat.  

Old Chen’s cart axle gave a dull “clunk”; on his control panel a line of gray‑green characters scrolled: **“Physical obstruction cleared, report retracted.”** Cleaning robot 07‑T’s treads restarted, the low growl of meshing gears rolling through the ducts like a great beast stretching in darkness. Vera’s projection ring pulsed blue; her fingers typed “Request Zero signature,” the countdown numbers jumping in the air, each second accompanied by a faint electrical hum, as if the whole corridor held its breath.  

“Don’t think about running.” The raspy cough returned from deep within the ducts, laced with a metallic chuckle. “When the upgrade window shuts, even breathing is metered by the beat.” The words rang like a hammer on rusted steel, each syllable trembling the corridor’s steel walls.  

7‑0423’s pupils shrank within the retinal bill’s crimson flow; the countdown leapt to **0.3 s**. He felt the correction program seize the edge of his memory like an invisible hand, forcibly stripping and rewriting that unpriced sunshine laughter. Agony erupted, molten lead coursing down nerve bundles to his limbs; fingertip sensation dulled and grew heavy. He clenched his teeth, a low groan escaping his throat; another blood droplet fell, striking the icy floor, its sound swallowed the instant the countdown hit zero.  

“Signature confirmed.” Vera’s voice cut through the projection ring, cold as a blade on glass. “Auditor ‘Zero’ has approved; window extended to 72 h.” The red countdown vanished, replaced by a pale blue line: **“Temporary authorization – pending supervisory review.”** Old Chen wheeled the cart slowly backward; the axle’s clunk faded toward the corridor’s end, leaving only the cleaning robot’s low growl and the intermittent cough of a distant test subject — an ill‑omened rhythm pounding against 7‑0423’s heart.
