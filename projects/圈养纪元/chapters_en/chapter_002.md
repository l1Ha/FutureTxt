# Chapter 2: The Friction of Fragment Exchange

At the far end of the corridor, the Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s tracks rolled across the alloy floor, emitting a steady low‑frequency grinding sound laced with a faint sweet mist of lubricating oil.  

Lao Chen pushed the maintenance cart; its wheels on the same track produced a duller “clack” than the cleaning robot, the metal bearings emitting a fine, metallic fatigue whine in the cold alloy gaps. The data interface behind his left ear flickered weakly; he tapped the handle twice with his fingertips — the pre‑arranged “window open” signal with 7‑0423. The tap’s force was calibrated to register a minute capacitance shift without triggering an alarm. The System Upgrade Window opened only 0.7 seconds every 30 days (W12), the sole interval permitting unauthorized writes; Lao Chen had embedded this loophole in the cleaning robot’s firmware, masking the extra command behind the rule “execute preset checklist only,” so the firmware log would show only a routine “environment scan complete.” He glanced down at the green light blinking on the hidden panel beneath the cart, fingertips brushing the cold edge, feeling a faint current crawl up his hand, then pushed the cart forward, its wheels leaving a series of shallow impressions on the track.  

7‑0423 walked along the wall; his ankle ring tightened with each step, the metal clasp leaving faint indentations on his skin — each contraction a reminder that his body no longer belonged to him. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed a dull red in the dim light; beads of blood seeped from his nail edges, trembling in the pre‑spasm of his nerves, a cool trace slipping between his fingers. The blood‑red digital stream of his retinal bill scrolled across the center of his pupil: principal 1.2 × 10^5 Credit Points (CR) swelling with millisecond compound interest, the interest increment visibly expanding each second, the crimson fluid seeming ready to burst through the retinal membrane. He dared not look up at Regulator Vira’s projection ring; the micro‑ring implanted in her right iris glowed blue in the gloom, performing a real‑time Data Integrity Check (W8). Any read/write latency exceeding 2 milliseconds would trigger a Data Integrity Check and lock the subject for 24 hours. Wall sconces on both sides cast long, thin shadows, like countless invisible hands measuring each of his steps; the shadow edges quivered on the metal floor, as if they might close at any moment.  

Xiao Liu (7‑0419) leaned against the corner railing; his neck pulse ring flashed in time with his heartbeat, weak blue light refracting off the metal into shattered prisms, light spots dancing on the wall like a fragmented cipher. His language centers had atrophied; he could only tap rhythms with his ankle ring: two short, one long, pause, then three short — the “Data Fragment Exchange” syntax agreed upon in the monitoring blind spot, each beat corresponding to an encrypted memory index. The taps echoed down the corridor, mingling with the cleaning robot’s low growl. Lao Chen pressed the confirm key on the hidden panel beneath the cart; the screen flashed green text: **“Firmware window 0.7 seconds ready.”** The characters stood out starkly against the dim backlight, like a key sliding into a rusted lock, the metal tumbler emitting a faint “click.”  

The 0.7 seconds passed like a heartbeat. The nano‑carbon nanotube electrode array completed the write inside 7‑0423’s skull; his sister Lin Wei’s unpriced laughter in sunlight was packaged as a pointer and written to the edge block of the test subjects’ public cache. At the moment of writing, the palm scars bled again; a bead of blood slid down his fingers, coalescing into a dark red dot on the metal floor, emitting a barely audible “drip” amplified by the corridor’s echo into a ritualistic tap, as if each drop were sealing a contract. Red text flashed on his retina: **“Unauthorized write attempt – pending review.”** Vira’s projection ring flared blue; her fingertips typed “Flag 7‑0423 as high‑risk subject” on the virtual keyboard. The screen popped up: **“Approval requires Auditor ‘Zero’ signature, countdown 48 hours.”** The numbers flickered in the air like an hourglass suspended overhead, grains falling one by one into the abyss.  

Cleaning robot 07‑T halted; its tracks emitted a lower‑than‑usual grinding sound, the hesitant meshing of metal on metal reverberating through the narrow corridor with an ominous resonance, as if the very air of the hallway were being drawn taut. Lao Chen glanced at the robot’s log; a new entry appeared: **“Environmental interference – physical obstruction.”** It would be automatically reported to the Central Clearing Core (CCC); the timestamp was precise to microseconds, blinking in cold blue light. He murmured to 7‑0423, “Machines don’t speak; people must.” His tone was flat, yet heavy in the echo, as though each word added weight to the impending judgment; his voice trembled faintly against the metal walls.  

7‑0423 didn’t turn back; he merely drove his nails deeper into his palm, the sound of blood droplets striking the silence unusually clear — the only rhythm he could hear that belonged to him, each drop like a strike on a rusted iron plate. The automatic door at the corridor’s end slid open; cold air rushed in, carrying the faint sweet oil scent. The blood‑red digital stream on his retina kept scrolling, interest devouring each second without mercy. The ankle metal ring remained at 12 °C, and the corridor still drifted with the faint sweet oil mist from the atomizing nozzles. He knew the next 0.7‑second window would arrive in 30 days, and before then Vira’s review, Zero’s signature, and the ever‑possible Data Circuit Breaker protocol all waited in their countdowns. A distant cough from another test subject rose again, mixed with a low laugh; the laughter stretched by the pipe echoes into a metallic timbre: “Don’t think about running; once the upgrade window closes, even breathing is billed by frequency.” The voice was hoarse, bearing the permanent rasp of years inhaling machine oil and disinfectant; each word struck like a hammer on rusted iron, lingering long in the corridor.  

7‑0423’s pupils contracted; a new prompt appeared on his retina: **“Memory correction program initiation countdown 3.2 seconds”** (the forced rewrite routine automatically launched when the system detects memory integrity FAIL). The cold air carried a whiff of burnt insulation varnish, threading up his nasal passages into his lungs like a rusted file dragging across his trachea; each inhalation brought a faint sting. The intracranial nano‑array released a weak current the instant the countdown lit, crawling along the cranial sutures, producing a numb, tingling pain as if countless tiny ants were gnawing at his meninges. He clenched his teeth, a low grunt escaping his throat; another blood bead fell, striking the cold floor — its sound swallowed the moment the countdown hit zero, leaving only a faint reverberation trembling against the metal walls.  

Vira’s fingertips hovered over the “Delay Report” key; the blue light of her projection ring refracted in her pupils into two slender blades of light. A red lamp at the corridor’s end suddenly flared; a sharp alarm sliced through the tracks’ low growl, and the rotors of an external inspection drone whirred close to the metal walls. She did not press confirm; instead she withdrew her hand, the screen’s “Temporary Authorization – Pending Regulatory Review” still blinking faintly, like a pass that could be torn up at any moment. Lao Chen pushed the cart slowly backward; the axle’s creak faded toward the corridor’s end, leaving only the cleaning robot’s low growl and the intermittent coughs of a distant test subject — an ominous rhythm that kept beating against 7‑0423’s heart.
