# Chapter 2: The Friction of Fragment Exchange

The Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T’s tracks rolled across the alloy floor at the far end of the hallway, emitting a steady low‑frequency grind, like a taut steel wire being plucked repeatedly in the silence. Every fifteen meters a misting nozzle released a faintly sweet mist of lubricant oil, mingling the stale scent of machine oil and disinfectant that slipped into the nostrils, coating the throat with a thin film of oil that slid down the airway into the lungs, leaving a faint metallic tang. The metal ring around the ankle clung to the skin, its constant 12 °C chill traveling with each heartbeat to the fingertips, reminding Subject 7‑0423 of his ownership. A blood‑red stream of debt numbers scrolled across the retina at 0.8 m, the principal 1.2 × 10⁵ CR swelling with millisecond compound interest, the crimson fluid devouring the last reserves of stamina. The tongue still bore the bitter aftertaste of the forced medication; the metallic texture of the breakfast nutrient paste made a faint “crunch” between the teeth, as if chewing an unnameable contract. The distant hum of centrifuges intertwined with the laboratory’s white noise, forming a thin gray membrane that vibrated against the eardrums; even the air seemed to carry a faint vibrational frequency, turning each breath into a count against some invisible metronome.  

Old Chen pushed his maintenance cart; its wheels groaned on the same track with a duller “clack” than the cleaning robot, metal bearings sighing in the cold alloy gaps. The data port behind his left ear flickered weakly; he tapped the handle twice with his fingertips—the agreed “window open” signal for 7‑0423—applying just enough pressure for the port to sense the minute capacitance change without triggering an alarm. The upgrade window opens only 0.7 seconds every 30 days (W12), the sole interval permitting unauthorized writes; Old Chen had seeded this gap in the cleaning robot’s firmware, masking the extra command behind the rule “execute preset checklist only,” so the firmware log would show only a routine “environment scan complete.” He glanced at the flashing green light on the hidden panel beneath the cart, his fingertip brushing the cold edge, feeling a faint current crawl up his hand, then rolled the cart forward, leaving a series of shallow impressions on the track.  

7‑0423 walked along the wall; the ankle ring tightened minutely with each step, the metal clasp leaving faint pressure marks on his skin, each contraction a reminder that his body no longer belonged to him. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed dull red in the dim light; beads of blood seeped from the nail edges, trembling in the pre‑spasm of his nerves, sliding through his fingers with a cool whisper. The retinal debt stream rolled across his pupil, the interest increment visibly ballooning each second, the crimson fluid threatening to rupture the retinal membrane. He dared not glance at Regulator Vera’s projection ring; the micro‑ring implanted in her right iris glowed blue in the gloom, auditing data integrity in real time (W8)—any read/write latency exceeding 2 milliseconds would trigger a “Data Integrity Check” and lock the subject for 24 hours. Wall lamps cast long, thin shadows along the corridor, like countless invisible hands measuring his every step; the shadow edges trembled on the metal floor, as if ready to close at any moment.  

Xiao Liu (Subject 7‑0419) leaned against the corner railing; the pulse ring on his neck flashed with his heart rate, its weak blue light refracting into shattered prisms on the metal surface, dancing across the wall like a fragmented code. His language centers had atrophied; he could only tap rhythms with his ankle ring: two short, one long, pause, three short—the syntax of “Memory Fragment Exchange” agreed upon in the monitoring blind spot, each beat mapping to an encrypted memory index, the taps echoing in the hallway amid the cleaning robot’s low growl. Old Chen pressed the confirmation key on the hidden panel beneath the cart; the screen flashed green text: **“Firmware window 0.7 s ready”**, the characters stark against the dim backlight, like a key sliding into a rusted lock, the tumblers emitting a faint “click.”  

0.7 seconds passed like a single heartbeat. The nano‑carbon nanotube electrode array inside 7‑0423’s skull completed the write; the unpriced laughter of his sister Lin Wei, bathed in sunlight, was encapsulated as a pointer and written into the edge block of the test‑subject public cache. At the instant of writing, the palm scars wept blood again; droplets traced his fingers, coalescing into dark red spots on the metal floor, emitting an almost inaudible “drip” amplified by the corridor’s reverberation into a ritualistic percussion, as if each drop sealed a contract. The retina flashed red: **“Unauthorized write attempt – pending review.”** Vera’s projection ring blazed blue; her fingertip typed “Mark 7‑0423 as high‑risk subject” on the virtual keyboard, and the screen popped up: **“Approval requires Auditor ‘Zero’ signature, countdown 48 hours.”** The numbers flickered in the air like an hourglass suspended overhead, grains falling one by one into the abyss.  

Cleaning Robot 07‑T hesitated, its tracks emitting a deeper, more ominous grind; the reluctant meshing of metal on metal reverberated through the narrow corridor, as if the very air had been drawn taut. Old Chen glanced at the robot’s log; a new entry appeared: **“Environmental interference – physical obstruction,”** slated for automatic upload to the Central Clearing Core, its timestamp precise to microseconds, blinking cold blue. He muttered to 7‑0423, “Machines don’t talk; people have to.” His tone was flat, yet the echo gave it weight, as if each word added to the impending judgment, the sound refracting off the metal walls with a subtle tremor.  

7‑0423 did not turn; he only drove his nails deeper into his palm, the sound of blood droplets striking the silence exceptionally clear—the only rhythm he could hear that belonged to him, each drop striking a rusted iron plate. The automatic door at the corridor’s end slid open, cold air laced with the sweet oil scent rushing in; the retinal blood‑red debt stream kept scrolling, interest devouring every second without mercy. He knew the next 0.7‑second window would arrive in 30 days, and before then Vera’s review, Zero’s signature, and the ever‑possible “Data Circuit Breaker” protocol all ticked down in the countdown. A distant cough from another test subject rose again, mixed with a low laugh; the laugh stretched by the ductwork into a metallic warble: “Don’t think about running; once the upgrade window closes, even breathing gets billed by the beat.” The voice was raspy, permanently hoarse from years of inhaling machine oil and disinfectant; each word struck like a hammer on rusted iron, lingering long in the hallway.  

7‑0423’s pupils contracted; a new prompt floated on his retina: **“Memory correction program initiation countdown 3.2 seconds”** (the forced rewrite automatically launched when the system detects memory integrity FAIL). A whiff of burnt insulation varnish rode the cold air into his lungs, like a rusted file dragging back and forth in his trachea; each inhalation brought a faint sting. The intracranial nano‑array released a weak current the moment the countdown lit, crawling along the cranial sutures, delivering a numb, tingling pain as if countless tiny ants gnawed at his meninges. He clenched his teeth, a low muffled groan escaping his throat; blood droplets fell again, striking the cold floor, their sound swallowed the instant the countdown hit zero, leaving only a faint reverberation trembling on the metal walls.  

“Signature verified.” Vera’s voice rang from the projection ring, cold and sharp as a blade across glass. “Auditor ‘Zero’ confirmed; window extended to 72 hours.” The red countdown vanished, replaced by a line of pale blue **“Temporary authorization – pending regulatory review,”** the characters glowing faintly in the air like a fragile pass. Old Chen eased the cart backward; the axle’s “clack” faded toward the corridor’s end, leaving only the cleaning robot’s low growl and the intermittent coughs of distant test subjects—an ominous cadence continuously hammering against 7‑0423’s heart.
