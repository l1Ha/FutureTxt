# Chapter 1: Debt Tower Dawn

The faint sweet‑oil scent of the respiratory lubricant seeped through the micro‑vibration of the bulkhead, mingling with the steady rolling friction of the corridor cleaning robots drilling into the eardrums. The cold bite of the metal cuff against the ankle felt like a thin chain of ice; every heartbeat reminded Subject 7‑0423 of his ownership. The crimson digit stream of the retinal bill hovered sharply at 0.8 meters, the principal 1.2 × 10⁵ CR compounding per millisecond (W2), interest accruing like breath, never repayable.  

The bitter aftertaste of the forced medication still lingered at the root of the tongue; the metallic chew of the breakfast nutrient paste emitted a faint “crunch” between the teeth, as if gnawing on some unnameable contract. Laboratory white noise and the low hum of centrifuges seeped from the vents, wrapping the lower levels of the Debt Tower in a stale rust odor—each inhalation like swallowing iron filings.  

Subject 7‑0423 opened his eyes. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed dull red in the dim light; the nail edges trembled, bleeding. He habitually scored his palm with his nails to confirm reality; pain was the sole coordinate not quantified by the ledger. Fingertips brushed the cold alloy wall, leaving a minute indentation, a silent protest against the closed loop.  

“Bill Wake” – a low‑frequency vibration against the sternum – arrived together with the sweet‑oil scent, completing the daily 06:00 physiological alarm. Heart rate locked at 14 bpm; any fluctuation was ruled a violation. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) had already uploaded cortisol and neural potentials to the Central Clearing Core (CCC). The vibration resonated in the chest cavity, forcing respiration into sync with the system; even the thyroid cartilage trembled.  

The routine Neural Reorganization Experiment initiated; the carbon‑nanotube electrode array pierced the nerves within two milliseconds (W8), like ice needles slicing consciousness. Data flickered across the retina, then an unauthorized fragment intruded: sunlight on grass, the laughter of his sister Lin Wei—an amber warmth beyond pricing, incalculable. The cortex spasmed instantly; the palm scars bled harder, droplets sliding through the fingers to land as dark red dots on the metal floor, a barely audible “tap” swallowed by the corridor’s low hum.  

An anomalous line appeared on the retinal bill—  

**Memory Integrity Check: FAIL**  

Countdown 0.7 seconds; the System Upgrade Window (W12) was about to open. The air thickened with a sweet‑metallic blend of disinfectant and machine oil; each breath felt like swallowing liquid lead. The cleaning robot at the corridor’s end halted, its red lamp casting a thin ribbon of light across Subject 7‑0423’s clenched jaw. He did not move, only let his fingertips circle the wound on his palm, feeling pain nail his awareness to the present, preventing memory from being flattened.  

A distant inmate, 9‑317, coughed low, the sound mingling with the hiss of a pipe vent— as if the entire Debt Tower held its breath. The cough carried a rust‑iron tang, lungs seemingly abraded by metal dust. Subject 7‑0423’s pupils contracted; **FAIL** still blinked, as though something cold and logical crawled up from the pipe depths, bearing a silent verdict.  

He closed his eyes, trying to grasp the sunlight and laughter, but touched only cold electrodes and the scrolling red numbers. The System Upgrade Window tightened like an invisible net, awaiting the next pulse to erase the neural signals flagged as “violation.”  

Countdown hit zero; the bulkhead tremor intensified, the sweet‑oil scent grew stronger. Subject 7‑0423 clenched his fist, nails digging deeper into his palm; the sound of blood droplets striking the floor rang unusually clear in the silence— the only beat that belonged to him.  

Blood hit the deck; the cleaning robot surged to life, tracks screeching a sharp “zzzt.” Red light swept the walls, stretching shadows. A mechanical arm sprayed a fine mist; the sting of disinfectant and machine oil pierced the nostrils like rusted iron filings forced up the nose. He did not dodge, letting the fog envelop his fingertips; microscopic droplets hissed on the wound, as if countless ants gnawed at nerve endings.  

“Abnormal neural fluctuation detected, Subject 7‑0423, initiating forced synchronization.” The voice seeped from the bulkhead’s directional speakers, low and temperature‑less, each syllable accompanied by a faint electric tremor against sternum, temple, tooth roots. Heart rate remained locked at 14 bpm; the cortisol curve spiked, a red warning bar flashing across the retina as if to split the visual field.  

He ground his molars, a muffled growl in the throat; nails drove deeper, fresh blood seeping through the fingers to strike the cold alloy with a crisp “tap.” Pain acted like a nail pinning consciousness, stopping the sunlight and laughter from being devoured by the data stream. The retinal digit flow accelerated; **Memory Integrity Check: FAIL** remained glaring, now followed by a new tag—  

**Sync Progress: 12%**  

At the far end of the corridor, 9‑317 coughed again, the sound tearing through the low‑frequency pulse: “Another ‘upgrade’? Do they think we’re hard drives that can be memory‑wiped at will?”  

Subject 7‑0423 did not turn; his fingertips traced slow circles in the blood ring on his palm, each revolution like oiling an ancient gear. The sweet‑oil scent thickened; the bulkhead tremor climbed the spine to the brainstem; teeth clicked faintly.  

Countdown zeroed; the red light snapped out, plunging the corridor into absolute darkness, leaving only the directional speaker’s low‑frequency pulse beating like a heart against metal plates. Subject 7‑0423 kept his eyes shut, fingertips still circling the wound; the blood‑drop “tap” rang sharply in the quiet— his sole, private metronome.  

In the dark, the low‑frequency pulse made the metal plates tingle; blood droplets on his palm cooled into tiny ice beads, each fall detonating a clear “tap” that echoed amid the robot’s track hum. The robot did not stop; after the red lamp died it continued along its preset path, nozzles emitting disinfectant mist that condensed into a pale white veil. The rust and bitter almond odor bored into the nasal passages, slid down the trachea to the lung lobes; every breath felt like swallowing metal shavings.  

“Sync Progress: 27%” flashed on the retina; the red warning bar cleaved the view like a crack. Heart rate still locked at 14 bpm; cortisol spiked to a needle point; the temporal arteries throbbed as if trying to split the skull. He did not open his eyes; fingertips kept circling the wound, guarding memory from consumption.  

9‑317 coughed again, voice rasping through the pulse: “Still ‘upgrading’? Do they think we’re drives that can be memory‑wiped anytime?”  

Subject 7‑0423’s fingertips paused in the blood circle; nails drove deeper, fresh red dripping onto the alloy with a sharp “tap.” Pain nailed consciousness; the retinal digit stream surged, **FAIL** persisting, now followed by—  

**Sync Progress: 41%**  

The low‑frequency pulse quickened; the bulkhead tremor turned violent; teeth clicked. The cleaning robot’s tracks screeched “zzzt” again; red light flared, shadows stretched anew. The mechanical arm sprayed a denser mist; the stench stabbed like rusted iron filings. He did not evade, letting the fog wrap his fingertips; microscopic droplets hissed on the wound, like ants gnawing nerves.  

“Abnormal neural fluctuation detected, Subject 7‑0423, initiating forced synchronization.” The voice seeped again from the bulkhead, low, temperature‑less, each word riding a faint electric shudder against sternum, temple, tooth roots. Heart rate locked at 14 bpm; cortisol curve peaked; the red warning bar flashed, threatening to split the visual field.  

In the darkness, the low‑frequency pulse made the metal plates tingle; blood beads on his palm cooled into fine ice pearls, each drop exploding a crisp “tap” in the silence, reverberating among the robot’s tracks. The robot did not halt; after the red lamp died it kept gliding, nozzles releasing disinfectant mist that formed a pale white curtain. Rust and bitter almond odor drilled into the nostrils, slid down the airway to the lung lobes; every inhalation felt like swallowing metal dust.  

“Sync Progress: 27%” flickered; the red warning bar cleaved the view. Heart rate still locked at 14 bpm; cortisol spiked; temporal arteries throbbed as if to burst the skull. He kept his eyes shut; fingertips continued their circles, staving off memory erasure.  

9‑317 coughed once more, hoarse through the pulse: “Still ‘upgrading’? Do they think we’re drives that can be memory‑wiped at any moment?”  

Subject 7‑0423’s fingertips froze in the blood ring; nails sank deeper, fresh red striking the alloy with a clear “tap.” Pain pinned awareness; the retinal digit stream accelerated, **FAIL** still blazing, now trailed by—  

**Sync Progress: 41%**

The mechanical arm sprayed an even thicker mist; the odor stabbed like rusted iron filings. Heart rate remained locked at 14 bpm; cortisol curve showed a sharp peak; the red warning bar flashed, as if to cleave the field of vision.
