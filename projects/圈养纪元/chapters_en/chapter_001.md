Subject 7‑0423 opened his eyes. Three parallel scars on the palm of his left hand glowed dull red in the dim light; blood seeped from the edges of his nails, trembling. He was accustomed to scoring his palm with his nails to confirm reality—pain the only coordinate not quantified by the ledger. Fingertips brushed the cold alloy wall, leaving a faint indentation, a silent protest against the closed loop.  

“Bill Wake” low‑frequency vibration traveled against the sternum, completing the daily 06:00 physiological alarm together with the faint sweet oil scent. Heart rate locked at 14 beats per minute; any fluctuation was ruled a violation. The Global Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) had already uploaded cortisol and neural potentials to the Central Clearing Core (CCC). The vibration resonated in the chest cavity, forcing breath to synchronize with the system; even the laryngeal cartilage trembled.  

Routine neural reconfiguration experiment initiated. The nanocarbon‑tube electrode array pierced the nerves within two milliseconds (W8), like ice needles slicing through consciousness. Data streams flickered on the retina; an unauthorized fragment suddenly intruded: sunlight spilling onto grass, his sister Lin Wei’s laughter like unpriced amber—warm and incalculable. The cortex spasmed instantly; palm scars bled harder, blood beads slipping through fingers, coalescing into dark‑red dots on the metal floor with a faint “tap,” swallowed by the corridor’s low‑frequency hum.  

An anomalous line appeared on the retinal bill—  

**Memory Integrity Check: FAIL**  

Countdown 0.7 seconds; the System Upgrade Window (W12) about to open. The air thickened with a cloying mix of disinfectant and machine oil; each breath felt like swallowing liquid lead. At the corridor’s end the cleaning robot halted, its red light casting a thin beam across Subject 7‑0423’s clenched jaw. He did not move; he merely let his fingertips trace the wound on his palm again and again, feeling pain anchor his consciousness in the present, preventing his memories from being erased.  

In the distance, prisoner 9‑317 coughed low, mingled with the hiss of pipe venting, as if the entire Debt Tower held its breath. The cough carried a rusty iron tang, lungs seeming abraded by metal dust. Subject 7‑0423’s pupils contracted slightly; FAIL still blinked, as though something crawled up from the depths of the pipes, bearing cold logic and silent judgment.  

He closed his eyes, trying to grasp the sunlight and laughter, only touching cold electrodes and scrolling red numbers. The System Upgrade Window tightened like an invisible net, awaiting the next pulse to erase the neural signals flagged as “violation.”  

Countdown reached zero; the hull’s micro‑tremors intensified, the faint sweet oil scent growing stronger. Subject 7‑0423 clenched his fist, nails digging into his palm again; the sound of blood drops hitting the floor rang unusually clear in the silence—the only beat he could hear that belonged to him.  

Blood hit the floor; the cleaning robot sprang to life, its tracks emitting a sharp “zzzt,” red light sweeping the walls and stretching shadows. The manipulator sprayed a fine mist; the acrid blend of disinfectant and machine oil stung the nostrils like rusted iron filings. He did not dodge, letting the mist envelop his fingertips; tiny droplets hissed on the wound, as if countless ants were gnawing at nerve endings.  

“Abnormal neural fluctuation detected, Subject 7‑0423, initiating forced synchronization.” The voice seeped from the directional speakers in the hull, low and temperatureless, each word accompanied by a faint current trembling against sternum, temple, tooth roots. Heart rate remained locked at 14; cortisol curve spiked; a red warning bar flickered on the retina, as if trying to split his vision.  

He clenched his molars, a muffled growl in his throat; nails dug into his palm again, blood seeping through fingers, dripping onto cold alloy with a crisp “tap.” Pain nailed his consciousness in place, stopping sunlight and laughter from being devoured by the data stream. The retinal number stream accelerated; **Memory Integrity Check: FAIL** still glared, followed by a new tag—  

**Synchronization Progress: 12%**  

At the other end of the corridor, 9‑317 coughed again, mixed with pipe vent hiss, like a rusted bellows gasping desperately. He huddled in shadow, left arm wrapped in a worn conduit, its tip flashing faint green; his murky yet sharp eyes curved into a mocking arc: “The system wants another ‘upgrade’? Do they really think we’re hard drives they can format at will?”  

Subject 7‑0423 did not turn; his fingertips slowly traced circles in the blood on his palm, each revolution like oiling an ancient timing gear. The faint sweet oil scent thickened; hull micro‑tremors crawled up his spine to the back of his skull; teeth clicked faintly.

The robot did not stop; after the red light died it continued along its preset path, nozzles spraying disinfectant mist that condensed into a pale white veil; rust and bitter almond odors pierced the nostrils, sliding down the trachea into the lobes, each breath like swallowing metal shavings.

Heart rate still locked at 14; cortisol curve climbed to a peak; temples throbbed as if trying to split the skull. He did not open his eyes; fingertips kept circling the wound, staving off memory consumption.  

9‑317 coughed again, voice rasping through the low‑frequency pulses: “Another ‘upgrade’? Do they really think we’re hard drives that can be formatted anytime?”
