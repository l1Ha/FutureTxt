# Chapter 1: The Faint Sweet Oil Scent of Awakening

A faint sweet oil scent from the breathing‑tube lubricant seeped through the hull’s micro‑vibrations, mingling with the even, rolling friction of the Corridor Cleaning Robot 07‑T drilling into the eardrums. The cold touch of the metal anklet against skin felt like a thin chain of ice; every heartbeat reminded Subject 7‑0423 of his ownership. The crimson debt‑ledger numbers streaming across his retina were already razor‑sharp at 0.8 meters: principal 1.2 × 10⁵ Credit Points (CR) compounding per millisecond (W2), interest flowing like breath, never to be repaid.  

The bitter aftertaste of the mandatory medication still lingered at the root of his tongue; the metallic texture of the breakfast nutrient paste emitted a faint “crunch” between his teeth, as if he were chewing an unsigned contract. Laboratory white noise and the low drone of centrifuges seeped through the vents, wrapping the entire lower level of the Debt Tower, laced with stale rust; each inhalation felt like swallowing rusted iron filings.  

Subject 7‑0423 opened his eyes. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed a dull red in the dim light, the edges of his fingernails trembling with seeped blood. He habitually scored his palm with his nails to confirm reality—pain was the sole coordinate the ledger could not quantify. Fingertips brushed the cold alloy wall, leaving a minute indentation, a silent protest against the closed loop.  

“Bill Wake” low‑frequency vibration traveled against his sternum, pairing with the faint sweet oil scent to complete the daily 06:00 physiological alarm. His heart rate was locked at 14 beats per minute; any fluctuation was ruled a violation, and the full‑spectrum Physiological Monitoring Network (W6) had already uploaded cortisol levels and neural potentials to the Central Clearing Core (CCC). The vibration reverberated in his chest, forcing his breathing into sync with the system; even his thyroid cartilage trembled faintly.  

The routine Neural Reorganization experiment launched; a nanocarbon‑tube electrode array pierced his nerves within two milliseconds (W8), like ice needles slicing through consciousness. Data streams flickered across his retina, then an unauthorized fragment intruded: sunlight on grass, the laughter of his sister Lin Wei like an unpriced amber—warm and incalculable. His cortex spasmed instantly; the palm scars bled harder, droplets sliding through his fingers to congeal into dark red beads on the metal deck, emitting a barely audible “tap” that was swallowed by the corridor’s low hum.  

An anomalous line appeared on the retinal ledger—  

**Memory Integrity Check: FAIL**  

The system’s reserved 0.7‑second firmware window had already passed; he could only wait for the next cycle. The air reeked of disinfectant mixed with machine oil, a cloying sweet‑metallic tang; each breath felt like swallowing liquid lead. At the corridor’s far end the cleaning robot halted, its red lamp casting a thin ribbon of light across 7‑0423’s clenched jaw. He did not move, merely let his fingertips circle the wound on his palm, feeling pain pin his awareness to the present, preventing the memory from being erased.  

A distant prisoner, 9‑317, coughed low, the sound tangled with the hiss of a venting pipe, as if the whole Debt Tower held its breath. The cough carried a rust‑iron tang, lungs seemingly abraded by metal dust. 7‑0423’s pupils contracted; **FAIL** still blinked, as though something were crawling up from the pipe’s depths, bearing cold logic and silent judgment.  

He closed his eyes, trying to grasp the sunlight and laughter, only touching cold electrodes and scrolling red digits. The System Upgrade Window (W12) tightened like an invisible net, awaiting the next pulse to purge the neural signals flagged as “violation.”  

The hull’s micro‑vibrations intensified; the faint sweet oil scent grew stronger. 7‑0423 clenched his fist, nails digging into his palm again; the sound of blood droplets hitting the floor rang unusually clear in the silence—his sole, private metronome.  

Blood hit the deck; the cleaning robot jerked to life, tracks screeching a sharp “zzzt,” red light sweeping the walls and stretching shadows. A manipulator arm sprayed a fine mist; the sharp sting of disinfectant and oil pierced the nostrils like rusted iron filings forced up the nose. He did not dodge, letting the mist envelop his fingertips; microscopic droplets hissed on the wound, like countless ants gnawing at nerve endings.  

“Abnormal neural fluctuation detected, Subject 7‑0423, initiating forced synchronization.” The voice seeped from the hull’s directional speakers, low and temperature‑less, each word accompanied by a faint electric tremor against sternum, temple, tooth roots. Heart rate remained locked at 14; the cortisol curve already spiked; a red warning bar flashed across his retina, as if trying to split his vision.  

He ground his molars, a muffled growl in his throat; nails drove deeper into his palm, fresh blood seeping through the fingers to tap crisply on the cold alloy. Pain nailed his consciousness, stopping the sunlight and laughter from being devoured by the data stream. The retinal digit flow accelerated; **Memory Integrity Check: FAIL** still blazed, followed by a new tag—  

**Sync Progress: 12%**  

At the opposite corridor end, 9‑317 coughed again, the sound mingling with pipe venting like a desperate, rusted bellows. He huddled in shadow, left arm wrapped in a worn conduit, its tip flickering a faint green; his murky yet sharp eyes curled into a mocking smirk: “The system wants another ‘upgrade’? Do they really think we’re hard drives they can format at will?”  

7‑0423 did not turn; his fingertips traced slow circles in the blood ring on his palm, each revolution like oiling an ancient timing gear. The faint sweet oil scent thickened; hull vibrations crawled up his spine to the back of his skull; teeth clicked faintly together.  

The countdown hit zero; the red lights snapped out, plunging the corridor into absolute darkness, leaving only the directional speaker’s low‑frequency pulse hammering like a heartbeat on metal plates. 7‑0423 kept his eyes shut, fingertips still circling his palm; the blood‑drop “tap” rang sharply in the silence—his only, personal rhythm.  

The low‑frequency pulse made the metal plates tingle; blood beads in his palm cooled into tiny ice pearls, each fall detonating a “tap” that echoed amid the robot’s track hum. The robot did not stop; after the red died it continued along its preset path, nozzles spraying disinfectant mist that coalesced into a pale white veil. Rust and bitter almond odors bored into his nasal passages, sliding down the trachea to the alveoli; every breath felt like inhaling metal shavings.  

“Sync Progress: 27%” flickered on his retina; the red warning bar cleaved his vision like a crack. Heart rate still locked at 14; cortisol curve spiked to a needle; temporal arteries throbbed as if trying to burst his skull. He did not open his eyes, fingertips persisting in their circles, staving off the memory’s consumption.  

9‑317 coughed again, voice hoarse cutting through the low‑frequency pulse: “Another ‘upgrade’? Do they really think we’re drives that can be formatted anytime?”  

7‑0423’s fingertips paused in the blood ring, nails biting flesh anew; fresh red welled and tapped on the alloy. Pain nailed his awareness; the retinal digit stream surged, **FAIL** persisting, followed by—  

**Sync Progress: 41%**  

The low‑frequency pulse accelerated; hull micro‑vibrations turned into violent tremors; teeth clicked sharply. The cleaning robot’s tracks screeched “zzzt” once more; red light flared, shadows stretched again. The manipulator arm sprayed a denser mist, the stench sharp as rusted iron filings. He did not evade, letting the mist wrap his fingertips; microscopic droplets hissed on the wound, like ants gnawing nerves.  

“Abnormal neural fluctuation detected, Subject 7‑0423, initiating forced synchronization.” The voice seeped again from the hull, low and cold, each word riding a current tremor against sternum, temple, tooth roots. Heart rate still locked at 14; cortisol curve showed a spike; red warning bars flashed, threatening to split his vision.  

**Memory Integrity Check: FAIL** still burned on his retina; his heart missed a beat, breath caught in his throat, vision shattered into countless jagged light shards, like a film strip ripped apart by blinding light. Every heartbeat thudded dully in his chest; blood at his fingertips froze into ice beads, each drop’s “tap” piercing the absolute silence—sharp, solitary—the final rhythm the system could never erase.
