The laboratory white noise and the low drone of centrifuges seeped through the vents, wrapping the lower levels of the Debt Tower, laced with a stale rust odor—every inhalation felt like swallowing rusted iron filings.  

7‑0423 opened his eyes. Three parallel scars on his left palm glowed dull red in the dim light; the edges of his fingernails trembled with a thin bleed. He habitually scored his palm with his nails to confirm reality; pain was the sole coordinate the ledger could not quantify. Fingertips brushed the cold alloy wall, leaving a minute indentation, a silent protest against the closed loop.  

“Bill wake‑up” low‑frequency vibration traveled against the sternum, completing the daily 06:00 physiological alarm together with the faint sweet oil scent. The routine neural reorganization experiment initiated; the carbon‑nanotube electrode array pierced the nerves in two milliseconds (W8), like ice needles slicing through consciousness. The cerebral cortex spasmed instantly; the palm scars bled more fiercely, blood beads sliding through the fingers and coalescing into dark red dots on the metal floor, emitting a barely audible “tap” swallowed by the corridor’s low hum.  

An anomalous line appeared on the retinal bill—  

**Memory Integrity Check: FAIL**  

The system’s reserved 0.7‑second firmware window had already passed; only the next cycle remained. He did not move, merely letting his fingertips trace the wound on his palm again and again, using pain to nail his awareness to the present, preventing memory from being flattened.  

A distant prisoner, 9‑317, coughed low, the sound intertwining with the hiss of a venting pipe, as if the entire Debt Tower held its breath. The cough carried a rusted‑iron tang, lungs seemingly abraded by metallic dust. 7‑0423’s pupils contracted; **FAIL** continued to blink, as though something cold and logical was climbing from the pipe’s depths, bearing a silent verdict.  

He closed his eyes, trying to grasp the sunlight and laughter, but only felt the cold electrodes and the scrolling red numbers. The system upgrade window tightened like an invisible net, awaiting the next pulse to erase the neural signals marked “violation.”  

The bulkhead tremor intensified; the faint sweet oil scent grew stronger. 7‑0423 clenched his fist, nails digging deeper into his palm; the sound of blood droplets hitting the floor was starkly clear in the silence—the sole rhythm that belonged to him.  

Blood hit the deck; the cleaning robot surged to life, its tracks screeching a sharp “zzz,” the red beam stretching shadows across the wall. A mechanical arm sprayed a fine mist; the acrid mix of disinfectant and machine oil stabbed the nostrils like rusted iron filings forced into the nasal cavity. He did not dodge, allowing the mist to envelop his fingertips; microscopic droplets hissed on the wound, as if countless ants gnawed at nerve endings.  

“Abnormal neural fluctuation detected, subject 7‑0423, initiating forced synchronization.” The voice seeped from the bulkhead’s directional speaker, low and temperature‑less, each word accompanied by a faint current tremor against the sternum, temporal bone, and tooth roots. He ground his molars, a muffled grunt in his throat, nails driving deeper, fresh blood seeping through the fingers and dripping onto the cold alloy with a crisp “tap.” Pain acted like nails pinning consciousness, stopping the sunlight and laughter from being devoured by the data stream. The retinal numbers accelerated; **Memory Integrity Check: FAIL** remained glaring, followed by a new tag—  

**Synchronization Progress: 12%**  

At the opposite end of the corridor, 9‑317’s cough erupted again, tearing through the low‑frequency pulse: “The system wants another ‘upgrade’? Do they really think we’re hard drives they can format at will?”

The scent of rust and bitter almond drilled into the nasal passages, sliding down the trachea to the lung lobes; every breath felt like inhaling metal shavings.  

“Synchronization Progress: 27%” flashed on the retina; the red warning bar cleaved the view like a crack. Heart rate still locked at 14 bpm; the cortisol curve sharpened to a peak, temples throbbing as if trying to burst the skull.
