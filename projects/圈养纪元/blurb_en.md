In a world where labor has been entirely outsourced to machines and algorithms, humanity has fractured into three castes: the creditor‑elite who price every breath, the nominally free who scrape by on credit, and the “subjects” — numbered, chipped, and bred for endless pharmacological, neural, and behavioral trials.  Debt accrues in real‑time, compounding per second, guaranteeing that no obligation can ever be cleared.  Subject 7‑0423 endures routine neural re‑mapping until a stray memory surfaces: a sister’s laughter, sunlight that carries no price tag, a name erased from every ledger.  The very existence of a past contradicts the doctrine that subjects have no history, and now each mandatory scan, each nutrient paste ration, each projected debt statement becomes a covert search for the system’s physical fissures.  Hunted by the architecture that created him, 7‑0423 must decide whether to exploit the cracks or be consumed by the interest that never sleeps.
