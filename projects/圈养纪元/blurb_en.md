In a world where labor has been wholly outsourced to machines and algorithms, humanity has fractured into three castes: the creditor‑overlords who price every breath, the nominally free who scrape by on credit, and the “subjects” — numbered, chipped, and bred from birth for endless pharmacological, neural, and behavioral trials. The system runs on a predatory credit currency that compounds interest by the second, guaranteeing that debt can never be repaid. Subject 7‑0423, during a routine neural‑reassembly, unexpectedly accesses a fragment of memory that belongs to no file: a sister’s laughter, sunlight without a price tag, and a name the regime erased. The very existence of that memory violates the doctrine that subjects have no past, and now every mandatory scan, every nutrient paste, every projected debt statement becomes a hunt for the physical cracks in an order designed to be airtight.
