In 2126, Nanyu City unveils the centerpiece of the Pan-Pacific Summit: a living Kunpeng, traced by light arrays across the bay and filled by 217 migrating white-naped cranes. Xu Dehai, the last hereditary crane herder, commands the flock with only a flag and whistle, pitted against eighteen directional speakers and a behavioral model calculated eight thousand times. The algorithm offers a brutal optimization: eject the crippled lead bird, "Number Three," and formation probability jumps from seventy-nine to ninety-six percent. His contract is binary—seven successful formations earn his craft Intangible Heritage status and a pension; failure revokes his license and demands a ruinous penalty. On the seventh attempt, the wind shifts. The model demands maximum output—130 decibels, a redline that would panic Number Three and seven followers into the light rigs. With the world watching, the choice collapses to a single binary: sign the authorization for a perfect symbol, or cut the power and let the birds fly their own imperfect truth.
