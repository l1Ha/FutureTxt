# Chapter 4: Formation Match

At four a.m. on the thirtieth, the Old House.

Father was coughing. A cloth pressed against his mouth, muffling the sound — once, twice, three times. Xu Dehai stood in the doorway for a moment, did not go in.

He took the flag from the windowsill. White cloth with red trim, one foot eight inches, the pole bamboo, stuck in a brick joint, stuck there for over a decade, the bricks in the joint loosened.

"Your mother embroidered it." Xu Changgeng said in the darkness.

"Mm."

"She only knew how to embroider cranes."

Xu Dehai rolled the flag, tucked it under his arm.

---

On the command platform, Qi Hai was waiting for him.

Dawn had not broken, the sea was black, the distant venue's silhouette outlined in lights.

The execution order had expired at midnight last night. He hadn't slept all night; the creases on that paper inside his padded jacket had been pressed soft. The paper had expired, the man had not.

"Sign or not." Qi Hai asked.

Xu Dehai pulled the paper folded in quarters from his jacket, unfolded it. The signature block was empty. He folded it again, handed it back.

"Birds don't recognize paper."

Qi Hai took it, glanced at the creases, nothing beyond the creases. He folded it in half, slipped it into an inner pocket.

"Master Xu," he said, "I've given you several options."

"You've urged me three times."

"Did you listen once?"

Xu Dehai turned and walked toward the tidal flat.

"Old Xu." Qi Hai called from behind.

He looked back.

"Ninety-six and seventy-nine." Qi Hai said, "I gave you two numbers. Don't blame me later for giving you something else."

---

Five forty, Baisha Spit.

The barriers had been pulled up before dawn, blue panels running from the highway edge to the tidal flat edge, enclosing the whole of Baisha Spit. Welcome slogans and maps printed on the panels. Dogs had been led away long ago. Only him on the flats.

Unit 03 stood on the east side.

Still in its old position, between two wooden stakes. He hadn't removed those stakes, hadn't taken the twelve thousand, and now they stood under the lights like two stakes grown from the mud.

At four fifty, the light array rose.

Two hundred forty drones lifted from both sides of the Yubei Corridor, fanning out over the sea. Each carried a dense panel of cool white lights, hundreds of points connecting into lines. Wind came from the north, the array stabilized, 170,000 light points beginning to converge on a shape.

That bird slowly grew in the eastern sky. Wings first two faint traces, then bone, then feathers, extending toward the wingtips one layer at a time. Wingspan three hundred meters, longer than the cross-sea bridge of Nanyu Bay.

Daylight brightened bit by bit. A little brighter, and this bird would vanish — the dot-matrix relied on the difference between sky and light to show form.

Xu Dehai stood in the mud, flag under his arm.

Five fifty-eight, the first speaker group began working. Sound pushed up from beneath the tidal flats, low, muffled. The crane flock rose in the shallows.

"Master Xu." Zhou Bai in the earpiece, "Array standing by. Model recommends high power, one-fifteen to one-thirty decibels, within four minutes, match rate can reach ninety."

"Who signed."

"No one. Safety redline needs Engineer Wei to sign."

Two seconds of silence in the earpiece.

"I won't sign." Wei Xiaoqing said, "High power, no sign. One-thirty decibels is the redline value, sign it and there's no way back. Three reasons. First, hard push will flush Unit 03 and the seven behind it out of the safety radius, farthest push three hundred meters, exit under those three light towers. Model gives collision probability, twelve percent. Second, four of those seven are this year's new birds, first time with full flight feathers. Third —" she paused, "Third, I won't sign, I have no on-site signature."

"Master Xu." Zhou Bai said, "Camera window six-ten to six-eleven-thirty."

Xu Dehai watched the east.

Unit 03 rose.

It left the water first, wings beating slow, left side not lifting high, whole body listing right. After rising it didn't head for the southern airspace, it veered north a little, bringing a flock behind — a scattered, crooked flock, one bird fallen back chasing to catch up.

Same as every year.

"Master Xu." Qi Hai in the earpiece, voice lower than usual, "I recommend high power. If not executed, formation dispersion probability exceeds standard in four minutes. For the summit image, please confirm."

Xu Dehai did not answer.

He stepped back two paces, the control box underfoot, iron, a knife switch on the cover, counterclockwise to cut.

Zhou Bai three meters beside him.

"No backup if cut." Zhou Bai said.

"I know."

Xu Dehai reached out, gripped the knife switch handle, turned counterclockwise.

Click.

Eighteen speaker groups fell abruptly silent. The wall squeezing the birds in the middle was gone.

The crane flock no longer pressed by anything. They scattered in the eastern sky, formation breaking into segments, then one segment held itself together — Unit 03 leading a dozen north, the rest hesitating, circling half a turn in place.

That glowing Kunpeng still in the sky. Wings spread, nothing inside the wings.

A small screen on the control box side, the director's signal return.

A tiny speaker emitted: "All positions, Nanyu side, standing by."

Screen showed sea first. Daylight just enough, sea gray-white. Then that bird.

A glowing one, huge, wings spread across the sea, from eastern sky all the way to western sky. Xu Dehai had seen this shape, in the city's promo reels, on the airport hall's looping screens.

Then the flock entered.

Not as he remembered. They didn't queue, didn't fill gaps, inserted diagonally from the right, chaotic, several nearly falling out, one banking sharply and returning.

They flew past the front of that glowing bird.

One flew very slow, trailing the formation, one wing high one wing low.

Ninety seconds.

The glowing outline on screen remained, wings empty, that small cluster of white dots passing through the void.

"Next item." Director said.

---

Xu Dehai stood in the mud, did not move.

Cold wind funneled into his cuffs. Mud sucked at his soles, pull once, a wet sound.

Later he checked his watch once. Six forty-two. The light array's points still burning, would burn until daylight fully overwhelmed them. That bird's shape fading bit by bit on the daytime sea, wings gone first, then body, finally tail.

Unit 03 and the rest gone north.

A Cityscape Platform vehicle stopped at the barrier entrance. Driver honked once, didn't get out. Xu Dehai walked out of the mud, shoes stuck, a wet sound pulling free. Zhou Bai collected his log board, followed behind.

Seven ten, they reached the command center.

On the platform, two people in reflective vests entering light array coordinates into tablets; another group went to Baisha Spit to dismantle barriers. No one came to ask him.

Second floor corridor window open. Cui Shouzhuo stood at the window, the "Release Consultant" badge on his chest catching morning light. He just stood there, didn't come down.

"Master Xu." Zhou Bai walked over, log board in hand, "Array shut down by you?"

"Me."

"Have to write it in the log." Zhou Bai wrote on the board, "Reason."

"No reason."

"Then write commander's judgment."

"Write it."

---

Nine a.m., command center meeting.

"Match rate zero." Zhou Bai said, "No cranes entered outline in footage. Light array normal, dot-matrix error within 1.2 meters. Array ceased operation at six-ten-zero-four."

Qi Hai sat at the head of the long table. A paper before him.

"Master Xu," he said, "Per contract, seventh failed match. First, your collaboration qualification revoked, effective immediately you may not enter any Baisha Spit work zone. Second, compensation 120,000 — training, equipment occupancy, and channel fees, due by November 10. Third, the Crane Herding intangible heritage registration, not filed this year."

"Mm."

"One more." Qi Hai said, "Your Viewscore, forty-three."

Xu Dehai said nothing.

"At nine twenty, the venue received two complaints. One said the bird in the image didn't form, one said lower left of frame had illegal structure, affecting city appearance." Qi Hai said, "Second one means those two stakes at your house and that shed roof."

"I didn't tear them down."

"I know." Qi Hai said, "Agreement says don't tear down, can't protect the score."

He pushed the paper across.

"But Master Xu," Qi Hai said, "What I can give you now is another job."

Xu Dehai looked at the paper.

"The city museum building a permanent exhibit, called Nanyu's Crafts. Missing a Crane Herding section. They're doing oral history, need audio, need video, need your flag, your whistle, those two old logs of yours." Qi Hai said, "Light work, three days a week. Long-term contract, social insurance. Manuscript fee I'll apply for you."

"Stay." Qi Hai said, "Stay in our cabinet."

Xu Dehai did not reach out.

"Master Xu?"

"My daughter starts work tomorrow." He said.

He walked out of the command center at ten. Sun was out, nothing on the sea.

---

November 10, community hospital.

Father's lungs needed another scan, doctor said scan first then set admission date. Xu Dehai got number 142 at the window at six a.m.

A row of plastic chairs at the end of the second floor corridor. He settled his father, stood beside him.

"One forty-two." Xu Changgeng said.

"Mm."

"You released them that day?"

"Released."

"Match?"

"No."

Xu Changgeng coughed twice, pressed cloth to mouth.

"The image."

"Image there." Xu Dehai said, "Birds didn't enter."

Old man nodded, closed eyes.

Around eleven Xu Yi arrived. She'd taken half day off, brought a lunch box, two dishes one rice.

"You've lost weight." She said.

"No."

"Yes." She opened the box, pushed it over, "Eat."

Xu Dehai ate half.

"When you paying back the 120,000." Xu Yi asked.

"Old House demolition November 30. Paperwork before then, can sell for half."

"Live where if sold."

"Rent." Xu Dehai said, "Hospital needs deposit, self-pay portion 14,000, I'll front it first."

"You front what." Xu Yi pulled a paper from her bag, "My side advanced three months' pay, park guide position, 3,500 a month. 10,500 total."

"Short 3,500."

"Down payment I'm not touching yet." Xu Yi said, "The short 3,500 I'll figure out. I'll lend you."

"I don't want you lending."

"Fine." Xu Yi put the paper back, "Then count it as me lending you."

Xu Dehai set down chopsticks.

"Wedding postponed." Xu Yi said, "Date can be reset."

She folded the paper, tucked it back in her bag. "Dad, today the director called 'standing by' over the broadcast."

"I heard."

"When he said it, we all stood there." Xu Yi said, "Thirty-two people, two rows, no one allowed to move."

Kids running in the corridor, soles slapping tiles. The number screen overhead, jumping every so often.

Wei Xiaoqing came up the stairwell, a logbook in hand. She walked to the chairs, glanced at Xu Changgeng first.

"Array stopped, six-ten-zero-four." She turned a page, "Unit 03 broke formation, bearing north by east twelve degrees. On record."

She turned the logbook toward Xu Dehai, for him to sign.

"Unit 03 didn't return to the tidal flat."

"Its wing's bad."

"It flies every year." Wei Xiaoqing said.

She tucked the logbook under her arm, stood a moment, then said: "There's a monitoring station to the north, all northbound migratory flocks pass there. Our flock arrives in three days."

"Three days and it's past?"

"Three days and if it passes, it's recorded." Wei Xiaoqing said, "Recorded, still exists."

She left.

Xu Yi watched her back.

"Dad, who's she."

"Conservation center."

"She talked about your bird."

"Mm."

Twelve twenty, the screen number jumped: 141.

Xu Dehai stood up. The flag under his arm brushed the doorframe, slid to the floor, caught a speck of dust. He picked it up, wiped the dust with his palm.

A crane embroidered on the flag. Two wings, one high one low, thread faded to gray.

"You brought this flag to the hospital?" Xu Yi asked.

"My mother embroidered it." Xu Dehai said, "She only knew how to embroider cranes."

Screen jumped again: 142.

Room 3 door open, daughter standing in the doorway.

"Dad," she said, "Come in."
