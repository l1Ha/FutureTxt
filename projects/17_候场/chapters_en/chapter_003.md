# Chapter 3: Rehearsal

At dawn on October 24, the third rehearsal.

Xu Dehai listened to the model.

The model's window was 5:40, ten minutes earlier than his own judgment. He stood on the tidal flat, the tide just receded to his ankles, two hundred–odd cranes crowded in the shallows beyond. He waited three minutes, didn't move.

"5:40." Zhou Bai's voice in the earpiece. "Wind force three, water level just right."

"I'll wait."

"Master Xu, wait any longer and you'll miss the tidal mouth."

"I'll wait."

He waited until 5:42, then raised the flag. First form, rise. One long whistle blast, fall.

When the crane flock lifted, the sky hadn't fully brightened. They were chaotic at first, then layered upward, tier by tier. The array's third segment kicked in, the alarm call pressing in from the east. The model had nailed the wind: a northeasterly cutting across the flock's right flank, shoving them precisely toward the desired position.

Xu Dehai watched the formation grow. Wings spreading, tail closing, like a net.

One, two, three—he lost count.

Then he saw an extra bird on the left.

Unit 03 was there. Left rear, one wing higher than the other, its whole body listing right. The formation dragged left behind it, worsening by the second, like a rope bent under pressure.

Match rate forty-three percent.

"Unit 03 is in the sequence," Zhou Bai said in the earpiece.

"It's in."

"The model's recommendation hasn't changed. Remove it from the guiding sequence. Remove it, forty-three climbs above eighty."

"I know."

"So do it."

"Not doing it."

In the morning playback, that glowing kunpeng banked with a crooked wing. Qi Hai had it enlarged, projected onto the wall.

"Forty-three." He said. "Look at this wing."

Xu Dehai stood at the very back. He knew this wasn't the model's fault: the model hadn't lied. Unit 03 flew slow, couldn't keep up, kept drifting left—every metric was a negative.

Without Unit 03, the formation was true.

---

On the morning of October 25, he took his father to the community hospital.

Follow-up: pulmonary function, quarterly, last time's appointment never came through. Xu Dehai went at six a.m. to register. The window asked if he had priority grounds; he said no. The window handed him a slip printed with a number.

142.

"One hundred forty-two." Xu Dehai said. "Afternoon and it'll still be over a hundred."

"Senior priority window is on the second floor." The window said.

"He doesn't do stairs."

"Then you wait."

He found his father a chair at the far end of the second-floor corridor. His father sat, folding a handkerchief into a square and pressing it to his mouth; when he pulled it away, a few more black specks had appeared.

"Over a hundred." Xu Changgeng said.

"Mm."

"You're thinking about that bird."

"Mm."

"About signing that form—you talked to the Cityscape people?"

"No."

"Why not."

"Talking equals signing."

Xu Changgeng coughed twice, tucked the handkerchief into his pocket.

"Dehai, let me ask you one thing. If you don't sign, come the thirtieth you're on the flat and the birds don't match, the image is just an image. Your forty years amount to nothing."

"I never did anything else."

"I know you never did anything else." The old man said. "That's why you're more afraid."

Leaving the hospital, Xu Dehai went to the Cityscape Platform's service hall. He queued at the window, twenty people ahead.

"Can registration jump the queue by Viewscore?"

"Registration doesn't look at score." The window said. "If you're urgent, there's an expedited channel. Expedited requires tier B or above."

"What affects it?"

"Nothing does."

He stood there, didn't move.

"I'm sixty-one."

"Then you can't expedite." The window said. "Next."

Later in the corridor he ran into Qi Hai.

"The internal regs do have that clause." Qi Hai said. "Tier B and above can apply for expedited. If your father signs that form tomorrow, his number jumps to top three by three p.m."

"That's a transaction."

"That's the rules." Qi Hai said. "You don't sign, no one jumps, no one needs to. You wait, your turn comes when it comes."

"You're hoping I sign."

"I'm hoping everything runs by process." Qi Hai said. "You signing is process, you not signing is process. The difference is whether the result looks good."

---

At dawn on October 26, the fourth rehearsal. Window failed.

The model still gave 5:50, ten minutes before the tidal mouth. This time Xu Dehai didn't wait; he followed the model.

4:50, clear the flat. 5:40, raise flag, rise.

5:50, a string of firecrackers went off at the flat's eastern end.

Groundbreaking ceremony for the viewing promenade, six hundred meters off at the hoarding line, a dozen people clapping. The sound carried far across the flat.

The crane flock blew.

They sprang from the water, scattered north, cleared the toss-wood, fled toward the inner river. By the time they returned, the window was gone.

Xu Dehai stood in the mud, flag still raised. He lowered it slowly.

"Groundbreaking wasn't on the checklist," Zhou Bai said in the earpiece.

"Any people on the checklist?"

"Only equipment, tides, wind speed, water level."

Wei Xiaoqing arrived in the morning. She glanced at the flat, then at the hoarding where incense still smoked.

"It didn't miscalculate." She said. "It didn't calculate you."

"It can't calculate people." Xu Dehai said.

"It can't calculate people, but it won't skip a calculation just because it can't." Wei Xiaoqing said. "Master Xu, equipment is dead, people are alive. You try to fit the dead to the living, the part that doesn't fit—it treats as zero."

"Zero is zero."

"Zero is zero." Wei Xiaoqing flipping her logbook. "No 'human interference' item on the checklist. In the parameter table, the human interference coefficient defaults to zero. Zero isn't 'didn't happen,' it's 'excluded from calculation.'"

---

Evening of October 27, Xu Yi came home.

She slammed a folder onto the table—inside, the Summit volunteer service certificate, a pale blue seal under the title. She wore that gray-blue vest, the 027 on the back worn white.

"Next month the scenic area converts to formal staff." She said. "This adds points."

"Good."

"I stood three nights at that intersection on Seaside Avenue, a hundred smiles an hour." She hung her jacket behind the door. "They have quotas, smiles per minute, written on little cards."

"Stop standing."

"I'm standing." Xu Yi said. "I have to get into the scenic area. You don't understand."

Xu Dehai was chopping scallions. He set the knife down.

"That image of yours." Xu Yi said.

"What."

"Your image—who've you shown it."

"Three hundred twenty channels."

"Channels are people?"

"They are."

"Then who've you shown it."

"People who watch."

Xu Yi sat, elbows on the table.

"Domestic and foreign?"

"Both."

"Do they know the bird is called Unit 03?"

Xu Dehai didn't speak.

"They see 'Nanyu can array two hundred birds into a kunpeng,' right." Xu Yi said. "That line goes up with the seawall proposal, doesn't it. Dad, I stand at the intersection smiling all day, smile till my jaw aches. They take this to negotiate the seawall."

"Stop standing."

"I'm standing." She met his eyes. "I'm standing tomorrow too."

The range hood roared in the kitchen. Xu Dehai finished the scallions, lined them segment by segment on the cutting board.

"Those two hundred birds," Xu Yi said. "Do they know what shape they're flying into tomorrow?"

He didn't answer.

"When I stand at the intersection smiling, no one tells me what shape to smile into either. They give a quota, smiles per minute, written on the card."

Xu Yi stood, hugging the folder.

"You do your birds." She said. "I'll do my quota."

---

At dawn on October 28, the fifth rehearsal.

This one hit seventy-four. Missing a wingtip.

"Seventy-four." Qi Hai froze playback on that frame. "This frame goes in the promo reel. Darken, add a line of English, make it page one of the city card."

On screen, that glowing bird's wing filled with light, right wingtip missing a small notch, the gap leaving a void on the sea surface.

"Looks good." Cui Shouzhuo said. He stood in the back row, hands in jacket pockets.

Xu Dehai didn't speak.

"Seventy-four beats zero." Qi Hai said. "Master Xu, the model gave two numbers: seventy-nine and ninety-six. You're still five off seventy-nine."

"I know."

"How's the consideration going."

Xu Dehai slipped a hand into his padded jacket, fingers finding that paper folded in quarters. The paper had softened against body heat.

"Unit 03 didn't come up today." Wei Xiaoqing beside him said. "It stood on the eastern flat all morning, didn't move a step. Old flight feathers from molt haven't fully dropped, it won't fly."

"It doesn't fly every year at this time."

"Every year at this time someone worries for it." Wei Xiaoqing said.

After the debrief, Xu Dehai stayed alone on the flat. Sun came out, mud began to dry, cracking into fine lines. He walked east, found Unit 03 standing there, one leg tucked, neck sunk into shoulders.

Its left wing couldn't lift high, right wing lifted higher; standing long, the body listed right.

"You know too." Xu Dehai crouched. "You know tomorrow's different."

Unit 03 didn't move.

---

At dawn on October 29, the final rehearsal.

The model's window: 5:38. Zhou Bai said tide level eight centimeters lower than yesterday, flock would lift late, estimated seventy-eight.

Xu Dehai stood at the shallows' edge waiting, didn't move until 5:40.

First form, rise. One long whistle blast, fall.

When the crane flock lifted, Unit 03 wasn't in it.

It was on the eastern flat, hadn't moved all night, molt unfinished. Zhou Bai said the model had predicted: molt-delay retention probability twelve percent, this batch always had a few that missed the window—Unit 03 happened to be one.

The flock drew its own arc. Wings spreading, tail closing, like a net.

One, two, three—he lost count.

Match rate eighty-one percent.

Playback was in the morning. On the command center wall, a glowing kunpeng hung over a gray-white sea, wings filled with light—that white the flock flashed in backlight.

"Eighty-one." Qi Hai said. "Three cities have better data."

Xu Dehai stood at the very back. He watched that bird, wings filled with light, lower right corner missing a small notch.

"What about Unit 03?" He asked Zhou Bai.

"Lifted four minutes late." Zhou Bai said. "It settled on the lower flat, didn't enter frame."

"It wasn't driven off."

"It was late on its own." Zhou Bai said. "That's what the model calculated."

That afternoon, Xu Dehai watched the playback three times. He saw his flag-raising posture, saw the flock close, saw the true formation. True.

When Unit 03 was in, the formation always drifted left a little, crooked a little, slow a little. His father spent a lifetime trying to straighten that drift, never succeeded. The model used four minutes to remove Unit 03 from the sequence, and the formation trued.

Trued, and correct.

Before adjourning, Qi Hai held him back.

"Master Xu, tomorrow's the live day." He said. "Today eighty-one, tomorrow run this, sign or not sign that form, the gap is fifteen points. Fifteen points, three hundred twenty channels, ninety seconds."

"Fifteen points short, one less bird."

"Fifteen points short, one more void in the frame." Qi Hai said. "You do the math."

The execution order was still in his padded jacket. Through the fabric, four folded corners dug into his ribs.

---

Evening of the 29th, his father pushed the window open.

Sea wind billowed the curtain onto the table. Xu Dehai went to close it, hand pausing on the frame.

"Wake me tomorrow morning." Xu Changgeng said.

"Sleep."

"I'm not sleeping." The old man said. "I'm watching the sea."

Xu Yi called after nine. Tomorrow she'd volunteer at the venue, assigned to the east corridor of the main hall. Her voice was light on the phone, said the volunteers there had uniform vests, prettier than the intersection ones.

"Dad," she said. "You're one paper away."

"What paper."

"The signing paper." Xu Yi said. "Sign it and it's done. You don't sign, image fails, you still owe 120,000, image still fails."

"You understand this."

"I don't." She said. "I only know when I don't sign, no one gives me expedited."

Call ended.

10:20, Wei Xiaoqing's messages came in, three.

One: tomorrow wind shifts north to east, Sonic Array fan offset, Match Rate projected drop to seventy-four.

Two: contingency: array output from 115 dB to 130 dB, sustained no more than four minutes, can pull Match Rate back to ninety. Cost: Unit 03 and seven behind it startled beyond safety radius, furthest may hit light rigs.

Three: I don't sign the safety redline. Decision rests with Release Commander.

Last message from Qi Hai, one sentence: Execution order valid until midnight tonight.

Xu Dehai sat on the stone block in the courtyard.

That quarter-folded paper in his padded jacket dug at his ribs. He didn't pull it out.

Wind came from the north. He went upstairs, cracked the window. The old flag on the sill snapped, a dense rattle. A crane on the flag face, embroidery faded to gray.

Tomorrow wind comes from the east. The flag will flip.
