# Chapter 2: Removal of Unit 03

At 3:40 a.m. on October 20, Baisha Spit.

Clearing the beach was old routine. The night before release, the tidal flats could have no people, no dogs, nothing that flew. Cranes recognized territory, and they recognized silence; their takeoff paths were fixed, and anything extra on the path meant they would detour.

When Xu Dehai left through the entrance, the water was invisible in the dark. The dogs belonged to Master Tao—two of them, leashed to the iron-sheet shed door the previous night. Old Tao had stuffed a bundle of dry grass into the door crack, saying it would keep them inside.

The first thing to drive off was the drones.

Ah Hai was flying his aircraft at the south end of Paomu. Twenty-four, did aerial photography for the scenic area, took private jobs at night. He saw Xu Dehai approaching and hid the remote behind his back.

"Uncle Xu, I'm just filming the sunset."

"Filming a sunset doesn't require flying into the flats."

"This stretch looks good."

"Tonight it doesn't."

Ah Hai packed up the drone. As he did, he muttered: Cityscape Platform had issued a notice, Baisha Spit no-fly zone from the 20th to the 30th, eight hundred yuan fine, equipment seized. He hadn't planned to fly far anyway—all the private gigs he'd lined up for those days had fallen through.

"Uncle Xu, on the 30th, can you really make the birds form a pattern?"

"Not me." Xu Dehai said. "They arrange themselves."

"Is that different?"

Xu Dehai didn't answer. He walked onto the flats, mud sucking at his ankles, cold, slick.

4:20 a.m. The speakers began to work.

First group at the east end of Paomu. The sound was low, nearly inaudible to human ears—only a pressure in the chest, like the air pressure before a downpour. The cranes in the shallows heard it—dozens, hundreds, rising, shaking wings, converging on a single spot.

Xu Dehai raised his flag. Flag signal one: rise.

The flag turned two circles in the air. He blew a long whistle. Fall.

The flock scattered.

Second group took over. That sound was a Simulated Alarm Call—sharp, short, one after another, the call birds make only when sensing danger. The cranes dared not go that way, yet would not come this way either, squeezed between the two walls of sound, their formation pulling itself into an arc.

Halfway through, the outermost birds broke. One, startled, couldn't check its wings in time and slammed into a light-stand leg; another plunged diagonally into the iron frame, crashing into the shallows. Newly fledged birds didn't fly like this.

Formation Match failed.

---

After dawn, a coordination meeting in the command center.

Zhou Bai projected the results on the wall: "First Rehearsal, Match Rate zero. System assessment: window suitable, flock condition poor. Recommend second rehearsal two days earlier, allow more trial iterations."

"Flock condition poor." He said. "Molting season. Flight feathers not fully grown, formation was scattered to begin with."

"The model doesn't know molting." Zhou Bai said. "The model knows wind speed, water level, time."

"Does the model know headcount?"

Zhou Bai glanced at him, didn't respond.

A man stood in the doorway. Dark gray soft-shell jacket, sleeves rolled to forearms, an old scar at the left thumb-web, pale as weathered rope.

Xu Dehai recognized that scar. From a drive-line rope.

"Safety Redline first." Wei Xiaoqing said. "Hard push past fifteen minutes, flock dispersal rate spikes. Today we used nine minutes, two strikes. One left wing contusion, one broken tail feathers. In this season, a strike means two weeks grounded."

"Two weeks later?" Qi Hai asked.

"Two weeks later feathers are full, it can fly."

"Then it works."

"That doesn't work." She said. "If that one is contused on the formal day, it falls out of formation during the livestream, the picture has a hole. The whole city watches the hole."

"The Redline you signed." Xu Dehai said.

"I signed." Wei Xiaoqing said. "Today's nine minutes, on my name."

"Rehearsal failure is fine." Qi Hai said. "Formal day failure, the whole city watches ninety seconds. Engineer Wei, what do I take to the outside to explain."

"Then there shouldn't have been a seventh."

Wei Xiaoqing turned to look at him. "The seventh was your call."

"Six is the same. The 30th has only one chance, you bring seven to test."

"Trial and error needs margin." Qi Hai said.

"Birds get no margin."

Meeting dismissed, Wei Xiaoqing waited in the corridor.

"Master Xu." She pulled a photocopy from her inner pocket, folded many times, edges frayed. Top line printed: Temporary Crane Displacement Protocol (2124). Three signature blocks below, one with her name.

"October 2024, I was monitoring at the Conservation Center. That batch of cranes refused to leave the west flats. We ran a displacement, used electrified hoses. That night one hit the grid. My signature."

Xu Dehai looked down at the paper. His fingertip brushed the edge, rough fibers pricking.

He remembered another bird. Twenty years ago, fresh on these flats, a kid from the next village poached with nets and stones. When Xu Dehai arrived, the crane lay in a ditch, neck limp.

"Incident happened, discipline on my name. A year later, discipline revoked under 'process revision,' archives left with one line."

"What happened to the crane."

"Died." Wei Xiaoqing said. "Neck snapped on impact. When I reached it, it was still convulsing."

Xu Dehai didn't speak. He knew how to lift a flock off the flats, and he knew what happened after they landed.

A stiff paper corner pressed against his chest inside his cotton jacket. Five years ago, the Technical Consultant letter of intent photocopy, corners worn fuzzy. Advance 120,000, he took it, returned half, the other half never mentioned.

"I'm telling you this for two things." Wei Xiaoqing folded the paper back. "One, I won't sign the Removal Order. Two, on formal release day I won't carry it for you. You have another way, come to me, I'll give you data; you don't, follow process, the signature I put on process, I'll explain."

"Why tell me."

"Because you'll make the mistake I made." She said. "You'll think you can carry it."

---

Noon, Zhou Bai briefing him by the Paomu edge.

"Eighteen groups all online, three-segment handoff clean." Zhou Bai tapped the box at his waist. "Low-frequency penetration verified, high-frequency directional calibrated. Master switch here, counter-clockwise."

"Why do the birds listen."

"Simulated Alarm Call. The call birds make only when sensing danger, sharp, short, one after another. They dare not go that way, won't come this way, squeezed between two sound walls, formation pulls itself into an arc."

"Redline locked at fifteen minutes," Xu Dehai said. "Afraid the machine breaks?"

"Machine doesn't fear heat, bird ears can't take it." Zhou Bai said. "Past 130 dB, eardrums bleed, next day they can't gain altitude. Redline locks the bird, not the machine."

"Within fifteen minutes?"

"Within is scare. Scared enough they veer. Scared too much they strike."

"Who controls."

"Master control here." Zhou Bai tapped the waist box. "Contingency we draft, field dispatch authority with Release Commander—that's you. We calculate, you execute."

"I can shut it off."

"Can. Master breaker in the control box, one knife switch, counter-clockwise."

"The Light Array?"

"Light Array not ours." Zhou Bai said. "Belongs to Cityscape Platform, we only handle flight. Two hundred forty units, each a dense lamp row, 170,000 points. Airborne 4:50 a.m., at 6:10 that sky-light they take shape, any later invisible. Normally dark, simulation screen shows trajectory plots. Outline is dead, inside must grow something on its own."

"Doesn't grow?"

"Doesn't grow, it's just a picture." Zhou Bai said.

---

Qi Hai waited on the viewing platform on the scenic belt.

No railings yet, only a row of temporary cones. Wind combed white lines across the sea.

"Master Xu, sit."

Xu Dehai didn't sit.

"Three hundred twenty channels, ninety-second window." Qi Hai said. "This morning twenty or thirty media outlets asked about this segment. Next four days, forty-seven more."

"Then what."

"Then footage goes into the dossier. Next round investment rating memorandum has a column, Urban Operational Predictability. That column isn't for people, it's for the scoring algorithm. Not in the scored items, in the remarks."

"Remarks count too."

"Remarks count." Qi Hai said. "This year Nanyu fights for three hundred million in levee matching funds, and a foreign-funded hospital surgical center. The readers flip to this column, they look for 'unpredictable'—those four characters."

"What's that got to do with birds."

"Nothing to do with birds, everything to do with the picture." Qi Hai said. "Picture has two hundred-plus birds forming a Kunpeng, three hundred twenty channels broadcasting ninety seconds. They won't remember Unit 03, they'll remember 'Nanyu can sort two hundred-plus birds into that shape.' That sentence rides up the chain with the levee."

"Reverse?"

"Reverse, they remember 'Nanyu can't even line up a flock.' Then they ask: is the levee handled the same way."

Xu Dehai watched the sea.

"Director Qi," he said. "Signed, Unit 03 hits the light stand, neck snaps. Left wing can't lift, right side folds first. Folded, it stays on the flats, next year that stretch gets walled off."

"Unsigned, whole city watches a scattered flock. You pick one, see if you sleep tonight."

"I want neither." Qi Hai said. "I want ninety-six."

"Ninety-six isn't a bird."

"You want me to sign."

"I want you to sign the best one." Qi Hai said.

He rotated the pen half a turn, no second turn.

"The best one takes a sheet of paper. Paper at your hand this afternoon."

---

October 22, pre-dawn, second Rehearsal.

Not Xu Dehai who came.

Cui Shouzhuo wore a dark blue jacket, new badge on chest: Release Consultant. Sixty-three, Xu Dehai called him senior fellow disciple; young, they'd walked side by side on these same flats.

He brought a new protocol: Light Array rises first, sketches outline; Sonic Array pushes flock segment by segment into outline per model sequence; final micro-adjust for lock.

Match Rate thirty-four percent.

"Thirty-four."

"Thirty-four." Cui Shouzhuo set down his thermos. Right index finger, second joint, trembled involuntarily, lid clacked against rim, crisp. He showed nothing, just gripped the cup tighter. "Not one injured."

"Picture doesn't form."

"Picture doesn't form." The old man admitted. "Birds fine."

"Back in our day—"

"Back in our day what equipment?" Cui Shouzhuo cut in fast. "One boat, one net, one flag. Someone bangs the hull, birds rise. We released thirty years, got three full formations."

"Three times."

"Three times."

"The 30th has only once."

Cui Shouzhuo lifted the cup again. His right index second joint trembling slightly, visible to himself.

"Your hand shakes." Xu Dehai said.

Old man gripped the cup harder. "Started two years ago."

"Two years ago the livestream incident wasn't hand shake." Xu Dehai continued. "Report said equipment failure. Insiders all say you pulled three all-nighters that week, third day hands shook too bad to catch the flag."

Cui Shouzhuo removed glasses, wiped with his hem.

"Xu Dehai, I'm not fighting you on this." He said. "You want to guard your craft, I won't stop you. But you admit, your way matches three times a year, people break. My way hits thirty-four, but it hits thirty-four every day, people still standing."

"People standing, picture fails."

"Picture fails, birds alive." Old man said. "You hand it over, I sign."

---

Evening, command center delivered a kraft envelope, cover printed four characters: Release Command, Internal.

One sheet inside.

《Removal Guidance Sequence Execution Order (No. 03)》

Per Flock Behavior Model prediction, Unit 03 crane causes Match Rate loss in guidance sequence. Recommend removal from guidance sequence prior to formal release. Execution method: Directional Speaker solo displacement, duration not exceeding four minutes. Post-execution flock convergence projected to rise to ninety-six percent.

Approval column: Release Commander.

Below, a line of small print: If not executed, proceed per original protocol, model predicts Match Rate seventy-nine percent.

"Refuse to sign, does it work." Xu Dehai asked Qi Hai.

"Works." Qi Hai said. "Seventy-nine is also a number. Ninety seconds in, seventy-nine means roughly one-fifth of the picture scattered. You and I can't say what those ninety seconds become."

"You want me to sign."

Qi Hai didn't answer. He laid the pen across the kraft envelope, pushed it to the edge. Pen stopped, didn't fall.

"Execution Order expires midnight tonight." Qi Hai said. "Past midnight, system auto-runs original protocol. I'm not keeping anyone."

Xu Dehai folded the order, four folds, shoved it into his cotton jacket.

Leaving, he glanced up at the wall clock. 22:40.

Until 6:10 a.m. on the 30th, seven days and seven and a half hours remained.
