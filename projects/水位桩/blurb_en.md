In 2126, Huaiyin County has been zoned into the Huai River’s "Flexible Dispatch Area," where downstream megacities trade water-rights futures to buy upstream flood capacity, compensating farmers by the *mu-per-event*. For Zhou Guangtian, a fifty-six-year-old weld inspector whose wrists bear the white-finger tremor of decades in the shipyard, the calculus is brutal. Option A offers a city apartment, a lump sum, and land transfer—the security his wife has calculated down to the last yuan for their son’s down payment. Option B means staying behind the dike: keeping the land, claiming flood payouts, and drawing a guardian’s allowance with an advance he can pledge. The deciding factor isn't a spreadsheet; it’s the water-gauge pile on the embankment, notched by his father for the floods of ’91, ’03, and ’16. Before a single dispatch order drops, Zhou signs Option B in his own hand, wagering his family’s future on the river’s return. Three months later, the sirens sound. He stands at his sluice gate, wrench gripped tight, waiting to carve the next notch into history.
