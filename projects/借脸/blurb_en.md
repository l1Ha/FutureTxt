In a world where facial data is legislated as a licensable asset, the platform CloudVisage harvests biometric identities of workers under the guise of image licensing. Forty‑six‑year‑old He Li loses her job to an AI model and signs an exclusive contract paying 3,200 yuan a month—just enough for her mother’s nursing‑home bills in a county town. One night she discovers a livestream host wearing her face, down to the scar beneath her brow, while a synthetic voice hawks a supplement; the only notice is a gray footnote reading “AI‑generated.” Reporting the breach would void her contract, letting the platform claim “reputational damages” and freeze her earnings; a supplemental license would give her only a 30 % cut. The only way to prove the face isn’t hers to sell is to erase her biometric record at the Identity Verification Center—making her unable to unlock bank cards, access health insurance, enter her apartment, or pay her mother’s care fees. At 3 a.m. in a corridor, she presses the “Revoke” button on the holographic terminal.
