# Chapter 1: Millimeters on Either Side of the Rainline

Mist pressed the plateau into a damp fingerprint, wrapping Hydrological Station 112 on the eastern edge of the Tibetan Plateau. Wind funneled through the mountain pass, carrying the ice-cold scent of pine needles, a faint susurrus in the nasal passages; boot soles bit into gravel with a fine *hiss-hiss*; fingertips touched the probe's micro-vibration, like a slender nerve pulsing in the palm. Lin Tan inhaled air tasting of iron rust. The old injury on the medial side of her right knee ached dully. She expertly tore a transdermal analgesic patch from her pocket, applied it to the knee, fingertips brushing the worn metal clasp on her cuff — a trace left by that arbitration ten years ago. The patch's adhesive face had hardened in the low temperature; the moment it adhered, a cool sting pierced through, then melted open under body heat, like a rusted key sliding back into a long-familiar lock.

She drew the W-12 Portable Laser Disdrometer from her pack. The screen lit with pale blue light, calibration countdown three seconds. Probe aimed at the baseline points on either side of the Rainline, the laser beam cut an invisible line through the mist, as if scoring an ultra-fine knife mark in the air. Readings jumped, locked at **112.3 mm ±0.12**. Lin Tan murmured: "Reading 112.3 mm, error 0.12, meets W2." The Biometric Stylus in the screen's lower right corner blinked, heart-rate sync confirmed, fingerprint and heartbeat dual-authentication passed, a green checkmark flashing once on the retinal projection. The instrument's internal fan gave a low hum, like a sleeping behemoth breathing; each rotation pushed faint heat into the surrounding chill. The fan's vibration traveled through the metal housing to her palm; fingertips felt a weak current sensation, like a heartbeat echoing inside metal.

The Rainline lay here: north side, soil loose, gravel mixed with fine white quartz powder, fingers pinching it felt like holding crushed glass, fine gray-white dust seeping between digits, drifting on the wind; south side, a single step away, red earth wet and sticky, moss scent rushing the face, fingertips sinking into mud, drawing out a thread of coolness, slurry pulling fine long strands between fingers, like ancient glue. Lin Tan took three samples from each side, data written to local cache, remarks column typed: "Traceability required." Fingertips striking the virtual keyboard felt crisp and decisive, each keystroke accompanied by a faint blue pulse on the screen, as if timestamping every millimeter of record. Sample tube caps twisting tight emitted a soft *click*, metal threads biting extra tight in the cold, a tingling numbness rising in the fingertips.

The instrument's micro-vibration traveled up the arm to the shoulder; Lin Tan's right knee swelled with soreness each time she crossed the Rainline. She habitually drew the Signing Gesture — "∟" — on the screen before closing a case, knuckles emitting a faint *click*, her verbal tic following: "Millimeters don't lie." Data didn't speak; she only read numbers, reported errors, cited clauses. The knee pain was a fine needle; each flexion-extension reminded her: measurement wasn't abstract digits, but bone and tendon compromising under physical law. Between breaths, mist coiled overhead, cold air seeping through the collar, skin rising in fine gooseflesh.

She stowed the disdrometer back in her pack, fingertips touching the metal clasp on her cuff; the cold touch recalled that arbitration ten years ago: her friend's district frozen out of quota for three years because she had privately adjusted upward by 0.2 mm; to this day no one knew. The right knee's pain reminded her: every crossing of the Rainline exacted payment for that decision. The pack's nylon strap cut a shallow groove into her shoulder, like a silent contract, reminding her that every measurement repaid data's debt with flesh. Wind blew distant pine surf into fragments, mixed with a low metallic fatigue groan, as if the entire mountain range moaned softly for that single deviation.

An encrypted comm popped up on the screen, sender tagged "Lao Chen." Content brief, bearing the distinctive directive tone of an orbital scavenger: "Cold Slot window 02:00-06:00 available, bring the magtape." Lin Tan's pupils contracted; fingertip drew the "∟" on the screen, then froze the gesture mid-air, knuckles clicking once more — this time she did not confirm immediately, but let the cursor hover between "✓" and "∟." Mist still swallowed near and far contours; only the instrument fan's low drone and the deep dull ache in her knee reminded her: the next step, still decided by millimeters.

Wind drove sea salt and ozone taste into the nasal cavity; the instrument fan's low drone trembled fine shivers on the eardrum. Lin Tan cinched the pack straps tighter; nylon bit a deeper white groove at the clavicle; fingertip pads touched the screen's cool glass surface, like touching a layer of thin ice. She didn't tap confirm; instead raised her wrist, letting the Quota Card's blue watermark flicker in the mist — annual quota 1200 mm, remaining 312 mm, digits projecting faint blue speckles on the retina, pulsing bright-dim with each breath. Speckles refracted tiny rainbows in the pupil, as if every millimeter quietly calculated the balance of survival.

"Lao Chen, received." She spoke low into the comm, voice shredded by wind, "Cold Slot window 02:00-06:00, magtape in pack side pocket. Send coordinates." A rasp of metal friction laughter returned, laced with the orbital scavenger's characteristic current shriek: "Don't let the knee slow you down, Lin Tan. Numbers won't lie for me, and the track underfoot won't wait for anyone." The instant the comm cut, a string of encrypted coordinates sprang on the screen, a red dot tracing a long thin arc across the mist curtain, pointing at Maintenance Shaft Seven of the abandoned maglev hub. The red dot left an afterimage on the retina, like an unwithdrawn thorn, reminding her that every step ahead was precisely measured.

The right knee's dull ache intensified with each deep breath, as if a fine nail were driven under the patella; each flexion-extension reminded her: measurement wasn't abstract digits, but bone and tendon compromising under physical law. She shoved the disdrometer deep into the pack, fingertips contacting the magtape's metal shell; icy touch climbed fingertips up the arm, straight to the scapula. Wind blew the holo-billboard's light spots more chaotic; blue light assembled fragmented characters "Revisit 2035" in the gray-white sky, as if mocking everyone still haggling over millimeters. The billboard's projection flickered in the mist; projected halos refracted into broken geometry in her pupils.

Lin Tan regulated her breath; the knee gave a muffled pop, like old machinery meshing, gears biting. She took the first step; boot sole landed on north-side loose quartz powder, gravel *hiss-hiss* under the sole, then crossed that invisible Rainline — south-side wet sticky red earth instantly wrapped the ankle, slurry pulling fine long strands, tangling the trouser cuff. Every step repaid data's debt with flesh; every flexion-extension paid for that privately corrected 0.2 mm ten years ago. Mud's fishy stench mixed with quartz powder's dry breath entering the nasal cavity, forming a compound scent belonging only to this Rainline.

Distant maglev's low drone rolled over again; current sound mixed with sea salt taste drilling the eardrum. Lin Tan switched off the Quota Card projection on her wrist; the blue watermark vanished into the mist, leaving only the fingertip suspended in micro-light between "✓" and "∟", like a star that might extinguish at any moment. She glanced down at her knee; the deep dull ache still reminded: the next step, still decided by millimeters.
