# Chapter 7: Wind From the Plateau Rift

Wind poured through the fissure of the plateau into her nostrils—first a cold, iron‑rust breath, then a damp, mossy stench, as if the entire mountain range were breathing softly. Fine grit whispered beneath her boots, and when her fingertips brushed the W‑12 probe a faint vibration climbed her finger bones to her wrist; the screen froze at **112.3 mm ±0.12**—the error band still within tolerance. Lin Tan pressed a pain‑relief patch onto the inner side of her right knee; the adhesive hissed faintly against sweat‑slick skin, and the deep ache in the joint sharpened with every step across the line. She glanced down at the gray‑white dust coating the probe’s housing, rubbed her fingertip across it, feeling the metal’s cool seep into her fingertips, as though the whole plateau’s respiration were concentrated in that single touch. Mist‑laden wind skimmed her ears, carrying a metallic tang of rust and ice; she inhaled involuntarily, the cold air sliding down her trachea, while her fingertips still retained the faint chill from the screen.  

The Rainline was no longer a static contour. In the cloud the algorithm swallowed ten years of measured millimeters, evaporation coefficients from the Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger, and water‑production figures from the Condensation Towers of the uncounted settlements, spitting out a “floating band” that drifted in real time with the measurements. A holographic projection unfolded overhead; the blue‑green boundary swung left and right like breathing, its width swelling from ±0.5 mm to ±2.3 mm before contracting back to ±0.8 mm. Lin Tan watched the band’s edges imprint simultaneously on the soils on either side: the left side dry gravel, the right side sticky red earth, the air scented with quartz dust and moss. She lifted her hand, fingertip tracing the projected edge, feeling the after‑image on her retina—as if she held the climate system’s uncertainty in her palm. The halo at the projection’s fringe trembled at her fingertip, as though each breath rewrote the definition of the boundary.  

Old Chen’s scavenger shift had long ended, yet the coolant’s red warning light still pulsed inside the Scavenger‑7 Pod. An unpublished orbital attitude log arrived via an encrypted channel on Lin Tan’s terminal; the log recorded an unauthorized micro‑adjustment of the Orbital Sunshade Ring, the very tweak that had nudged the floating band northward by 0.07° over the past three months. Lin Tan fed the log into the local node; the screen flashed **“Disputed Version·Pending Arbitration”**, and the Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger automatically stamped a timestamp on the distributed ledger. A low voice message crackled from the comm: “Don’t let those ledger auditors smooth this drift away, Lin Tan. The data will speak.” She didn’t reply, merely dragged the log’s hash into the verification queue, feeling each confirmation’s faint haptic click. A green progress bar crept forward like a heartbeat echoing in the silent pod.  

In the archive’s cryogenic vault the tape reader growled. Guan Qiming, magnifying lenses perched on his nose, fingertips dusted with the musty scent of old paper, uploaded the original signed footage of the 2043 V‑3.1 version onto the chain; Li Wei completed verification. Within seconds the data streams converged into a new baseline curve, and the floating band’s central line lifted slightly, like a rope gently hoisted. Guan Qiming raised his head, glasses reflecting the screen’s blue glow, and let out a rasping sigh: “Finally history has been returned to itself.” The air was thick with the mingled odor of aged paper and ozone.  

At the same moment the Insurance Consortium’s claims model detonated. Xue Lan’s holographic watermark flickered across the conference‑room walls; a crimson probability cloud swallowed the entire plateau sector, warning that adherence to the old baseline would raise the uncounted’s mortality rate by 12 % over the next decade. The platform city‑states’ Programmable Quota Card terminals refreshed simultaneously, annual quota dropping from 1200 mm to **1080 mm**; remaining balances jumped on every household display, and each resident’s water cup—roughly 0.001 quota per cup—emitted a soft “ding” at the instant of refresh. Xue Lan’s voice seeped from the projection, edged with a metallic reverberation: “This is the price we must pay, Lin Tan. Don’t let the numbers decide for us.” Lin Tan stared at the pulsing red cloud on the wall, fingertips tightening until nails bit into her palm, a sharp sting flaring; a thin film of sweat and quartz dust melded on her fingertips into a faint gray veil.  

In the night the Condensation Tower of the dry‑zone village dripped, each drop sounding like a tap on an empty can. Aya stood beneath it, clutching a handwritten Rainline coordinate version, the paper’s edge frayed where her fingers had worn it. She watched the floating band breathe overhead, lips pressing into a shallow line, then slipped a one‑way ticket for the Maglev Pilgrimage Bus into Lin Tan’s pocket; the carriage still reeked of rust and damp mildew. Aya’s fingers brushed Lin Tan’s palm, whispering, “Take this. The wind at the next stop will remember you.” Lin Tan gripped the ticket stub, the paper’s roughness intertwining with her sweaty fingertips, a nameless heaviness rising in her chest as if the whole plateau’s weight pressed onto that thin slip.  

Lin Tan withdrew the probe, tapping the confirm key at the screen’s edge. The display popped **“Confirm and Witness”**; the blue‑green frontier locked in that instant, dust scent and damp moss aroma recorded together into the Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger as the first paired synchronous samples of the new baseline. She drew a deep breath of air laced with dust and moss, cold sliding down her trachea, fingertips still cool from the screen, while a thin sheen of sweat—mixed with quartz dust and moss—coated the ridges of her fingerprints like an unset seal. The wind howled in her ears, carrying away an unspoken vow.  

The console fan droned low; coolant flowing through the pipes whispered like distant glacial melt. The conference room’s hologram still hovered, Xue Lan’s watermark rotating slowly on the wall, the red cloud’s fringe tightening under the data stream’s wash. A silver data crystal lay centered on the table, its surface refracting the baseline curve Lin Tan had just confirmed; each inflection point shimmered in the faint light. Lin Tan approached, fingertip grazing the crystal’s edge, feeling a faint static bite—as if an invisible contract left its mark on her skin. The crystal’s weak pulse synced with her heartbeat, a reminder that every confirmation is a re‑signing of the pact.  

Aya’s silhouette appeared in the doorway, still clutching the one‑way ticket, its worn corner catching a dull gold in the light. She said nothing, merely slipped the stub into Lin Tan’s coat pocket, the fabric whispering against her fingertip. Lin Tan looked down; the ticket bore the words **“Witness 2126, the Rainline will breathe from now on”**, ink bleeding into the morning mist as if the text itself were inhaling. Far off, the floating band rippled with a faint glow in the data flow, like a waking vessel ready to redraw the boundary for the next drift. Lin Tan lifted the cup on the table; the screen instantly flashed **“First cup 0.012 quota”** in red, her fingertip trembling at the rim—stay, or leave this sip for the next waiting soul? The wind at the corridor’s end surged sharply, a rising countdown tone forcing her to choose in the next second.
