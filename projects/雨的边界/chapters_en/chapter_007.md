# Chapter 7: Wind from the Plateau Cleft

Wind surged into her nostrils from the plateau’s fissure, first a cold, iron‑rust breath, then a damp, mossy tang, as if the entire range were breathing low. Gravel crunched beneath her boots with a fine “shhh” sound. When her fingertips brushed the W‑12 probe, a faint vibration climbed her finger bones to her wrist; the screen froze at **112.3 mm ±0.12**—the error band still within tolerance. Lin Tan pressed an analgesic patch onto the inner side of her right knee; the adhesive edge hissed faintly against sweat‑slick skin, and the deep, dull ache in the joint sharpened with every step across the line. She glanced down at the gray‑white dust coating the probe’s housing, brushed her fingertip across it, feeling the metal’s chill seep into her fingertip, as though the plateau’s whole respiration were concentrated in that single touch. Wind, laced with thin mist, skimmed her ear, carrying a metallic scent of rust and ice; she inhaled involuntarily, the cold air sliding down her trachea, her fingertips still tingling from the screen’s cool contact.  

The Rainline was no longer a static contour. In the cloud, the algorithm swallowed a decade of measured millimeters, the evaporation coefficients from the Carbon Ledger, and the water output of the Condensation Towers in Uncounted settlements, spitting out a “floating band” that drifted in real time with the measurements. A holographic projection unfurled overhead; the blue‑green boundary swayed left and right like a breath, its width expanding from ±0.5 mm to ±2.3 mm before contracting back to ±0.8 mm. Lin Tan watched the band’s edges imprint simultaneously on the soils on either side: dry gravel on the left, sticky red earth on the right, quartz dust and moss scent mingling in the air. She raised her hand, fingertip tracing the projection’s edge, feeling the afterimage of light on her retina—as if she held the climate system’s entire uncertainty in her palm. The halo at the projection’s fringe trembled at her fingertip, as though each breath rewrote the definition of the boundary.  

Lao Chen’s Scavenger‑7 shift had long ended, yet the cabin’s coolant still pulsed red. An un‑uploaded orbital attitude log arrived via an encrypted channel on Lin Tan’s terminal; it recorded an unauthorized micro‑adjustment of the Orbital Sunshade Ring, the very tweak that had nudged the floating band northward by 0.07° over the past three months. Lin Tan fed the log into the local node; the screen flashed a **“Disputed Version·Pending Adjudication”** tag, and the Carbon Ledger automatically timestamped the entry on the distributed chain. A low voice message from Lao Chen crackled over the comm: “Don’t let those ledger auditors smooth this offset away, Lin Tan. The data will speak.” She didn’t reply, merely dragged the log’s hash into the verification queue, feeling each confirmation’s faint haptic click on the touchpad. The green progress bar crept forward like a heartbeat echoing in the silent cabin.  

The archive’s tape reader growled in the cryogenic vault. Guan Qiming, wearing an optical magnifier, fingertips dusted with moldy paper fibers, uploaded the original 2043 V‑3.1 signing footage to the chain; Li Wei completed the verification. Within seconds the data streams converged into a new Baseline curve, and the floating band’s central line lifted, like a rope gently hoisted. Guan Qiming lifted his head, his glasses reflecting the screen’s blue glow, and let out a rasping sigh: “Finally, history returned to itself.” The air was thick with the stale scent of old paper and ozone.  

The Insurance Syndicate’s claim model erupted at the same moment. Xue Lan’s holographic watermark flickered on the conference‑room wall, a crimson probability cloud swallowing the entire plateau sector, indicating that adherence to the old Baseline would raise Uncounted mortality by 12 % over the next decade. The Platform City‑State’s Programmable Quota Card terminals refreshed simultaneously; annual allocation dropped from 1200 mm to **1080 mm**, remaining quotas pulsing on every household screen. Residents’ water cups—each roughly 0.001 quota—emitted a soft “click” at the instant of refresh. Xue Lan’s voice seeped from the projection, edged with a metallic reverberation: “This is the cost we must bear, Lin Tan. Don’t let the numbers decide for us.” Lin Tan stared at the throbbing red cloud, fingers tightening involuntarily, nails digging into her palm, a sharp sting shooting through her; a thin film of sweat and quartz dust formed on her fingertips, a gray veil.  

The Condensation Tower in the arid village dripped in the night, each drop sounding like a strike on an empty can. Aya stood beneath it, clutching a handwritten Rainline coordinate version, her fingertips fraying the paper’s edge. She watched the floating band breathe slowly overhead, a faint line pressing her lips, then slipped a single‑journey Maglev Pilgrimage Bus ticket into Lin Tan’s pocket; the carriage still reeked of rust and damp mold. Aya’s fingers brushed lightly across Lin Tan’s palm, whispering, “Take this. The wind at the next stop will remember you.” Lin Tan gripped the ticket stub, the paper’s roughness intertwining with her sweaty fingertips, a nameless heaviness rising in her chest—as if the whole plateau’s weight rested on that thin slip.  

Lin Tan retracted the probe, tapping the confirmation key at the screen’s edge. The display popped up **“Confirm and Witness”**; the blue‑green boundary locked in that instant, dust scent and damp moss aroma recorded together into the Carbon Ledger as the first paired synchronous sample of the new Baseline. She drew a deep breath of air mingled with dust and moss, cold sliding down her trachea; her fingertips still held the screen’s chill, while her palm already secreted a thin sheen of sweat, quartz dust and moss scent sticking in the ridges of her fingerprints like an undried impression. The wind howled in her ears, carrying away an unspoken oath.  

The console fans droned low; coolant flowing through the pipes sounded like the distant whisper of glacial melt. The conference‑room hologram still hovered, Xue Lan’s watermark rotating slowly on the wall, the red cloud’s edges receding under the data stream’s wash. A silver data crystal lay centered on the table, its surface refracting the Baseline curve Lin Tan had just confirmed; each inflection point glimmered in the faint light. Lin Tan approached, fingertip brushing the crystal’s edge, feeling a faint static prickle—as if an invisible contract left its mark on her skin. The crystal’s weak pulse synced with her heartbeat, a reminder that every confirmation is a re‑signing of the contract.  

Aya’s silhouette appeared in the doorway, still clutching the one‑way ticket, its worn corner catching a dull gold in the light. She said nothing, merely slipped the stub into Lin Tan’s coat pocket, fingertips grazing the fabric with a faint rustle. Lin Tan looked down; the ticket bore the words “Witness 2126, the Rainline shall breathe” fading in and out in the morning mist, ink and vapor intertwining as if alive. Far off, the floating band shimmered in the data flow, a waking vein ready to redraw the boundary for the next drift. Lin Tan lifted the cup on the table; the screen instantly flashed **“First cup 0.012 quota”** in red, her fingertip trembling on the rim—to stay, or to leave this sip for the next waiting soul? The wind at the corridor’s end surged, a rising countdown tone, forcing her to choose in the next second.
