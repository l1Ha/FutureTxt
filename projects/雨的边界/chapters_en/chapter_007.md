# Chapter 7: Wind from the Plateau Rift

Wind surged into her nostrils from the plateau’s fissure, first the dry taste of quartz dust, then a damp, cloying scent of moss, as if two unrelated fabrics had been forcibly stitched together. Gravel whispered beneath her boots with a fine “shhh,” and when her fingertips brushed the W‑12 probe a faint vibration climbed her finger bones to her wrist; the screen froze at **112.3 mm ±0.12**—the error band still within tolerance. Lin Tan pressed a pain‑relief patch onto the inner side of her right knee; the adhesive’s edge hissed faintly against sweat‑slick skin, and the dull ache deep in the joint sharpened with every step across the line. She glanced down at the gray‑white dust coating the probe’s housing, brushed it lightly with her fingertip, feeling the metal’s coolness seep into her fingertips, as though the entire plateau’s breath were concentrated in that single touch. A mist‑laden wind skimmed her ear, carrying a metallic tang of rust and ice; she inhaled involuntarily, the cold sliding down her trachea, while her fingertips still retained the faint chill from the screen.

The rain line was no longer a static contour. In the cloud the algorithm swallowed ten years of measured millimeters, evaporation coefficients from the carbon ledger, and water‑output data from the uncounted settlements’ condensation towers, spitting out a “floating band” that drifted in real time with the measurements. A holographic projection unfolded overhead; the blue‑green boundary breathed left and right, its width swelling from ±0.5 mm to ±2.3 mm before contracting back to ±0.8 mm. Lin Tan watched the band’s edges imprint simultaneously on the soils on either side: the left side dry gravel, the right side sticky red earth, the air scented with both quartz dust and moss. She lifted her hand, fingertip tracing the projected edge, feeling the after‑image of light spots on her retina, as if she held the climate system’s uncertainty in her palm. The halo at the projection’s rim trembled at her fingertip, as though each breath rewrote the definition of the boundary.

Old Chen’s scavenger shift had long ended, yet the cabin’s coolant red light still pulsed. An un‑uploaded orbital attitude log slipped through an encrypted channel onto Lin Tan’s terminal; it recorded an unauthorized micro‑adjustment of the sunshade ring, the very tweak that had nudged the floating band northward by 0.07° over the past three months. Lin Tan fed the log into the local node; the screen flashed **“Disputed Version·Pending Arbitration”**, and the carbon ledger automatically timestamped the entry on the distributed chain. A low voice crackled from the communicator—Old Chen’s recorded message: “Don’t let those ledger auditors smooth this drift away, Lin Tan. The data will speak.” She didn’t reply, only dragged the log’s hash into the verification queue, feeling each confirmation’s faint haptic click. A green progress bar crept forward like a heartbeat echoing in the silent pod.

In the archive’s cryogenic vault the tape reader growled. Guan Qiming, magnifying glass perched on his nose, fingertips dusted with mold‑scented paper fibers, uploaded the original 2043 V‑3.1 signed imagery to the chain; Li Wei completed the verification. Within seconds the data streams converged into a new baseline curve, the floating band’s centre line lifting slightly, like a rope gently hoisted. Guan lifted his head, lenses reflecting the screen’s blue glow, and let out a rasping sigh: “Finally history has been returned to itself.” The air was thick with the stale perfume of old paper and ozone.

The Insurance Consortium’s claim model erupted at the same moment. Xue Lan’s holographic watermark flickered across the conference‑room wall; a red probability cloud engulfed the entire plateau sector, warning that adherence to the old baseline would raise the uncounted’s mortality rate by 12 % over the next decade. Platform city‑state quota terminals refreshed simultaneously, annual allocation dropping from 1200 mm to **1080 mm**; remaining quotas jumped on every household display, and the water cups in residents’ hands—each cup roughly 0.001 quota—emitted a soft “click” at the instant of refresh. Xue Lan’s voice seeped from the projection, edged with a metallic reverberation: “This is the price we must bear, Lin Tan. Don’t let the numbers decide for us.” Lin Tan stared at the pulsing red cloud on the wall, fingers tightening involuntarily, nails biting into her palm, a sharp sting flaring; a thin film of sweat and quartz dust melded on her fingertips into a gray veil.

In the dry‑zone village the condensation tower dripped through the night, each drop sounding like a tap on an empty can. Aya stood beneath it, clutching a handwritten version of the rain‑line coordinates, the paper’s edge frayed to a fine burr. She watched the floating band breathe overhead, lips pressed into a shallow line, then slipped a single‑ticket maglev pilgrimage bus pass into Lin Tan’s pocket; the carriage still reeked of rust and damp mold. Aya’s fingers brushed Lin Tan’s palm, whispering, “Take this. The wind at the next stop will remember you.” Lin Tan gripped the ticket stub, the paper’s roughness intertwining with her sweaty fingertips, a nameless heaviness rising in her chest as if the whole plateau’s weight rested on that thin sheet.

Lin Tan withdrew the probe, tapping the confirmation key at the screen’s edge. The display popped **“Confirm & Witness”**; the blue‑green seam locked in that instant, the dry quartz scent and the damp moss aroma recorded together into the carbon ledger as the first paired synchronous samples of the new baseline. She drew a deep breath of dust‑and‑moss‑laden air, cold sliding down her windpipe, fingertips still cool from the screen, while a thin sheen of sweat—mixed with quartz and moss—settled in the ridges of her fingerprints, like an unfinished seal. The wind howled past her ear, carrying away a half‑spoken vow.

The console fans droned low; coolant flowing through the pipes murmured like distant glacial melt. The conference‑room hologram still hovered, Xue Lan’s watermark rotating slowly on the wall, the red cloud’s fringe receding under the data flood. A silver data crystal lay centered on the table, its surface refracting the baseline curve Lin Tan had just confirmed; every inflection point shimmered in the faint light. She approached, fingertip grazing the crystal’s edge, feeling a faint static prick—an invisible contract imprinting on skin. The crystal’s weak pulse synced with her heartbeat, a reminder that each confirmation is a re‑signing of the pact.

Aya’s silhouette appeared in the doorway, still clutching the one‑way ticket, its worn corner catching a dull gold in the light. She said nothing, merely slipped the stub into Lin Tan’s coat pocket, fingertips brushing the fabric with a soft rustle. Lin Tan looked down; the ticket bore the words **“Witness 2126, the rain line now breathes”**, the ink wavering in the morning mist as if alive. Far off, the floating band flickered with a faint glow in the data stream, a waking vein ready to redefine the border for the next drift. Lin Tan lifted the cup on the table; the screen instantly flashed **“First cup 0.012 quota”** in red, her fingertip trembling at the rim—stay, or leave this sip for the next waiting soul? The wind at the corridor’s end surged sharply, a rising countdown tone forcing her to choose in the next second.
