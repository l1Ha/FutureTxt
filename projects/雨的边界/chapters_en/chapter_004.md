Lin Tan closed her eyes, trying to separate that odor from the mossy, brine‑laden air of the rain‑line’s southern fringe ten years ago, only to find them woven together in the same olfactory texture: damp cold soil, fermenting leaf litter, the faint sweetness of dew condensing on distant vapor.  

“The cold slot doesn’t wait.” Lao Chen’s voice squeezed through the comms, laced with the whine of his prosthetic’s motors, delivered at the speed of a rehearsed script, yet its tail carried an almost imperceptible weariness: “Capture, lock, withdraw. She recalled the rain line’s two faces on the ground: the north side, dry, sand slipping through fingers, smelling of quartz dust and arid wind—each breath a mouthful of fine grit; the south side, slick with moss, the scent climbing the throat, red loam clinging to soles, every step a viscous drag. She had once slapped a transdermal analgesic patch on the line itself; now, in orbit, the two smells lingered as if she had crossed not merely latitude but two entirely different survival logics.  

A terse ground‑control instruction came over the comms, flat as an automated broadcast: “Thermal‑zone transit credentials verified; cold‑slot window extended to 06:00.” Lin Tan glanced at her Programmable Quota Card: **“Annual quota 1200 mm, remaining 312 mm.”** She logged a single sip’s allowance—**0.02 quota**—in the memo, a reminder that children in the arid villages survived on the **4 L** dripped daily from the Condensation Towers. He exhaled long, voice unusually gentle: “Data written to ledger, flagged disputed version, countdown 47:59:12.” A network‑wide crimson alert blossomed center‑screen—**“Baseline tampering detected; dial rotation authority frozen; countdown 47:59:12.”**

Lao Chen inserted the capture arm’s probe into the bulkhead maintenance port. Blue‑white arcs erupted in vacuum, scattering fine sparks that illuminated the dense lattice of scratches on his prosthesis—each groove a record of a capture, each a near‑miss with death.  

“Authority frozen doesn’t mean locked,” Lao Chen rasped, his voice metallic. “Curator Guan destroyed the logs only because that mold‑stained scrap of paper hid the rain line’s true baseline. Export the disputed version, mount it on backup node 03; I’ll pry the dial’s physical lock.”

Lin Tan clenched the quota card, the cold at her fingertips hardening into a firm cadence, syncing with the scarlet countdown burning on her retina. She knew the next forty‑eight hours would see the rain line rewritten by their own hands.  

The maintenance port still sputtered faint blue arcs. The coolant pump’s low‑frequency pulse faltered for a single cardiac interval, then shuddered at a frequency never felt before—as if the entire orbit held its breath awaiting the next droplet’s landing. Lao Chen glanced back, eyes reflecting that weak blue glow, and whispered, “The rain line is breathing; we decide where the next drop falls.” Lin Tan offered no reply, only pressed the quota card against her chest, feeling the metal’s chill seep into her palm as the countdown continued its relentless tick, red numbers blazing on her retina.
