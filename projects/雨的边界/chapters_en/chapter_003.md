# Chapter 3: The Musty Archive and Magnetic Tapes

A thin layer of must hovered in the air of the underground constant-temperature vault, like aged paper pulp fermenting slowly in damp darkness, blended with the low hum of magnetic tape machinery turning, drifting through ventilation ducts into Lin Tan's nostrils. She stood on concrete flooring coated in anti-static paint, chill rising through her soles; fingertips brushing the metal shelf edges caught a dry, abrasive roughness — time-dust accumulated from over a hundred thousand reels of magnetic tape held at twenty degrees Celsius, thirty-five percent humidity. A few vintage sodium lamps hung on the walls, their halos congealing into pale yellow mist in the air, illuminating the narrow gaps between shelves and the fine gray dust clinging to Lin Tan's cuffs.  

Curator Guan Qi-ming sat on a mottled iron chair, a vintage optical loupe perched over his left eye, fingertips stained with yellowed paper scraps rubbing together in a soft *shush-shush*. He didn't look up, only stared at the magnetic tape reader still spinning before him, its screen flickering with version number **V‑3.1**, timestamp frozen at **2043‑06‑17 14:23**. Rollers inside the machine rotated slowly amid a faint electrical whine; the oxide layer at the tape's edge bloomed a thread of eerie blue fluorescence beneath the read head, like an ancient pulse beating weakly in darkness.  

"Paper doesn't lie. Magnetic tape doesn't lie either." His voice was low, threaded with a barely perceptible tremor — confirming it to himself as much as to Lin Tan. The words were swallowed by the machine's drone, leaving only the silhouette of moving lips wavering in the lamplight.  

Lin Tan fished the Biometric Stylus from her pocket; its tip glowed, fingerprint and heart-rate pulsing in sync. She pressed the tip to the reader's authentication port; the screen popped **“Identity Confirmed ✓”**, then she drew the fixed **「∟」** gesture (C1) across the display, knuckles clicking faintly. No extra words — only the professional mantra she recited by reflex: "Millimeters don't lie." As fingertips touched metal, a weak static charge crawled up her wrist from the knuckle joints, recalling the chill of the first time she'd gripped this stylus in the lab.  

"The Three Blocs are already pressuring me," Guan Qi-ming's voice dropped, carrying a suppressed rasp. "They want me to destroy this reel — say the 'version is archived, no need to retain.' But I still hold an unverified checksum node they don't know about." He pointed at the server array behind him, fingertip tracing a row of blinking green LEDs, light refracting tiny rainbows in his nail beds. "The Carbon Ledger write process requires double-blind signing. If I upload the original footage now, the ledger will auto-tag 'Disputed Version,' triggering the Climate Mutually Assured Destruction condition (W7)."  

Lin Tan fell silent a moment; the old injury on her right knee's inner side ached dully in the damp air. She pressed the pain-relief patch instinctively; the peeled adhesive edge hissed faintly at her fingertips. Then she reached out and tapped the magnetic tape reader's metal housing, feeling fine vibration travel through finger bones to palm — like a heartbeat echoing inside steel ribs. She knew: only by writing this data into the distributed verification nodes before the Cold Slot cooling window (W6) opened could they force the Three Blocs to halt the 27th Dial turn within the forty-eight-hour veto window.  

"I'm not uploading data to the Bloc servers," Lin Tan said, pace even, gaze fixed on Guan Qi-ming's mold-dusted fingertips. "Only to the distributed verification nodes, co-signed by you and me, generating an immutable hash. What follows goes to the Arbitration Court's procedure." Her voice held no inflection, yet cut strangely clear through the machine's low moan, as if each syllable were etched into the tape's magnetic domains.  

Guan Qi-ming finally lifted his head; eyes behind the loupe were scalpel-sharp, mouth corner curling in a weary smile. "Good. Cold Slot window runs 02:00 to 06:00 — four hours of cooling slots reserved for us. You take the tape; I'll align the read head with the original signing footage. As long as we keep those engineers from shoving the tape into their proprietary channels, the ledger will speak for itself." Bittersweet humor laced his words, like a joke aimed at some unspeakable fate.  

The two held each other's gaze in the humming; the vault's must seemed to thicken, as if every mote of dust were recording the weight of this transaction. Lin Tan shoved the tape cartridge into her personal radiation-shielded case; the lid thudded shut like an oath settling in metal. The inner foam squeaked faintly under compression, as if protesting this sudden sealing.  

The reader's rollers abruptly accelerated, releasing a shriek of metal friction that tore the silence. The screen flashed, green characters dancing on black: **“Version V‑3.1 Original Signing Footage 2043‑06‑17 14:23.”** The cursor blinked after the string — a waiting eye, quietly watching this seventeen-year-sealed truth, awaiting the next dawn's Cold Slot window when it would be written into that Carbon Ledger that never forgets.  

Before two a.m., the reader's fans suddenly spun up; cold air raked across Lin Tan's cheekbones like a sharp scraper, carrying a metallic tang of ozone and lubricant into her nose. She clamped the radiation-shielded case against her thigh; the thermal conductive gel inside warmed against body heat, and through the thin carbon-fiber layer her fingertips could still feel the fine pressure-mark on the cartridge's edge — the checksum code Guan Qi-ming had carved by hand seventeen years ago, like a wound that never healed.  

Guan Qi-ming's fingers hovered above the read head, nail beds still embedded with solder dross from last week's server teardown. He stared at the screen; each green cursor blink drew a dull *clunk* from the server array, as if swallowing something heavy. His Adam's apple bobbed; a hoarse laugh scraped his throat: "Three minutes till the cooling slots fully open. Don't let your knee start shaking then."  

Lin Tan didn't answer, only pressed the pain-relief patch on her right knee tighter; adhesive hissed faintly in the damp air, a silent protest. She felt the case's internal foam slowly rebound under pressure, each rebound accompanied by a weak *creak* — as if reminding her: time waits for no one.  

The screen's countdown ticked from 03:58 to 03:57; a low pneumatic roar sounded from the Cold Slot's cooling valves in the distance. Liquid nitrogen expanded instantly in the pipes; white mist climbed the metal grilles, condensing into dense frost flowers on the walls. Guan Qi-ming slammed "Write"; data streamed through fiber like liquid mercury. The Carbon Ledger's double-blind signing module lit two rows of red text: **“Disputed Version · Pending Arbitration.”**  

The must in the air was diluted by ozone, leaving only the machine's monotone drone and two rapid, restrained breaths. Lin Tan watched the cursor halt on the final line, fingertips stroking the case lid's edge; the micro-vibration through her finger pads synced like a heartbeat — two minutes of the four-hour window already gone, the countdown **47:59:12** still whispering at her ear. The radiation-shielded case's latch suddenly jammed; the lid tilted up, revealing an unlogged reel, its edge bearing a faded handwritten label: **“Spare.”** Guan Qi-ming's fingertip hovered over the latch, lips moving, but only half a sentence escaped: "If this reel…"
