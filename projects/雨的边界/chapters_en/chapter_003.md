# Chapter 3: The Musty Smell of the Archive and the Magnetic Tapes

A thin layer of must drifted through the air of the underground constant‑temperature vault, like aged paper pulp fermenting slowly in damp darkness, mingling with the low hum of the tape drives that seeped through the ventilation ducts into Lin Tan’s nostrils. She stood on concrete painted with anti‑static coating; a faint chill rose through the soles of her shoes. When her fingertips brushed the edge of a metal shelf, a dry, gritty roughness scraped across her skin — the accumulated “time dust” of hundreds of thousands of magnetic tapes kept at a steady 20 °C and 35 % humidity. A few old sodium lamps hung on the walls; their halos congealed into pale yellow mist in the air, illuminating the narrow gaps between shelves and the fine gray dust clinging to Lin Tan’s cuff.  

Old Curator Guan Qiming sat on a mottled iron chair, a vintage optical magnifier perched over his left eye, yellowed paper fragments stuck to the fingertips of his right hand. He rubbed them gently, producing a soft “shhh” sound. He did not look up, eyes fixed on the tape reader still turning before him. The screen flickered with version **V‑3.1**, timestamp frozen at **2043‑06‑17 14:23**. Inside the machine the rollers rotated slowly amid a faint electrical whine; the oxide layer at the tape’s edge glowed a ghostly blue beneath the read head, as if an ancient pulse were beating weakly in the dark.  

“Paper does not lie, and magnetic tape does not lie either,” he murmured, a barely perceptible tremor in his voice, as though confirming it to himself as much as to Lin Tan. The words were swallowed by the machine’s hum, leaving only the outline of his moving lips visible in the lamp light.  

Lin Tan withdrew the biometric fingerprint pen from her pocket; its tip glowed faintly, fingerprint and heartbeat flashing in sync. She pressed the tip to the reader’s authentication port. The screen displayed **“Identity confirmed ✓”**, then she traced the fixed **「∟」** gesture (C1) on the display, a faint click sounding from her knuckle. She offered no extra words, only the professional mantra: “Millimetres do not lie.” As her fingertip touched metal, a weak static charge crawled up her wrist, recalling the chill of the first time she had gripped this pen in the lab.  

“The Three Great Blocs are already pressuring me,” Guan Qiming’s voice dropped, a suppressed rasp. “They want me to destroy this reel, claiming the ‘version is archived, no need to retain.’ But I still hold an unpublished checksum node they don’t know about.” He pointed toward the server array behind him, his fingertip brushing a row of blinking green LEDs; the light fractured into tiny rainbows in the crevices of his nails. “The carbon ledger’s write process requires a double‑blind signature. If I upload the original footage now, the ledger will automatically flag ‘disputed version,’ triggering the climate‑mutual‑destruction condition (W7).”  

Lin Tan paused; the old injury on the inner side of her right knee throbbed in the humid air. She instinctively pressed the analgesic patch; the lifted edge of the adhesive hissed softly at her fingertips. Then she reached out and lightly tapped the metal casing of the tape drive, feeling a subtle vibration travel through her fingertips to her palm, like a heartbeat resonating inside steel ribs. She knew that only before the Cold Slot’s cooling window (W6) opened could this data be written to the distributed verification nodes, forcing the Three Great Blocs to halt the 27th dial turn within the forty‑eight‑hour veto window.  

“I will not upload the data to the Blocs’ servers,” Lin Tan said, voice steady, eyes on Guan Qiming’s dust‑speckled fingertips. “Only to the distributed verification nodes, co‑signed by you and me, producing an immutable hash. What follows belongs to the Arbitration Tribunal’s procedure.” Her tone carried no inflection, yet each syllable seemed etched into the magnetic domains of the tape amid the machine’s low drone.  

Guan Qiming finally lifted his head; the eyes behind the magnifier were sharp as blades, a weary smile tugging at the corner of his mouth. “Good. The Cold Slot window runs 02:00‑06:00, the cooling slots allotted to us for only four hours. You take the tape; I’ll align the read head with the original signature footage. As long as we keep the engineers from shoving the tape into their proprietary channel, the ledger will speak for itself.” A bitter humor laced his words, as if resisting some unspeakable fate.  

The two faced each other in the humming gloom; the musty scent seemed to thicken, as though every mote of dust recorded the weight of this transaction. Lin Tan slipped the tape cartridge into her personal radiation‑shielded case; the lid thudded shut with a dull thump, like a vow settling into metal. The foam lining protested with a faint “creak” under the pressure.  

The drive’s rollers suddenly accelerated, releasing a sharp metallic screech that sliced the silence. The screen flashed, green characters dancing on black: **“Version V‑3.1 original signature footage 2043‑06‑17 14:23.”** The cursor blinked after the line, a patient eye awaiting instruction, quietly watching the seventeen‑year‑old truth wait for the next dawn’s Cold Slot window to be inscribed onto the ever‑remembering carbon ledger.  

Before 02:00 the drive’s fans surged, a blast of cold air raking across Lin Tan’s cheekbones like a keen scraper, carrying a metallic tang of ozone and lubricant into her nose. She pressed the shielded case against her thigh; the thermal paste on the case warmed slightly against her skin. Through the thin carbon‑fiber layer her fingertips could still feel the faint indentation on the cartridge’s edge — the verification code Guan Qiming had etched by hand seventeen years ago, a wound that never healed.  

Guan Qiming’s fingers hovered above the read head, flecks of solder from last week’s server teardown still lodged under his nails. He stared at the screen; each blink of the green cursor elicited a heavy “clunk” from the server racks, as if they were swallowing something massive. His Adam’s apple bobbed, a hoarse laugh escaping his throat: “Three more minutes until the cooling slots fully open. Don’t let your knee buckle then.”  

Lin Tan did not reply, only pressed the analgesic patch tighter against her inner knee; the adhesive hissed faintly in the damp air, a silent protest. She sensed the foam inside the case slowly rebound under pressure, each rebound accompanied by a soft “creak,” reminding her that time waits for no one.  

The countdown on the screen ticked from 03:58 to 03:57. In the distance the Cold Slot’s vent valves let out a low pneumatic roar; liquid nitrogen expanded instantly, white vapor climbing the metal grilles and frosting into delicate crystals on the walls. Guan Qiming slammed “Write.” Data streamed through the fiber like liquid mercury; the carbon ledger’s double‑blind signature module lit two rows of red text: **“Disputed version·Pending arbitration.”**  

The musty odor was diluted by ozone, leaving only the monotonous drone of machinery and the two’s rapid, restrained breathing. Lin Tan watched the cursor halt on the final line, her fingertip brushing the case’s edge; the micro‑vibration transmitted to her fingertip synced like a heartbeat — two minutes of the four‑hour window already elapsed, the countdown **47:59:12** still whispering in her ears. Suddenly the case’s latch jammed; the lid lifted a fraction, revealing an unregistered tape, its edge bearing a faded handwritten label: **“Spare.”** Guan Qiming’s fingertip hovered over the latch, lips moving, but only half a sentence escaped: “If this reel…”
