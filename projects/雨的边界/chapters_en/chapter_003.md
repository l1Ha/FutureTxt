# Chapter 3: The Archive’s Musty Smell and the Tape

A thin veil of mold drifted through the air of the underground constant‑temperature vault, the scent of aged paper pulp fermenting slowly in damp darkness, mingling with the low hum of the tape drive as it travelled the ventilation ducts into Lin Tan’s nostrils. She stood on concrete coated with anti‑static paint, a faint chill rising through her soles; her fingertips brushed the edge of a metal shelf, catching the dry, gritty residue accumulated by hundreds of thousands of reels held at twenty degrees Celsius and thirty‑five percent humidity. A few old sodium lamps hung on the walls, their halos congealing into pale‑yellow mist that illuminated the narrow gaps between shelves and the fine dust clinging to Lin Tan’s cuff.  

Curator Guan sat on a mottled iron chair, a vintage optical magnifier perched over his left eye, yellowed paper fragments stuck to the fingertips of his right hand as he rubbed them together, producing a soft “shhh.” He did not look up, his gaze fixed on the still‑spinning tape reader; the screen flickered with version **V‑3.1**, timestamp frozen at **2043‑06‑17 14:23**. Inside the machine the rollers turned in a faint electrical whisper, the oxide layer on the tape’s edge glowing a ghostly blue beneath the read head, like an ancient pulse throbbing weakly in the dark.  

“Paper does not lie, and tape does not lie,” he murmured, a barely perceptible tremor in his voice, as if confirming it to himself as much as to Lin Tan. The words were swallowed by the machine’s drone; only the outline of his moving lips was visible in the lamp light.  

Lin Tan retrieved the Biometric Fingerprint Pen from her pocket; its tip glowed faintly, fingerprint and heartbeat syncing in a steady blink. She pressed the tip to the reader’s authentication port — the screen flashed **“Identity confirmed ✓”** — then drew the fixed **「∟」** gesture (C1) across the display, a faint click sounding from her knuckles. She offered no extra words, only the professional mantra: “Millimeters don’t lie.” As her fingertip touched metal, a weak static charge crawled up her wrist, recalling the chill of the first time she had gripped this pen in the lab.  

“The Three Blocs are pressuring me,” Guan’s voice dropped, hoarse with suppressed strain. “They want me to destroy this reel, claim the ‘version is archived, no need to retain.’ But I still hold an un‑uploaded verification node, unknown to them.” He gestured toward the server rack behind him, his fingertip tracing a row of blinking green LEDs; the light fractured into tiny rainbows in his nail beds. “The Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger’s write process requires a double‑blind signature. If I upload the raw footage now, the ledger will automatically tag it ‘Disputed Version,’ triggering the Climate Mutually Assured Destruction condition (W7).”  

Lin Tan paused, the old injury on the inner side of her right knee aching in the humid air; she pressed the pain‑relief patch, the adhesive edge hissing softly against her fingertip. Then she tapped the metal casing of the tape drive, feeling the minute vibration travel through her finger bones to her palm, like a heartbeat echoing inside steel ribs. She knew that only before the Cold Slot cooling window (W6) opened could this data be written to the distributed verification node, forcing the Three Blocs to halt the 27th knob turn within the forty‑eight‑hour veto window.  

“I won’t upload the data to the Bloc servers,” Lin Tan said, tone even, eyes on Guan’s mold‑stained fingertips. “Only to the distributed verification node, co‑signed by you and me, producing an immutable hash. The rest goes to the Arbitration Court’s procedure.” Her voice held no inflection, yet cut sharply through the machine’s low moan, as if each syllable were etched into the tape’s magnetic domains.  

Guan finally lifted his head; behind the magnifier his eyes were razor‑sharp, a weary smile tugging at his mouth. “Good. The Cold Slot window runs 02:00–06:00, the cooling slot only grants us four hours. You take the tape; I’ll align the read head on the original signature footage. As long as we keep the engineers from shoving the reel into their proprietary channel, the ledger will speak for itself.” A bitter humor laced his words, as though he were fending off some nameless destiny.  

The two faced each other amid the hum, the mold scent seeming to thicken, as if every dust mote recorded the weight of this transaction. Lin Tan slipped the tape cartridge into her radiation‑shielded case; the lid thudded shut like a vow sealed in metal. The foam lining protested with a faint “creak” under the sudden pressure.  

The drive’s rollers suddenly accelerated, emitting a piercing metallic screech that shattered the silence. The screen flared, green characters dancing on black: **“Version V‑3.1 original signature footage 2043‑06‑17 14:23.”** The cursor blinked after the line, a patient eye awaiting the command that would, at the next dawn’s Cold Slot window, write this seventeen‑year‑old truth into the ever‑remembering carbon ledger.  

Before 02:00 the drive’s fans roared to life, a sharp blast of cold air slicing across Lin Tan’s cheekbones, carrying a metallic tang of ozone and lubricant. She pressed the shielded case against her thigh; the thermal paste on the case warmed faintly against her skin, and through the thin carbon‑fiber layer she could still feel the minute ridge on the cartridge’s edge — the verification code Guan had etched seventeen years ago, a wound that never healed.  

Guan’s fingers hovered over the read head, flecks of solder from last week’s server teardown still lodged under his nails. He stared at the screen; each blink of the green cursor elicited a dull “clunk” from the server rack, as if it were swallowing something heavy. His Adam’s apple bobbed, a rasping laugh escaping his throat: “Three minutes left before the cooling slot fully opens. Don’t let your knee give out then.”  

Lin Tan didn’t reply, merely pressed the patch tighter on her inner knee; the adhesive hissed again in the damp air, a silent protest. She sensed the foam inside the case slowly rebounding under pressure, each rebound accompanied by a faint “creak,” reminding her: time does not wait.  

The countdown on the screen ticked from 03:58 to 03:57; far away the Cold Slot’s vent valves groaned open, liquid nitrogen expanding instantly, white vapor climbing the metal grilles and frosting into delicate crystals. Guan slammed “Write.” Data surged through fiber like liquid mercury; the Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger’s double‑blind signature module lit two rows of red text: **“Disputed Version·Pending Arbitration.”**  

The mold scent was diluted by ozone, leaving only the monotonous drone of machinery and the two restrained, rapid breaths. Lin Tan watched the cursor settle on the final line, her fingertip brushing the case’s edge; the micro‑vibration transmitted to her fingertip synced like a heartbeat — two minutes of the four‑hour window had elapsed, the red “Disputed Version·Pending Arbitration” tag began to flash, the countdown **47:59:12** ticking in lockstep — the Three Blocs’ authorization chain must decide within forty‑eight hours, or the global carbon ledger will automatically freeze knob‑turning authority.
