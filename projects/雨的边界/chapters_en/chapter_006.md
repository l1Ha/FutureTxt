# Chapter 6: The Arbitration Court's 48-Hour Game

Cold air seeped from the holo‑wall, carrying the scent of ozone and metal coolant into the nostrils, like a thin frost clinging to Lin Tan’s skin; a low‑frequency hum rose from the floor, vibrating the bones of her feet, and a faint static prickle tingled at her fingertips when they brushed the edge of the signing screen. A stale iron‑rust odor still drifted in the air—the musty, damp breath that had accumulated year after year in the maglev pilgrimage buses of the Abandoned Zone, seeping through the ventilation ducts into the conference room, reminding every person seated at the dial that the land beyond the rain line had long since cracked into a salt crust.  

The room was oval. A real‑time rain‑line projection hovered in the centre, its blue‑green contour lines trembling slightly with each data stream. Engineers from the Three Blocs, monitors from the Global Thermostat Treaty Organization, actuaries from the Insurance Consortium formed a circle, each with a biometric fingerprint ring on the table, the knuckles catching the light with a cold metallic sheen. Chief Judge Hermann sat at the head, the ring on his left ring finger pulsing faintly with his heartbeat, his right index finger tapping the tabletop in a crisp “click, click,” as if timing the imminent double‑blind signing. Scattered data chips on the tabletop condensed tiny droplets in the chill, refracting the hidden fiber‑optic array above.  

Lin Tan’s old knee injury tightened with the draft from the air‑conditioning; the analgesic patch on her sweat‑slick skin emitted a faint hiss, the lifted corner of the adhesive turning cool and sticky under her fingertip. She pulled the W‑12 Portable Laser Raindrop Spectrometer from her pocket; its metal casing radiated a subtle residual warmth, the probe trembling minutely at her fingertips. The display froze at **112.3 mm ±0.12**—the last reading she had taken at Hydrological Station 112, the error band still within the **W2** tolerance. She pressed the Biometric Fingerprint Pen to the reader, feeling a faint electric sting at the tip; the screen flashed **“Identity confirmed ✓”**, and she traced the fixed **「∟」** gesture on the panel, the knuckles clicking like a precision gear meshing.  

“Reading 112.3 mm, error 0.12, compliant with W2.” Lin Tan’s tone was flat, as though reciting a physical constant; her voice stretched and flattened in the holo‑wall’s echo, leaving only pure numbers.  

Chief Judge Hermann’s gaze swept the three delegations, his cadence measured: “Authorization chain **W1** has completed double‑blind signing; the log is written to the Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger. The 27th dial‑turn window has 48 hours remaining; any party that vetoes twice consecutively within 48 h will trigger **W7** climate mutually assured destruction.” His voice carried a mechanical flatness; a swipe of his fingertip across the ring shifted its light band from cyan to violet.  

North American Federation engineer Mark spoke first, his fingers dancing across the holo‑keyboard: “We submit a veto on the grounds that baseline version V‑3.1 shows signs of tampering; we demand a halt to the dial turn pending archive verification.” A red line appeared on the screen: **Veto 1/2**.  

Eurasian Alliance monitor Ye Lin frowned, her fingertip lingering on the ring before confirming: “Agree to the pause; require original magnetic‑tape upload to the ledger within 24 hours.” A second red line lit: **Veto 2/2**.  

East Asian Consortium actuary Sun Hao fell silent for three seconds, his fingertip hovering over the confirm key, micro‑data streams flickering across his brow. He murmured, “Further delay will miss the Cold Slot auction this quarter; the Compute Standard exchange rate will swing more than 7 %.” He finally pressed, and **“Pending confirmation”** replaced the third red line.  

A dull ache throbbed deep in Lin Tan’s right knee; the patch’s adhesive edge curled further in the sweat. She inhaled deeply; palm sweat evaporated on the metal, leaving a thin crust of salt. The holo‑rain line before her drifted, the ±0.5 mm contour at the blue‑green boundary rising and falling like breath.  

Ten years ago, that night flashed back: at Station 112 she had altered the reading from 112.5 mm to 112.3 mm, solely to grant her friend’s district an extra 0.2 mm quota. The result—her friend’s district was ruled “over‑quota,” the entire farmland frozen for three years, the old friend losing the whole harvest to drought. She had heard no protest then, only the whisper of wind over cracked soil, like fingernails on a blackboard. Now the same fingertip hovered over the same screen; the only differences were the knee’s old scar and the countdown ticking on the display.  

“Numbers don’t lie.” She whispered it in her mind, then repeated aloud to the holo‑wall: “The digits won’t deceive me.”  

The countdown in the corner rolled down in seconds: **47:59:12**. The blockchain‑style ledger of the Carbon‑Meter Dual‑Unit Ledger refreshed in sync; each signature was accompanied by a low, resonant electronic chirp, like a heartbeat transmitted through the floor. Lao Chen’s encrypted message flashed: Cold Slot postponed to 06:30, log on‑chain. Lin Tan’s fingertip twitched; the **「∟」** gesture on the screen blinked under the cursor, ready to become **「✓」**.  

Chief Judge Hermann’s ring abruptly flared deep crimson. He rose before the holo‑projection; the chair emitted a faint groan of metal fatigue. “If consensus is not reached within the remaining 48 hours, the Orbital Sunshade Ring will open fully, the Ocean Alkalinity Pump Array will shut down, and global precipitation will shift instantaneously by more than 15 %.” His voice reverberated through the room, bearing an irreversible finality.  

Lin Tan felt the static prickle at her fingertips intensify; the countdown jumped to **47:58:03**. She pressed the W‑12 probe to her forehead; a faint laser traced a thin line across her retina, as if刻ing a scale for the imminent judgment. The scents of the two sides of the rain line rose simultaneously in memory: dry quartz dust and damp moss, compressed into this cramped conference room at the same moment.  

The communicator chimed again; the dispatcher’s cold female voice pierced the encryption layer: “Countdown 00:02:17, please confirm final signing authority.” Lin Tan drew a slow breath, tapped the card three times; the screen popped up **“Sign confirmed·Effective”**, the amber light band snapping to deep blue, a low tone resonating like a bell struck inside her chest.  

The holo‑rain line froze instantly; the blue‑green boundary ceased its drift. Lin Tan’s fingertip remained suspended over the signing screen, the gesture unfinished; the room held only the low electronic chirp and the continuous hum of the air‑conditioning, as if the planet’s entire water cycle had just held its breath. A sharp spike of pain shot from deep in her right knee; the analgesic patch finally peeled away completely, revealing a small patch of inflamed skin. She did not reach to press it, merely let sweat trace down her jaw and fall onto the cold metal tabletop with a clear **drip—tap**.  

The wall projection flickered; the countdown digits trembled faintly. Lin Tan heard, from far away, a low hydraulic valve sigh open.
