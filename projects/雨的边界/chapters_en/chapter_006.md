# Chapter 6: The Tribunal's 48‑Hour Gambit

Cold air seeped from the holographic walls, carrying the scent of ozone and metal coolant into Lin Tan’s nostrils like a thin frost clinging to her skin; a low‑frequency hum rose through the floor, vibrating the seams of her foot bones, and a faint static sting prickled her fingertips when they brushed the edge of the signing screen. A stale rust odor drifted in the air—the musty dampness that had accumulated year‑round in the maglev pilgrimage bus compartments of the abandoned zone, seeping through the ventilation ducts into the conference room, reminding everyone at the dials that the land beyond the rainline had long since cracked into a salt crust.  

The conference room was oval. A real‑time rainline projection hovered in the center, blue‑green contour lines trembling with the data stream. Engineers from the Three Blocs, Treaty Organization inspectors, and Insurance Syndicate actuaries formed a circle, each with a biometric fingerprint ring before them, knuckles catching a cold metallic gleam under the lights. Chairman Hermann sat at the head, the fingerprint ring on his left ring finger pulsing with his heartbeat; his right index finger tapped the table, a crisp “click, click,” as if timing the upcoming double‑blind signing. Scattered data chips on the table condensed tiny dew droplets in the cold air, refracting the hidden fiber‑optic array in the ceiling.  

Lin Tan’s old injury on the inner side of her right knee tightened with the AC draft; the pain patch hissed faintly beneath sweat‑slick skin, a curled edge of adhesive turning cool and tacky under her fingertips. She pulled the W‑12 Portable Laser Disdrometer from her pocket; the probe lit up, the screen still showing the same figure: 112.3 mm ±0.12. For twenty years she had verified that number on the same instrument thousands of times; today she made each verification step deliberately slow—fingerprint, gesture, those clicking knuckles.  

“Still 112.3,” she said. The voice fell into the holographic wall’s echo, stretched, flattened, until only the number remained.  

Chairman Hermann’s gaze swept the three delegations, his tone even: “Authorization chain **W1** has completed double‑blind signing; the log is written to the Carbon Ledger. The 27th dial‑turn window has 48 hours remaining; any party that vetoes twice consecutively within 48 h will trigger **W7** Climate Mutually Assured Destruction.” His voice carried a mechanical flatness; his fingertip brushed the fingerprint ring, its light band shifting from cyan to violet.  

North American Federation engineer Mark spoke first, fingertips typing a command string on the holographic keyboard: “We submit a veto, citing suspected tampering in Baseline version V‑3.1; the dial must be paused until archival verification is complete.” A red line appeared on the screen: **Veto 1/2**.  

Eurasian Union inspector Ye Lin frowned slightly, fingertip lingering on the fingerprint ring before pressing confirm: “Agree to pause; require original tape on‑chain within 24 hours.” The second red line lit: **Veto 2/2**.  

East Asian Synergy actuary Sun Hao fell silent for three seconds, fingertip hovering over the confirm key, faint data‑stream patterns dancing across his brow. He murmured: “If we delay further, the Cold Slot auction will miss this quarter’s window; the compute‑rate will swing more than 7 %.” He finally pressed, and **“Pending Confirmation”** replaced the third red line.  

Lin Tan felt a dull ache deep in her right knee; the adhesive edge of the pain patch curled further in the sweat. She inhaled deeply; palm sweat evaporated on the metal surface, leaving a thin crust of salt. The holographic rainline drifted before her, the ±0.5 mm contour at the blue‑green boundary rising and falling like breath.  

Ten years ago that night flashed back: at Station 112 she altered the reading from 112.5 mm to 112.3 mm, hoping to give her friend’s district an extra 0.2 mm quota; the result was that district was ruled “over‑quota” for the season, its entire farmland frozen for three years, and her old friend lost the whole harvest to drought. What she heard then was not protest but the hiss of wind over cracked soil, like nails on a chalkboard. Now the same fingertip hovers over the same screen; the only differences are the knee’s old injury and the countdown ticking on the display.  

“The reading doesn’t play nice,” she repeated silently, then whispered to the holographic wall: “Numbers won’t lie for me.”  

The countdown scrolled in seconds in the corner: **47:59:12**. The Carbon Ledger’s blockchain‑style log refreshed in sync; each signature was accompanied by a low electronic chirp, like a heartbeat transmitted through the floor. Lao Chen’s encrypted flash message appeared: Cold Slot delayed to 06:30, log on‑chain. Lin Tan’s fingertip twitched; the **「∟」** gesture on the screen flickered beneath the cursor, ready to become **「✓」**.  

Chairman Hermann’s fingerprint‑ring light band snapped to deep crimson. He rose before the holographic projection; the chair back emitted a faint metal fatigue groan: “If consensus cannot be reached within the remaining 48 hours, the Sunshade Ring will open fully, the Alkalinity Pumps will shut down entirely, and global precipitation will shift instantaneously by more than 15 %.” His voice reverberated through the room, carrying an irreversible finality.  

Lin Tan felt the static sting at her fingertips intensify; the countdown jumped to **47:58:03**. She pressed the W‑12 probe to her forehead; the faint laser beam drew a thin line across her retina, as if inscribing a scale for the imminent adjudication. The soil scents from both sides of the holographic rainline rose together in memory: dry quartz dust and damp moss, compressed into this cramped conference room as if simultaneously.  

The comms buzzed again; the dispatcher’s crisp female voice cut through the encryption: “Countdown 00:02:17, confirm final signing authority.” Lin Tan inhaled, tapped the card face three times; the screen popped “Signature Confirmed·Effective,” the amber light band flipped to deep blue, a low chime resonating like a bell struck in her chest.  

The rainline projection on the holographic wall froze instantly; the blue‑green boundary stopped drifting. Lin Tan’s fingertip remained suspended over the signing screen, gesture uncompleted; the room was left with only the low electronic chirp and the continuous hum of the HVAC, as if the planet’s entire water cycle had held its breath. A sharp spike of pain shot from deep in Lin Tan’s right knee; the pain‑patch adhesive finally peeled away completely, revealing a small patch of red, swollen skin. She did not reach to press it, merely let sweat slide down her jaw and drop onto the cold metal tabletop with a clear “drip—tap.”  

The wall projection suddenly flickered; the countdown digits jittered. Lin Tan heard a distant, low hydraulic valve opening.
