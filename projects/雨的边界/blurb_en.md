In 2126, the "Global Thermostat"—a lattice of stratospheric injectors, orbital mirrors, and alkalinity pumps—turns rainfall into a tradable currency, each millimeter locked to the 2040s baseline by the Global Thermostat Accord. When Precipitation Arbitrator Lin Tan audits Station 112 on the Tibetan fringe, a 3.7-millimeter surplus shatters the mandate of zero variance. The anomaly pulls her into the archives of the 2043 Baseline Convention, where a single forged signature slashed a nation’s quota from 210 to 165 millimeters, legally desiccating four hundred million people downstream for eight decades. Now, the three power blocs converge to ratify a new allocation cycle built on that same poisoned data. With the signing ceremony hours away, Lin must weaponize hydrology and history to force the falsified numbers back into the soil on both sides of the rain line—before the next turn of the Thermostat makes the drought permanent.
