In 2126 the planet’s climate is governed by the Global Thermostat — a triad of stratospheric aerosol injections, orbital reflectors, and ocean alkalinity pumps that shift monsoon belts with every degree of adjustment. Under the Global Temperature Accord, each jurisdiction’s annual rainfall quota is frozen to a 2040 baseline, turning precipitation into a tradable commodity. Arbiter Lin Tan’s routine audit of Hydrological Station 112 on the Tibetan fringe reveals a 3.7‑millimetre excess over the locked figure, a discrepancy that should not exist. Tracing the anomaly to the 2043 baseline revision, she uncovers a delegate’s covert alteration of a key watershed’s record from 210 to 165 millimetres, legally depriving four hundred million downstream people of water for eight decades. As the three major power blocs prepare to ratify a new allocation cycle using the same forged baseline, Lin must expose the fraud and restore the true numbers before the signing ceremony seals the injustice forever.
