In 2126 the planet’s climate is governed by the Global Thermostat — a triad of stratospheric aerosol injectors, orbital reflectors, and ocean alkalinity pumps that shift monsoon belts with every degree of adjustment. Under the Global Temperature Accord, each jurisdiction’s annual rainfall quota is frozen to a 2040s baseline, turning precipitation into a tradable allocation. Arbitrator Lin Tan’s routine audit of Hydrological Station 112 on the Tibetan fringe reveals a 3.7‑millimetre excess, a discrepancy that should not exist. Tracing the anomaly to the 2043 baseline revision, she uncovers a delegate’s covert alteration of a key figure from 210 mm to 165 mm, legally depriving the nation and 400 million downstream people of rain for eight decades. As the three dominant consortia prepare to ratify a new allocation cycle using the same falsified baseline, Lin must restore the true numbers before the signing ceremony seals the injustice into the soil on both sides of the rain line.
