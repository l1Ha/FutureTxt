In 2126 the planet’s climate is governed by the “Global Thermostat” — a triad of stratospheric aerosol injectors, orbital reflectors, and ocean alkalinity pumps that keep temperature stable by shifting the monsoon belt with every fractional adjustment. Under the Global Temperature Accord, each jurisdiction’s annual rainfall is frozen to a 2040s baseline, turning precipitation into a tradable quota. When precipitation arbitrator Lin Tan audits a remote Tibetan hydrological station, the reading exceeds the archived figure by 3.7 millimetres — an impossibility within the treaty’s zero‑error margin. Tracing the anomaly to the 2043 baseline revision, she uncovers a delegate’s covert alteration of a key watershed’s baseline from 210 mm to 165 mm, legally depriving that nation and four hundred million downstream people of rain for eight decades. As the three dominant power blocs prepare to ratify a new allocation cycle using the same forged baseline, Lin must race the arbitration ceremony to restore the true numbers before they are etched into the soil on both sides of the rain line.
