In 2126, the "Virtue Flow Index" monetizes altruism: merchants distribute free meals for "virtue points" redeemable for algorithmic visibility, rent subsidies, and tax breaks. On Nine Rivers Street, Lao Fan has stewed his "Lao Fan Noodle House" for thirty-one years, its soul a master stock broth never once replaced. Then "Zero-Yuan Noodles" opens—central-kitchen bases, total data upload, zero barriers—siphoning the lunch crowd and cratering neighboring revenues by thirty percent. Regulators intervene: charity is legal but must be "targeted." Free quotas are restricted to registered lists; the freed "virtue nodes" are re-tendered. The terms: absorb the subsidies, adopt certified bases, surrender full operational data, keep the signboard. Lao Fan’s ancestral broth isn't on the whitelist. His daughter works the rival counter; the district manager slides a boilerplate contract; compliance officers stress legality; scalpers auction queue spots; regulars push bowls toward him, whispering, "Brother Fan, just sign." Lao Fan stares at the paper that would drag the queue back to his door—at the cost of extinguishing the last human flavor in the pot.
