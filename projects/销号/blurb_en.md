In 2126, Yunling Village is the county’s final settlement slated for erasure. Seventy‑three‑year‑old Chen Laoshuan is its sole resident, offered a 1.2‑million‑yuan compensation package that locks 800,000 into “nostalgia vouchers” usable only on a state‑approved platform. His wife’s grave is reclassified as a surface attachment and must be cleared within forty‑eight hours; his terrace field will be replanted as a carbon‑sink forest. His granddaughter, Chen Xiaoyu, a county‑appointed rural‑operations specialist, secures him an “on‑site witness” role: twenty hours of live‑streamed farming each month. He signs, preserving three cypress trees and a single acre, but the price is a life now performed for an audience, every daily ritual priced and broadcast. The arrangement turns his remaining days into a curated spectacle, where memory, labor, and landscape become commodities under a relentless digital gaze.
