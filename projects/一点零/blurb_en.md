When a routine school vision test flags ten‑year‑old Xiaozhou’s right eye at 0.4, her mother, He Xiulan, confronts a cascade of hard choices. A cashier earning 4,200 yuan a month, she juggles a 2,300‑yuan mortgage and a husband who has been on the road for half a year. The ophthalmologist delivers a blunt verdict: true myopia, irreversible, only manageable — specialty defocus lenses cost 8,000 yuan a year, ordinary frames a mere 300. Desperate, He follows a viral “degree‑reset” scheme promoted on social media, sending 2,260 yuan to a distant clinic for custom eye patches that promise a two‑line gain on a private chart. The school screening, however, still reads 0.4. When the story breaks, the clinic demands a signed waiver — no further claims, no publicity — and offers only a partial refund. Caught between a binding fingerprint box and a flashing WeChat reminder, He finally walks into an optical shop, accepting the glasses she can afford.
