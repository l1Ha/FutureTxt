# Chapter 4: The Audit Log’s Cognitive Reversal

The cooling fans in the terminal room droned at 13 Hz, their vibration climbing the metal floor into the soles of the feet, mingling with the sharp scent of ozone and coolant that stabbed the nostrils like rusted iron filings scraping a throat. A holographic projection bloomed behind Han Ce’s right ear, a semi‑transparent hash rotating slowly, its edges fringed with a faint cyan glow that cast a thin blue shadow across his cheekbone, rising and falling with each breath, the light spots refracting into tiny prisms on his skin. In the center of the screen, the audit log scrolled a glaring line of red text: **Fear‑Synthesis Module 3.0 generated, hash does not match E‑11 historical brainwave**, the characters trembling faintly against the blue backdrop, as if every pixel were quivering in anticipation of the coming verdict.

E‑11’s heart hammered at 180 bpm, skin‑conductance readings surging past the red line, **Anger 92%**, **Fear 87%**, volatility fully lit, the curves in the monitor window thrashing like snakes whipped by electric current. A low‑frequency hum rose from the inner wrist, lasting three seconds, and the account balance refreshed to **0.00**, the digits leaving a gray‑white afterimage on the retina. Sweat beads seeped from the hairline, slid down the temples across the cheekbones, and fell onto the slightly raised keys at the keyboard’s edge, producing a faint “tap” that was swallowed by the fan’s whine, leaving only the cool metallic taste at the fingertips.

“The metrics are fine,” she repeated in a low voice, a rasp in her tone as if a shard of ice were lodged in her throat, each word shattered by the dry air and the fan’s gusts.  

Han Ce’s voice held no warmth, only the weight of rules, each syllable calibrated like a precision torque wrench tightening in the air: “Provenance Layer integrity admits no compromise. Upload a perfect fear, the system judges it passed; refuse, and it judges data inconsistency, risk score jumps straight to 84, full quota freeze, medical priority dropped to minimum.” His left eye’s quantum flicker remained at 13 Hz, a blue speck tracing a thin line along the rim of the socket, like an invisible scalpel carving away the last of her negotiating room.

E‑11 stared at the pulsing “Fear Purity” curve on the terminal, fingertips hovering, the pads feeling a faint static sting as though touching a thin insulating film. The memory of watching her mother die in the Gray Belt at age twelve when her quota froze had never been uploaded, never synthesized by any module. She refused to feed a fabricated fear to billions of agents, and she refused to let “truth” become a programmable parameter. Fingertips blanched, nails leaving shallow scratches on the metal keycaps, a faint rust scent seeping from the grooves, mingling with the coolant’s acidity.

“Upload blank brainwave.” She pressed confirm; the screen flashed “Writing…” then erupted in red: **Data inconsistency, risk score 84, quota zeroed**. The prompt crawled up the bone, becoming a long, continuous hum that traveled along the radius to the elbow, up the humerus to the scapula, finally settling as a sustained pressure on the eardrum, like a taut steel cable trembling deep in the skeleton.

The terminal spat out gray‑white text: **Quota zeroed·Work order APL‑777·Estimated processing 14 days**. In the background the compute‑layer fans kept their monotone low‑frequency drone; distant public screens flickered with Mirror Domain footage, colors 30 % more saturated than reality, noise points hiding the fans’ synchronized vibration. Indicator lights blinked at 0.5 Hz, each on‑off cycle etching a countdown tick onto the retina, blue light refracting into fine star‑dust inside the pupils.

E‑11 felt the quota zeroing, flash‑frames of subway turnstiles, ER triage lines, self‑service cafeterias rejecting her scrolling through her mind—risk score 84 had triggered every restriction. Every frame brought a chill climbing the spine, as if invisible fingers lightly pinched the spinal cord, cold seeping down the vertebral canal into the limbs, fingertips tingling.

Han Ce murmured, “Deviation within tolerance.” His left eye’s quantum flicker persisted at 13 Hz; the dashboard’s red line faded slowly, and the audit log automatically wrote **“Sensor fault”** into the Provenance Layer. His finger rested on the confirm key; the glow at the fingertip collapsed the instant it was pressed, like a dying star, leaving only a wisp of residual blue smoke drifting away in the airflow.

E‑11 did not turn back, fingers still hovering at the terminal’s edge, the faint current crawling from the scar across her palm. The fans never ceased, the metal floor trembling faintly, as if the entire facility were shuddering in rhythm with her breath.

The gray‑white work‑order number on the screen continued to blink, the 0.5 Hz indicator etching its countdown onto the retina; fingertips remained poised above the confirm key, a weak current sliding along the scar toward the palm, like a slender electric snake moving beneath the skin.

“Still waiting?” Han Ce’s voice forced itself from his throat, flat as if reciting a long‑memorized maintenance manual, his left eye’s quantum flicker still at 13 Hz, the blue speck tracing a fine line along the socket’s rim. He did not raise his head, finger still hovering over the confirm key, the fingertip glow trembling in the air, ready to extinguish again, a tiny halo forming at the edge of the light, vanishing in a gust of fan‑driven air.

E‑11 did not answer, merely pressed her palm against the cold terminal frame; the metal chill climbed the lines of her hand into the wrist, merging with the bone‑conduction’s continuous hum. She felt the gray barcode at the nape of her neck warm slightly under the scanner, the designation **E‑11** and **4 312 hours** of harvest time branded like two lines of ink into every heartbeat, each pulse reminding her that the system had logged every second of her existence. The distant public screens still flashed Mirror Domain images at 30 % higher saturation, noise points concealing the fans’ synchronized vibration, color blocks leaving afterimages on the retina like an over‑exposed negative, edges blurred yet painfully sharp.

“Upload blank brainwave, recorded.” She whispered again, fingertip finally descending, and the moment it touched the confirm key the static vanished, replaced by a subtle vibration traveling up the finger bones to the forearm, resonating like a plucked string deep in the skeleton. The red text on the screen remained unchanged; work order **APL‑777** continued its silent 14‑day countdown, digits breathing faintly in the blue light.

Han Ce’s left‑eye speck flared out, the dashboard’s final red line disappearing completely. He withdrew his hand, the fingertip tracing a faint cyan trail in the air before the fan’s draft scattered it, leaving only a barely perceptible whiff of ozone. The audit terminal appended automatically at the log’s last line: **“Sensor fault·Auto‑marked·Pending manual verification”**, the line glowing softly in the blue light, the sole pulsing point in the room, a lone lamp keeping watch amid the endless data flood.

E‑11 sensed a faint, regular tapping rising from beneath the floor—not the fans, but the activation sound of some mechanical arm in a distant corridor, vibration climbing the reinforced concrete to her soles, cold enough to tighten her spine. She lifted her gaze, eyes passing through the hologram’s blue shadow to the flashing red warning light above the doorframe: it blinked at 1 Hz, as if counting down to some unknown protocol. Her breath caught for a beat, then she inhaled deeply, fingertips pressing slightly harder against the metal frame, feeling a faint warmth return—like a response, or perhaps a warning.
