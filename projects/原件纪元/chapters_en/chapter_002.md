# Chapter 2: Gray Market Broker's Dynamic Tattoo

The settlement terminal’s fans droned a low‑frequency hum, blowing the sweet‑metallic scent of coolant into the nostrils. The faint chill seeping from the metal grille brushed the fingertips like a thin sheet of ice. E‑11 heard the subtle electric pulse from the dark‑blue tattoo on Zhao Tie’s neck—green flashing, yellow jumping—each color shift accompanied by a minute 3.2 unit fluctuation in the account balance. She did not enter the rest pod; instead she halted before the terminal, boot soles thudding dully on the alloy floor, the echoes bouncing back and forth in the narrow corridor.  

A faint tremor on the inner wrist—quota credited **0.03 units** (account 0.25 → 0.28)—and instantly a retinal projection flared red: “Risk Score 78”. In the same second the subway gate, emergency‑room queue, and self‑service dispenser were all flagged “Denied”.  

Zhao Tie didn’t look up; his fingertips slid across the screen, the prompt crawling up the bone—Verification Quota deducted **0.3 kWh (W‑1)**, credit **0.02 units**. His voice was crisp, like unscrewing a bolt: “Order received, money in, go.”  

E‑11 watched the tattoo’s color change, mentally calculating: account 0.28 → −0.02 = 0.26, still **0.04** units shy of the freeze line **0.30 units**, yet the system treated “two points” as the nearest alert threshold. Breath came sharply amid the ventilation’s drone; fingertips unconsciously traced the terminal’s metal edge, the cold touch climbing the hand’s back, the fingertip pads feeling the faintly raised weld spots, tinged with a stale machine‑oil odor.  

“ORD‑X9’s provenance.” She met Zhao Tie’s eyes, tone flat, no embellishment.  

Zhao Tie’s mouth curled into a cold grin; the tattoo snapped red, the inner wrist pulsed again—**Quota Freeze countdown 48 h** activated. “Want to know? Pay 0.5 % of the original neural signal.” He pressed a finger on the confirmation key, eyes never leaving the screen. “Violation (W‑5). But I have a channel; the Black Box Alliance needs this data for model training.”  

E‑11’s throat tightened; the current sensation at the scar on her left forearm intensified, as if invisible hands were pulling at nerves. She knew the platform forbade private backup or resale of physiological metrics harvested during collection—yet refusal would push her Risk Score higher before the next harvest, locking gates, ER, and dispensers. The “produce emotion on schedule” muscle memory drilled in the harvest pods made compromise habitual, but the price this time was deeper neural damage; the one‑time occupational Neural Fibrosis compensation **0.2 units** had already been exhausted last month.  

“Deal.” Two words, fingertip pressing confirm on the terminal. The inner wrist pulsed twice, then a three‑second low tone—quota deducted **0.04 units** (account 0.26 → 0.22), Verification Quota **0.3 kWh** logged to the Provenance Layer (W‑1). A holographic “Work Order Generated” popped up, red characters burning onto the retinal field like a blood‑colored scar at the edge of vision.  

Zhao Tie withdrew his hand; the tattoo reverted to green, speech accelerating: “Order sourced from the ‘Master Copy Audit’ interface, Auditor K‑9 Han Ce issued it personally. Harvest permit auto‑renews quarterly (W‑4), but he bypassed the permit and attached an audit tag directly. The fear fragment you want—system doesn’t have it.” He paused, glancing at the flashing “Risk Score 78” on the terminal. “Predictive Governance has already flagged you ‘High‑Risk Reserve’; two more points and the gate locks.”  

E‑11 said nothing, merely brought her wrist to the terminal; a low‑frequency hum resonated in the palm, syncing with a heartbeat‑like countdown. The current sensation beneath the scar crawled along vessels toward fingertips; Neural Fibrosis pain reminded her: threshold **12 mC** breached, subsequent rehab uninsured.  

She stopped at the gate; retinal red still flashing: “Risk Score 78, Quota Freeze countdown 48 h”. The gate’s lattice scanned her pupil; a synthetic voice, metallic and raspy, announced: “E‑11, identification passed, but quota insufficient. Recharge within 48 h or trigger W‑5 lock.”  

She didn’t turn; fingertip brushed the hidden interface at her waist, the cold alloy edge warming slightly under the pad, a warm data line sliding beneath like a coiled silver snake. An “emergency quota” script already cached in the Black Box Alliance’s darknet—inject **0.05 units** quota (equivalent **0.15 kWh** Verification Quota). As the script loaded, the terminal screen glowed a pale blue; the fingertip reflected a faint image.  

“Recharge complete, quota **0.30 units**.” The synthetic voice repeated, flat as a log entry.  

The gate’s lattice turned green again; the mechanical arm retracted slowly, the metal grille emitting a deep “clack”, like a giant beast’s satisfied sigh after swallowing prey. E‑11 stepped across, boots meeting the cold deck once more; the ventilation drone persisted, ozone thicker now, laced with a faint whiff of burnt insulation varnish.  

At the corridor’s end, a heavy door marked “Master Copy Audit·K‑9” stayed shut; an audit tag pulsed a weak red light on the frame, the glow jumping across metal like a predator’s eye. From a pocket she produced a worn nanocapsule, fingertips gripping the微凸 ridges; the capsule’s micro‑LED flashed once under the pad—last exchange from the Black Box Alliance, a “Neural Buffer”, only able to temporarily suppress fibrosis pain at the cost of a **0.07 kWh** drop in next harvest’s emotional output.  

She pressed the capsule into the sub‑dermal injector on her left forearm; a brief sting melted into a cool wave spreading through vessels, the nerve‑end current dulling as if wrapped in thin mist. Retinal projection flashed green: “Neural Buffer active, 6 h remaining”.  

At that moment Zhao Tie’s neck tattoo flared crimson; the inner wrist delivered a sharp, brief vibration—Quota Freeze countdown **48 h** now at **2 h**, **46 h** left. The terminal’s red “Work Order Generated” hologram still pulsed; E‑11’s gaze locked on the red characters, heartbeat syncing with the countdown, fingertips again brushing the cold metal edge.  

“K‑9 audit door ready, awaiting authorization.” The synthesized voice seeped from wall speakers, laced with a crackle of static, like an old tape rewinding. E‑11 raised her left hand, fingertip hovering over the red tag, the微凸 nano‑keys cold under the pad, a faint warmth bleeding from the alloy as data streamed silently beneath, a noiseless silver thread.  

“Authorization code: E‑11‑07‑A.” She whispered, voice dry, rasped from long ozone inhalation. The tag’s blinking shifted to steady green; the door’s hydraulic cylinders groaned a heavy “boom”, the massive leaves sliding inward to reveal a corridor outlined in pale blue light, air thick with coolant scent and a trace of burnt insulation.  

Zhao Tie’s silhouette appeared behind the door, shoulder exoskeleton joints catching the dim light with a cold metallic sheen. He said nothing, only slipped a worn data crystal into E‑11’s palm; its micro‑LED flickered once in the palm lines then died. A subtle resonance passed between their wrists, a silent handshake.  

“Quota still short **0.02 units**, **46 h** left.” Zhao Tie’s voice low, edged with the grind of mechanical joints. “Black Box’s buffer only lasts till audit ends; after that you’re on your own.”  

E‑11 clenched the crystal, feeling the faint internal pulse; retinal green text scrolled: “Temporary authorization written, countdown synced”. She nodded, eyes scanning the audit tag flickering at the corridor’s far end, footsteps echoing heavily on the metal deck, steady and resolute.  

The door sealed; the tag’s glow settled into a constant green on her retina. E‑11 felt the crystal warm in her palm, a miniature star beating in her hand. She didn’t look back, letting that green light burn in her eyes, stride unbroken, moving deeper into the blue‑lit corridor.
