# Chapter 2: The Gray Market Broker's Dynamic Tattoo

The settlement terminal’s fans droned at a low frequency, blowing the metallic, sweet scent of coolant into the nostrils. The faint chill seeping from the metal grille clung to fingertips like a thin sheet of ice. E‑11 heard the subtle electric pulse from the dark‑blue tattoo on Zhao Tie’s neck — green flashing, yellow jumping — each color shift accompanied by a minute fluctuation of 3.2 units in the account balance. She did not enter the rest pod; she stopped before the terminal, boots thudding dully on the alloy floor, “thud, thud,” the echoes bouncing back and forth in the long corridor.  

A faint tremor on the inner wrist — Quota Credited **0.03 units** (account 0.25 → 0.28) — followed instantly by a retinal projection flashing red “Risk Score 78”. In the same second the subway gate, emergency triage, and self‑serve cafeteria were all marked “Denied”.  

Zhao Tie didn’t look up; his fingertips swept the screen, the prompt climbing the bone surface — Verification Quota Deduction **0.3 kWh (W‑1)**, Credited **0.02 units**. His voice was crisp, like unscrewing a bolt: “Order received, money in, move.”  

E‑11 watched the tattoo’s color change, mentally calculating: account 0.28 → −0.02 = 0.26, still **0.04** units shy of the freeze line **0.30 units**, yet the system treated only “two points” as the nearest alert threshold. Breath sounded unusually clear amid the ventilation hum. Fingertips unconsciously brushed the terminal’s metal edge, the cold touch crawling up the hand, the fingertip pads even feeling the raised weld spots, tinged with a stale machine‑oil odor.  

“ORD‑X9’s provenance.” She met Zhao Tie’s eyes, tone flat, no embellishment.  

Zhao Tie’s mouth curled into a cold grin; the tattoo flared red, the inner wrist trembled again — **Quota Freeze Countdown 48 h** activated. “Want to know? Pay 0.5 % of the raw neural signal.” He pressed a finger on the transaction confirm key, eyes never leaving the screen. “Violation (W‑5). But I have a channel; the Black Box Alliance needs this batch for model training.”  

E‑11’s throat tightened; the electric sensation at the scar on her left forearm intensified, as if invisible hands were pulling at nerves. She knew the platform forbade private backup or resale of physiological indicators generated by harvesting — yet refusal would push her Risk Score higher before the next harvest, locking gates, triage, and cafeteria. The “produce emotion on schedule” muscle memory drilled in the harvest pods made compromise habitual, but this time the price was deeper neural damage; the one‑time compensation for occupational Neural Fibrosis **0.2 units** had been exhausted last month.  

“Deal.” She uttered the two words, fingertip pressing confirm on the terminal. The inner wrist pulsed twice, then a three‑second low hum — Quota Deduction **0.04 units** (account 0.26 → 0.22), Verification Quota **0.3 kWh** logged to the Provenance Layer (W‑1). A holo‑projection popped up **“Ticket Generated”**, red characters burning like a blood‑red scar at the edge of vision.  

Zhao Tie withdrew his hand; the tattoo returned to green, speech accelerating: “Order sourced from the ‘Source Audit’ interface, Auditor K‑9 Han Ce issued directly. Harvest License auto‑renews quarterly (W‑4), but he bypassed the license and mounted an audit tag. The fear fragment you want — none exists in the system.” He paused, glance flicking to the flashing “Risk Score 78” on the terminal. “Predictive Governance has already flagged you ‘High‑Risk Preliminary’; two more points and the gate locks.”  

E‑11 said nothing, only brought her wrist near the terminal; a low‑frequency hum rose from the palm, like a heartbeat‑synced countdown. The electric sensation beneath the scar crawled along vessels toward fingertips; Neural Fibrosis pain reminded her: threshold **12 mC** breached, subsequent recovery unguaranteed.  

She halted at the gate; retinal red still blinked: “Risk Score 78, Quota Freeze Countdown 48 h”. The gate’s grating scanned her pupil; a synthetic voice, metallic and raspy, intoned: “E‑11, identification passed, but quota insufficient. Please recharge quota within 48 h, otherwise W‑5 level lock will trigger.”  

She didn’t turn; fingertip brushed the hidden interface at her waist, the cold alloy edge under the fingertip, a warm data line sliding beneath like a coiled silver snake. She had long stashed an “emergency quota” script in the Black Box Alliance’s darknet cache — inject **0.05 units** quota (equivalent **0.15 kWh** Verification Quota). As the script loaded, the terminal screen washed with a pale blue glow, a faint reflection appearing on the fingertip.  

“Recharge complete, quota **0.30 units**.” The synthetic voice spoke again, flat as a log entry.  

The gate’s grating turned green again; the mechanical arm retracted, the metal barrier emitting a deep “clack”, like a giant beast’s satisfied sigh after swallowing prey. E‑11 stepped across, boots hitting the cold deck once more; the ventilation hum persisted, ozone thicker now, laced with a faint scent of burnt insulation varnish.  

At the corridor’s end, a heavy door marked “Source Audit·K‑9” stood shut; its frame bore a faintly pulsing red audit tag, the red light dancing on metal like an eye fixed on prey. She withdrew a worn nanocapsule from her pocket, fingertips pinching the微微 raised texture on its rim; the tiny LED on its surface flashed once under her fingertip — the “neural buffer” bought last time from the Black Box Alliance, able only to temporarily suppress fibrosis pain, at the cost of a **0.07 kWh** drop in the next harvest’s emotional output.  

She pressed the capsule into the subcutaneous injection port on her left forearm; a sharp sting melted into a cool wave spreading through vessels, the nerve‑end electric sensation instantly dimming, as if wrapped in a thin mist. Retinal projection flashed green: “Neural buffer active, remaining validity 6 h”.  

At that moment, Zhao Tie’s neck tattoo snapped red; a sharp, brief vibration shot from his inner wrist — **Quota Freeze Countdown 48 h** now in its **2nd hour**, **46 h** remaining. On the terminal screen, the red **“Ticket Generated”** holo‑projection still blinked; E‑11’s gaze locked on the red characters, heartbeat syncing with the countdown, fingertip again unconsciously tracing the cold metal edge.  

“K‑9 audit door ready, awaiting authorization.” The synthetic voice seeped from wall speakers, tinged with a static hiss like an old tape rewinding. E‑11 raised her left hand, fingertip hovering over the red tag on the doorframe; the fingertip touched the微微 raised nano‑keypad, cold alloy edge exuding a faint warmth, data flowing silently beneath like a voiceless silver thread.  

“Authorization code: E‑11‑07‑A.” She spoke low, voice dry, raspy from long ozone inhalation. The red tag’s light shifted from pulsing to steady green; the door’s hydraulic cylinder groaned a heavy “boom”, the massive metal leaf sliding inward to reveal a corridor outlined in soft blue light, air thick with coolant scent and a hint of burnt insulation.  

Zhao Tie’s figure appeared behind the door; his exoskeleton joints caught the dim light, refracting a cold metallic sheen. He said nothing, simply slipped a worn data crystal into E‑11’s palm; the crystal’s tiny LED flashed once in the palm lines, then dimmed. A subtle resonance passed between their wrists — a silent handshake.  

“Quota still short **0.02 units**, **46 h** left.” Zhao Tie’s voice was low, edged with the rasp of mechanical joints. “The Black Box buffer only lasts until audit ends; after that you’re on your own.”  

E‑11 clenched the crystal, feeling the faint internal current pulse; retinal projection flashed green: “Temporary authorization written, countdown synced”. She nodded, eyes scanning the blinking audit tag at the corridor’s far end, footsteps echoing heavily and steadily on the metal floor.  

The door sealed; the red tag’s light resolved into a steady green on her retina. E‑11 felt the crystal warm in her palm, like a miniature star beating in her hand. She did not look back, only let that green glow burn in her eyes, stride unbroken, moving deeper into the blue‑lit corridor.
