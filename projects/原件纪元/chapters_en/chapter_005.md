# Chapter 5: Blind Spot Coordinates and the Noise of the Shared Cabin

E‑11’s boots struck the galvanized grating of the maintenance corridor, each step squeezing out a crisp “click.” The ozone scent was thicker than in the harvest pods, laced with a burnt‑insulation char that stabbed the nostrils. The low‑frequency drone of the turbine fans at the corridor’s ceiling pressed against the eardrums, while condensation dripping from the metal pipes fell onto the grating with a clear “drip, drip,” like a heartbeat tapping inside steel ribs.  

Chen Mo (E‑23) leaned against the opposite grating, eyes bloodshot, the faint fluorescent after‑glow of nanoprobes still clinging to the roots of his lashes; his breath carried a scorched edge. He whispered a string of coordinates: “Row seven, pod three, monitoring blind spot 0.8 meters, left turn three meters at the maintenance hatch.” His voice was hoarse, like sandpaper on metal. E‑11 nodded; a faint tremor on the inner wrist signaled **Quota Credited 0.00, Verification Quota deducted per regulation (W‑1)**.  

The two moved forward through the cramped, winding passage. Pipework wept condensation that fell onto the metal floor with the same clear “drip, drip.” Each drop blossomed into a tiny echo in the darkness. Ahead, the surveillance probes had already been masked by Chen Mo’s magnetic patches; their infrared indicators were dead, leaving only the maintenance lights casting a sickly white glow that stretched into needle‑thin points along the pipe walls. E‑11’s heart hammered at 160 bpm; the biometric dashboard flickered: **Anger 88%**, **Fear 81%**, the **Volatility Index** redline flashing. A thin sheen of sweat coated her palms; cold air slipped between her fingers like a swarm of microscopic ice needles threading through her veins, and she could almost hear the blood whispering in the capillaries at her fingertips.  

The entrance to the underground **Compute Layer** was a massive alloy door, its frame embedded with a **Quota Settlement Terminal**. The terminal emitted a three‑second low‑frequency hum, displaying **“Quota Freeze·Risk Score 84 (C‑9)”**. E‑11 pressed her palm to the frame; the metal chill climbed her palm lines to her wrist, the faint vibration at her fingertips feeling like a taut string trembling in the night. The lock gave a dull thud and slid open a crack. Cold air, heavy with machine‑oil and the same burnt‑insulation char, rushed in, stabbing the throat. She instinctively held her breath, a low swallow rattling in her throat, the ozone lingering sharper than before.  

Server racks formed a maze; indicator lights blinked like stars, fan roar reverberating in the narrow space into a continuous low‑frequency white noise that numbed the jaw and seemed to vibrate the very bones. She inserted the chip into the nearest audit node interface. E‑11 looked up. The **Mirror Domain** public screen still flickered at a saturation 30 % higher than reality; fan‑frequency vibrations hid in the noise, color blocks leaving afterimages on the retina like an over‑exposed negative. Data streams continued scrolling at the screen’s edge, ignored; only the fans’ roar filled the silence, occasionally punctuated by the low pulse of a distant coolant pump.  

She inhaled a lungful of ozone‑and‑oil‑laden air, chest rising, and stepped toward the exit. Footsteps echoed on the metal floor, like a heartbeat striking the brink of collapsing rules. Ahead, the airtight door stood half‑closed; a faint blue arc leaked from the frame’s edge, ozone drilling into the nostrils, tightening the throat; each breath even tasted the bitter scorch of molten metal.  

Chen Mo’s silhouette halted at the threshold, still gripping the now‑cooled chip, fingertips tracing its edge with a sandpaper‑like rasp. He did not turn; his voice was swallowed by the fans, leaving only the low resonance transmitted through his palm: “The purge will wipe all data streams marked as ‘Source Copy’, including the coordinates you stored.”  

E‑11 stopped, her chest’s rise and fall stark in the red light. She returned the chip to her waist pocket; the clasp’s “click” echoed down the corridor’s end, like a belated verdict. “The coordinates aren’t in the data,” she said low, breath misting, “they’re in the nerves.”  

Chen Mo finally turned. The eyes behind his mask glinted with two cold blues in the red glow. The door hinge gave a heavy thud; the airtight door slid shut. The **High‑Risk Flag** purge lights flared at the seam, strobing red slicing the corridor into blood‑colored shards, accompanied by a shrill buzzer proclaiming that this blind sector was now permanently sealed.
