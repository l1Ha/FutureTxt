# Chapter 5: Blind‑Spot Coordinates and the Pod‑Mate’s Noise

E‑11’s boot soles struck the galvanized grating of the maintenance corridor, each step squeezing out a crisp “click.” The ozone scent was thicker here than in the harvest pods, laced with a burnt‑insulation bitterness that stabbed the nostrils. The low‑frequency drone of the turbine fans at the corridor’s ceiling pressed against the eardrums, while condensation beading on the metal pipes fell onto the grating with a clear “drip, drip,” like a heartbeat tapping inside steel ribs.  

Chen Mo (E‑23) leaned against the opposite grating, eyes bloodshot, the faint fluorescent after‑glow of nanoprobes still clinging to his lashes; his breath carried a scorched edge. He whispered a string of coordinates in a voice like sandpaper on metal: “Row 7, Pod 3, surveillance blind spot 0.8 meters, left turn three meters at the maintenance hatch.” E‑11 nodded; the inner side of her wrist gave a faint tremor, signalling a quota credit of 0.00, the Verification Quota deducted per regulation (W‑1).  

The two moved forward through the narrowing passage. Pipework wept condensation that pattered on the metal floor, each droplet blooming into a tiny echo in the dark. Ahead, the surveillance probes had already been masked by Chen Mo’s magnetic patches; their infrared LEDs were dead, only the maintenance lamps casting a sickly pallor that stretched into needle‑thin cones on the pipe walls. E‑11’s heart hammered at 160 bpm; her wearable dashboard flashed **Anger 88 %**, **Fear 81 %**, the volatility redline blazing. A thin film of sweat coated her palms; cold air slipped between her fingers like a swarm of microscopic ice needles crawling through her veins, and she could almost hear the blood whispering in the finest capillaries.  

The entrance to the subterranean compute layer was a massive alloy door, its frame embedded with a Quota Settlement Terminal. The terminal emitted a three‑second low‑frequency hum, displaying **“Quota Freeze·Risk Score 84 (C‑9).”** E‑11 pressed her palm to the frame; the metal chill climbed the lines of her hand, a micro‑vibration at her fingertips like a taut string trembling in night. The lock gave a dull thunk and slid open a slit; a rush of cold air, heavy with machine‑oil and burnt‑insulation fumes, struck her face. She instinctively held her breath, a low swallow rising in her throat, the ozone lingering sharper than before.  

Server racks formed a labyrinth; indicator lights blinked like stars, fan roar reverberating in the long space as a continuous low‑frequency white noise that set teeth on edge and made bone seem to resonate. E‑11 drew a storage chip from her waist; its surface was warm, and a faint vibration sang through her fingertip like a plucked string echoing deep in bone. She inserted the chip into the nearest audit‑node interface. The screen instantly scrolled a raw log in green characters: **“K‑9 ordered: synthesize fear to fill training‑set gap, tagged as E‑11 Master Copy.”** Background noise points hid the fan‑synced hum. Each line’s scroll was accompanied by a faint electric sigh, as if the data itself were breathing; the screen’s edge glow left a pale green afterimage on her retina.  

Chen Mo stood beside her, hands pressed to his temples, eyes closed, then opened to a blank stare. He deliberately noise‑ified his own neural signals—skin conductance, magnetoencephalography, trace hormone levels—pushing them to random peaks. The audit sampler captured a chaos field; the log recorded **“Data anomaly·Sampling interrupted.”** The upper‑right corner read **“Remaining usable quota 0.00·Data integrity check passed.”** E‑11 felt the chip warm in her palm; the fingertip vibration steadied, like a dormant cicada trembling beneath its wings, while beads of sweat condensed into tiny droplets on the metal.  

In two seconds Chen Mo’s body temperature plunged three degrees; his pupils dilated to maximum. The wrist prompt shifted from a low drone to a piercing shriek, then cut out, leaving a single dull low tone reverberating inside E‑11’s skull—Chen Mo’s final heartbeat sync before entering **Silent Mode**.  

Far off, alarm strobes flared, red light refracting through the pipework. A synthetic voice broadcast: **“High‑risk Purge initiated. Relevant personnel proceed to designated zones immediately.”** The sound bounced off metal walls, hollow and echoing, as though the entire compute layer were shuddering for the coming purge. Chen Mo repeated quietly, **“Pain is right.”** His tone was flat, like confirming routine maintenance, yet edged with an almost imperceptible weariness. He rubbed the chip’s edge, as if sealing a transaction, then slipped it back into his waist pocket; the metal clasp’s “click” rang clear above the fan roar, echoing in the pipe gaps.  

E‑11 looked up. The Mirror Domain public screen still flickered at a saturation thirty percent higher than reality; noise points concealed the fan‑synced vibration, color blocks burning afterimages onto her retina like an over‑exposed negative. Data streams continued scrolling at the edges, ignored; only the fan roar filled the silence, punctuated occasionally by the deep pulse of a distant coolant pump.  

She inhaled a breath mingling ozone and machine oil, chest rising, and stepped toward the exit. Her footfalls echoed on the metal deck like a heartbeat striking the crumbling edge of rule. Each step carried a faint metallic scrape; her knee joints emitted a soft “creak” on every landing, reminding her that her body was nearing its limit.  

The fans’ low growl receded behind her; the corridor’s curved lighting sliced the red alarm into broken blood‑strip segments. With every bootfall E‑11 heard a minute “creak” of loosening screws, a reminder that this passage had long exceeded its load. Ahead, the airtight door stood half‑closed; a thin blue arc of electricity seeped from the frame’s edge, ozone drilling into her nostrils, tightening her throat, each breath tasting of molten metal’s bitter ash.  

Chen Mo’s silhouette halted at the threshold, the cooled chip still pinched between his fingers, fingertips rasping the edge with a sandpaper hiss. He did not turn; his voice was swallowed by the fans, leaving only a low resonance from his palm: **“The purge program will erase every data stream marked ‘Master Copy,’ including the coordinates you stored.”**  

E‑11 stopped, her chest’s rise stark in the red glow. She returned the chip to her waist pocket; the clasp’s “click” reverberated down the corridor’s end, like a belated verdict. **“The coordinates aren’t in the data,”** she said low, breath forming a thin mist, **“they’re in the nerves.”**  

Chen Mo finally turned; behind his mask two cold blue points flickered in the red light. He extended a hand, palm pressing to the door frame’s electric arc; blue light raced up his fingers and vanished, leaving a faint scorch mark glowing on the metal. **“Then cut the nerves,”** he said calmly, his fingertip leaving another faint scorch, **“or turn yourself into noise, never to be sampled.”**  

The door hinge gave a heavy thud; the airtight door slid shut. Red purge alarms flared at the seam, strobing the corridor into blood‑colored slices, accompanied by a piercing buzzer—declaring this blind zone sealed forever.
