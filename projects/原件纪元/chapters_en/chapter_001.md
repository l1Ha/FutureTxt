# Chapter 1: The Disinfectant Smell of the Harvest Pod

The hatch slid open, and the acrid tang of scorched insulation varnish mixed with ozone stabbed into the nostrils. The chill of the metal bulkhead crawled up the spine to the fingertips, and the silver‑gray scar on the inner left forearm tingled with a faint current. Ultra‑fine metal dust floated in the air; each breath felt like swallowing a mouthful of cold rust flakes, the throat parched as if it had taken a handful of sandpaper. The wrist trembled—**Quota Credited 0.03 units**, the system automatically deducted 12 % Compute Maintenance Tax (0.0036) and 5 % Provenance Storage Fee (0.0015), net gain **0.0249**, balance refreshed to **0.25**. E‑11 aligned the gray barcode at the nape with the scanner; the laser swept across the retina, leaving a green afterimage. The screen popped up ID **E‑11** and cumulative harvest time **4 312 hours**. Weak blue light refracted into tiny specks in the pupil, as if every pixel whispered of unauthorized experiments; the specks danced on the iris like silent Morse code.

The targeted neural stimulation array injected nanoprobes; a numb current spread along the neural‑fibrosis scar. She clenched her molars, the temporomandibular joint emitting a dull pop, forcing the nonexistent “fear” into consciousness: a dark corridor, dead emergency lights, the sweet‑cloying scent of nutrient paste mingling with disinfectant when her mother collapsed at the distribution point, even the pale yellow liquid seeping across the floor rendered in perfect detail. Skin conductance spiked to **1.84 µS**, heart rate surged to **112**, the purity needle pushed to **92 %**, the volatility redline extinguished, the screen’s red fading back to gray‑green. Every pulse seemed to strike the metal ribs of the pod; vibration traveled up the bone to the wrist, becoming a silent count. Fingertips trembled on the metal surface, as if they could feel the temperature of the data stream.

A red line flashed at the center of the screen: **Risk Score 78, 2 points from Freeze (W‑2)**. E‑11’s fingertips went cold; the chill crept up the palm to the wrist. The next badge‑in at the gate would most likely be rejected, that monotonous “beep—beep” refusal tone echoing. She instinctively clenched her fist, nails digging into the palm, trying to use pain to detach from the impending freeze. The instant the nails broke skin, blood beads were instantly wicked away by nanofibers, leaving only a dark red imprint.

The harvest log wrote itself to the Provenance Layer; Verification Quota Deduction **0.3 kWh (W‑1)**, a new order **ORD‑X9** appeared at the bottom, annotated: “E‑11 Exclusive Fear·Source Audit”. She had never seen this data, never been asked to reproduce a “fear that does not exist.” Pale blue light again refracted into fine specks in her pupil; throat dry, tongue pressed to the roof of the mouth, she tried to swallow a nonexistent mouthful of saliva. The throat felt cinched by a thin cord; each swallow accompanied by a faint metallic scrape.

The hatch closed slowly, disinfectant smell wrapped in ozone seeping into the nasal cavity, mingling with the faint scent of scorched insulation. E‑11 raised a hand to press the scar on her left forearm; the faint current at her fingertips reminded her: neural fibrosis accumulated **12 mC (W‑10)**, the platform granted a one‑time **0.2 units** medical quota, everything thereafter was on her own. She closed her eyes, listening to the low‑frequency hum of the ventilation fan reverberating inside the metal shell, like a countdown heartbeat. Every rotor cut reminded her: oxygen circulation, temperature control, even survival quota—all quantified and sliced by the same precise algorithm, even the humidity locked at a rigid 38 %.

“Do you need me to transfer quota to the maintenance crew?” A synthetic voice seeped from the ceiling speaker, code **A‑7**, tone flat and temperatureless, like a prerecorded command echoing in vacuum. E‑11 didn’t answer; she merely lifted her right hand, fingertips hovering over the touch panel, **ORD‑X9** blinking “E‑11 Exclusive Fear·Source Audit”. The panel’s nano‑coating shimmered under her fingertips, as if awaiting a confirmation ritual; the halo expanded and contracted with her breathing rhythm.

“Source Audit…” she whispered, the sound flattened between metal walls, carrying a metallic echo. The memory of that fear surged again; skin conductance spiked, heart rate hit **118**, the purity needle trembled at **93 %**. Something in her chest contracted—not the heart, but the survival instinct quantified by algorithm, a spring drawn tight, ready to snap.

The ventilation fan continued its low hum. E‑11 stood; her knee emitted a faint “click”, joint lubricant thickening in the cold, like congealed glue binding bone. She pressed the left‑forearm scar against the bulkhead, feeling the weak current conduct through metal into bone, confirming the contract still held. Down the corridor came the distant gate’s monotonous “beep—beep” refusal, mixed with heavy footfalls; each sound struck her skull, reminding her of the fragile boundary of quota. Wall indicator lights pulsed at 0.7 Hz, casting a ghostly blue band that formed an illusory guide line at the corridor’s end.

“Next harvest, request ID **E‑12**.” She spoke to the empty pod, voice flat, as if confirming a ritual to herself. The gray‑green digits on the screen kept jumping: **Risk Score 78, 2 points from Freeze**, balance **0.25**. She turned her gaze to the timer bar above the hatch; numbers flickered in the dim light, as if each flash calculated the cost of her next breath; the stopwatch’s red graduations left afterimages on her retina.

A gust of cold air carrying lubricant oil and ozone drilled into the nasal cavity, slid down the trachea into the lung lobes. E‑11 pressed her right hand into the recessed groove of the bulkhead; fingertips touched the warm alloy texture, feeling current like tiny ants crawling under the skin. She inhaled deeply, pressed hard; the metal surface sparked a faint glow—the hatch slid open again. The gate at the corridor’s end emitted a low “beep—beep” refusal, mingled with the distant hydraulic screech of a maintenance manipulator arm. She stepped across the threshold; her knee joint clicked in the cold lubricant, like ancient gears meshing. Her eyes fell on the harvest station ahead: sample tubes neatly arrayed, each tube wall reflecting cold white indicator lights, waiting for the next extraction of fear.

The cold air from the vent, laced with lubricant oil and metallic ozone, drilled into the nostrils and slid down the trachea into the lung lobes; each breath felt like swallowing a mouthful of ice shards. The alloy floor transmitted a subtle vibration; the maintenance manipulator’s hydraulic pump growled in the distance, coolant striking pipe walls with a dull “thud—thud”, like a massive heart beating in darkness. E‑11’s fingertips remained in the bulkhead groove; the warm alloy pattern seeped through skin into marrow, current like ants crawling through vessels, reminding her the contract remained valid.

Before the harvest station, sample tubes stood in orderly rows; tube walls reflected ghostly blue indicator lights, the light refracting into saw‑tooth bands on the liquid surface. She reached for the nearest tube; the fingertip brushed the nano‑coating at the mouth, slippery with a hint of gritty static. A manipulator arm extended silently; the needle tip flashed cold white under the microscope, precisely piercing the tube mouth. The suction pump engaged; the liquid level dipped, emitting a barely audible “hiss” of airflow. The fear sample was quantified into a data stream, fed via fiber into the central core.

“Harvest complete, ID **E‑12**, Purity **94 %**, Risk Score **77**.” The synthetic voice seeped from the overhead speaker, still flat and temperatureless, laced with a faint electrical hiss. E‑11 didn’t look up; she simply returned the empty tube to the rack, fingertips grazing the metal edge, leaving a shallow scratch. Green numbers on the screen jumped: **Balance 0.24**, **1 min 58 s to Freeze**.

The gate at the corridor’s end sounded its low “beep—beep” refusal again; a red afterimage lingered on the retina. She pressed the left‑forearm scar against the bulkhead once more, feeling the weak current conduct through metal into bone, confirming the contract still held. Her knee joint clicked in the cold lubricant, like ancient gears meshing; she leaned forward, stepped toward the next airtight door. Behind her the fan continued its low hum, as if pricing every breath.
