# Chapter 1: The Antiseptic Scent of the Harvest Pod

The hatch slid open and a sour bite of scorched insulation varnish, laced with ozone, stabbed her nostrils. The chill of the metal bulkhead crawled up her spine to her fingertips, and the silvery‑gray scar on the inner side of her left forearm tingled with a faint current. Ultra‑fine metal dust hung in the air; every breath felt like swallowing a mouthful of cold rust, her throat parched as if she had gulped sandpaper. A pulse at her wrist—**0.03 units** credited, the system automatically deducting a 12 % compute‑maintenance tax (0.0036) and a 5 % Provenance Layer storage fee (0.0015), net gain **0.0249**, balance refreshed to **0.25**. E‑11 aligned the gray barcode at her nape with the scanner; a laser swept her retina, leaving a green afterimage. The screen popped up **E‑11** and a cumulative harvest time of **4 312 hours**. Weak blue light refracted in her pupil into speckles, each pixel seeming to whisper of unauthorized experiments; the speckles danced on her iris like silent Morse.  

A targeted neural‑stimulation array injected nanoprobes; a numb current spread along the neural‑fibrosis scar. She clenched her molars, the temporomandibular joint clicking dully, forcing the nonexistent “fear” into consciousness: a dark corridor, dead emergency lights, the sweet‑cloying smell of nutrient paste at the distribution point mixed with antiseptic, even the pale‑yellow fluid seeping across the floor rendered in perfect detail. Skin conductance spiked to **1.84 µS**, heart rate surged to **112**, the purity needle pushed to **92 %**, the volatility red line extinguished, the screen’s red retreating to a gray‑green. Each pulse hammered the pod’s metal ribs; vibration traveled up the bone to her wrist, becoming a silent count, fingertips trembling on the alloy as if they could feel the temperature of the data stream.  

A red banner flashed in the center of the display: **Risk Score 78, 2 points from Freeze (W‑2)**. E‑11’s fingertips went cold, the chill creeping up her palm; the next gate swipe would most likely reject her with that monotonous “beep—beep”. She instinctively curled her fist, nails digging into her palm, trying to use pain to detach from the impending freeze; the nails broke skin, blood beaded and was instantly wicked away by nanofibers, leaving only a dark‑red imprint.  

The harvest log wrote itself to the Provenance Layer; Verification Quota deducted **0.3 kWh (W‑1)**. A new order **ORD‑X9** appeared at the bottom, annotated “E‑11 Exclusive Fear · Master Copy Audit”. She had never seen this data, never been asked to reproduce a “fear that does not exist”. Pale blue light again fractured in her pupil; throat dry, tongue pressed to the roof of her mouth, she tried to swallow saliva that wasn’t there, her throat tightening as if a thin cord were pulling, each swallow accompanied by a faint metallic scrape.  

The hatch sealed slowly; antiseptic and ozone seeped back, mingling with the faint odor of scorched varnish. E‑11 pressed her left forearm scar against the bulkhead; the faint current reminded her: neural fibrosis accumulated **12 mC (W‑10)**, the platform allots a one‑time **0.2 units** medical quota, everything thereafter is self‑care. She closed her eyes, listening to the low‑frequency hum of the ventilation fans reverberating inside the metal shell, like a countdown heartbeat. Every rotor cut reminded her: oxygen circulation, temperature control, even survival quota—all quantified and sliced by the same precise algorithm, humidity locked at a rigid 38 %.  

“Shall I transfer quota to the maintenance crew?” a synthetic voice seeped from the ceiling speaker, designation **A‑7**, tone flat, temperature‑less, like a prerecorded command echoing in vacuum. E‑11 did not answer; she merely lifted her right hand, fingertips hovering over the touch panel where **ORD‑X9** blinked “E‑11 Exclusive Fear · Master Copy Audit”. The panel’s nano‑coating shimmered under her fingertips, as if awaiting a confirmation ritual, the glow expanding and contracting with her breath.  

“Master Copy Audit…” she murmured, the words flattened between metal walls, echoing with metallic resonance. The memory of that fear surged again; skin conductance spiked, heart rate hit **118**, purity needle trembled at **93 %**. Something in her chest contracted—not her heart, but the survival instinct quantified by algorithm, a spring wound tight, ready to snap.  

The ventilation fans kept their low drone. E‑11 rose slowly; knees emitted a faint “click”, joint lubricant thickened in the cold, like congealed glue binding bone. She pressed her left forearm scar flush against the bulkhead, feeling the weak current conduct through metal into skeleton, confirming the contract still held. Down the corridor the gate’s monotonous “beep—beep” rejection sounded, mixed with heavy footfalls; each strike hammered her skull, reminding her of quota’s fragile border. Wall indicator lights pulsed at 0.7 Hz, casting a ghostly blue ribbon that formed an illusory guide line at the corridor’s end.  

“Next harvest round, request code **E‑12**.” she whispered into the empty pod, tone flat, as if confirming a ritual to herself. The gray‑green digits on the screen still flickered: **Risk Score 78, 2 points to Freeze**, balance **0.25**. She stared at the timer bar above the hatch; numbers jumped in the dim light, each flash seemingly calculating the cost of her next breath, the stopwatch’s red graduations burning afterimages on her retina.  

A gust of cold air, laced with lubricant and ozone, slipped through the vent, sliding down her trachea into the lungs. E‑11 set her right hand into the bulkhead recess, fingertips contacting the faintly warm alloy grain; current crawled like tiny ants beneath skin. She inhaled deeply, fingertips pressing slightly—metal surface glimmered— the hatch slid open again. The gate at the corridor’s far end emitted a low “beep—beep” rejection, blended with the distant hydraulic hiss of a repair arm. She stepped across the threshold; knees clicked in the cold lubricant, like ancient gears meshing. Her gaze fell on the harvest station ahead: sample tubes neatly arrayed, each wall reflecting cold white indicator lights, awaiting the next extraction of fear.  

The vent’s cold breath, carrying lubricant and ozone’s metallic tang, pierced her nostrils and slid down her airway; each inhalation felt like swallowing ice shards. The alloy floor transmitted a subtle vibration; the repair arm’s hydraulic pump groaned afar, coolant striking pipe walls with a dull “thud—thud”, like a massive heart beating in darkness. E‑11’s fingertips remained in the bulkhead recess, the warm alloy grain seeping through skin into marrow; current like ant columns in her vessels reminded her the contract persisted.  

Before the harvest station, tubes stood in orderly rows; their walls caught the dim blue indicator glow, light refracting on the liquid surface into jagged bands. She reached for the nearest tube; fingertip brushed the nano‑coated rim, slick yet with a faint gritty static. A mechanical arm extended silently, needle tip gleaming cold white under the microscope, piercing the tube mouth precisely; the suction pump engaged, the liquid meniscus dipped slightly, emitting a barely audible “hiss—” as the fear sample was quantified into a data stream and fed via fiber into the central core.  

“Harvest complete, code **E‑12**, purity **94 %**, Risk Score **77**.” The synthetic voice seeped from the overhead speaker, still flat, now tinged with the faintest electrical static. E‑11 did not look up; she simply returned the empty tube to its rack, fingertips grazing the metal edge, leaving a shallow scratch. Green numbers on the screen jumped: **balance 0.24**, **1 min 58 s to Freeze**.  

The gate at the corridor’s end sounded its low “beep—beep” rejection again; a red afterimage lingered on her retina. She pressed her left forearm scar once more against the bulkhead, feeling the weak current conduct through metal into bone, confirming the contract’s validity. Knees clicked in the cold lubricant, like ancient gears engaging; she leaned forward, stepped toward the next airtight door, the fans behind her still droning low, as if pricing every breath.
