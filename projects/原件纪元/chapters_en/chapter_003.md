# Chapter 3: The Vent in the Validator's Office Blows a Rust‑Tinged Cold Wind

The vent in the Validator's office blew a cold wind tinged with rust. Lin Wei's holo‑projector behind her ear flickered on, and a translucent hash chain rotated slowly in the air, its edges glowing with a pale blue aura. Her wrist trembled faintly—account balance refreshed to 0.20. E‑11 raised a hand to press the silver‑gray scar on the inner side of her left forearm; a faint current prickled at her fingertips, the neural‑fibrosis pain crawling like fine needles through muscle, tapping gently beneath the skin with each heartbeat. The cold wind slipped through her collar, whisking away a thin sheen of sweat and crystallizing into delicate frost flowers at her fingertips, a reminder that the cabin's climate control had failed during the last compute‑peak cycle.  

Lin Wei (ID V‑7) sat behind a translucent workstation; the micro holo‑projector behind her right ear pulsed once, the cursor halting at the end of the hash chain. Her voice was flat, each sentence beginning with a regulatory clause: “Per Clause W‑7, a compliant signature cannot take effect before the Source Audit clears. Auditor K‑9 has initiated the audit and demands the missing fear fragments be supplied within 24 hours.” Her fingertips hovered over the terminal panel; the faint scrape of nail on synthetic resin sounded like a whisper, as if she were weighing every character. The status lights on the bulkhead flickered a sickly pale blue, casting light on her wan cheek; her brow furrowed, as though she were re‑reading a block of code she had memorized long ago yet felt compelled to verify once more.  

E‑11 stared at the rotating hash, her breath unusually clear amid the ventilation ducts' hum. Her pupils contracted between the red numerals of the retinal projection; her throat moved once, voice even and unadorned: “The metrics are fine.” Her left hand absently rubbed the edge of the magnetic pocket at her waist; the fingertip brushed a thin residue of conductive gel, sticky and cold, a reminder of the haste of her last offline backup.  

Lin Wei's fingertip swept across the terminal; a prompt climbed the bone‑conduction display—account balance refreshed to 0.19. The holo hash jittered in the air, and a line of tiny text flashed at the projector's edge—“E11‑raw‑12y”—the raw brainwave backup Lin Wei kept offline, never committed to the ledger. She did not look up; her tone remained log‑like: “Conditional signature: the ‘raw fear brainwave’ must be uploaded before the audit for Provenance Layer writing, consuming 0.3 kWh of Verification Quota. Upon upload the Risk Score will be recalculated per W‑6.” Using her Validator authority, Lin Wei pre‑tagged an “Emergency Review” label in the backend, to be activated before the audit cleared. A faint ozone scent lingered in the cabin air, the by‑product of the cooling fans running at high speed, stinging the nostrils.  

The current at E‑11's left forearm scar spiked, as if invisible fingers were tugging at the nerves; pain radiated along the ulnar nerve toward her fingertips. She knew W‑6's decision logic: if the same biometric signature mapped to multiple provenance chains, or if a provenance‑chain hash mismatched the on‑chain record, the system would deem it forged—penalty: permanent Quota Zeroing, a High‑Risk Flag, and exclusion from all Platform Polity facilities. Yet if she refused to upload, her quota would be frozen before the next harvest; turnstiles, emergency bays, and auto‑vendors would all lock down. Her fingertip hovered over the cold confirmation key, feeling the static prick; heartbeat synced with the countdown, the pulse at her throat thudding as if it might burst the skin. Breathing grew shallow; each inhale carried a metallic cold, each exhale a white mist that vanished instantly in the projection's glow.  

“Upload.” E‑11 gritted her teeth and pressed the confirm key. The account balance slid to 0.18 on her retinal display; a low hum persisted, and the screen flashed “Risk Score 81·Passage Denied·Medical Downgrade to Tier 4.” The red characters burned on the retinal projection; the subway turnstile’s light grid snapped to red, the mechanical arm retracted, the metal grille emitted a deep “clack,” vibration traveling through the floor to her soles and up her spine. She felt the metal plate under her feet grow hot, as if current were seeping through her shoes into bone, a reminder that the system had marked her “untrusted.”  

Lin Wei's holo hash stopped rotating; the projector dimmed, leaving only the faint indicator behind her ear still blinking. She logged the ticket number into the terminal: “Estimated processing 14 days.” The tap of her fingertips sounded unusually crisp in the cabin, like a death knell for each irreversible ledger entry. The projected clock on the bulkhead ticked seconds; each tick accompanied the faint whine of fan blades slicing air, as if counting down an irrevocable deadline.  

E‑11 stood before the turnstile, the red characters on her wrist terminal pulsing in her vision. In the background, the low‑frequency drone of the Compute Layer’s cooling fans still penetrated the bulkhead; the Mirror Domain’s public screens displayed colors 30 % more saturated than reality, their edges flickering with compute‑layer thermal noise. She felt the current at the depths of her left forearm scar rise again; fingertips involuntarily traced the cold metal grille, sensing the faint texture of metal fatigue. Heartbeat matched the countdown, like a core about to overload, waiting silently for the next judgment. Passersby glanced briefly, then were swallowed by their own holo‑ads; no one lingered.  

Lin Wei's terminal lit a line of green text: “Ticket dispatched to ‘Black Box’ maintenance team; estimated completion of Provenance write within 14 days.” Her voice came through bone conduction, edged with a barely perceptible rasp: “If the upload is not completed before the audit, the quota will be permanently frozen, medical tier downgraded to Tier 5, and emergency channels locked as well.” Before the words faded, the indicator behind her ear blinked twice and went dark; the cabin was left with only the monotonous spin of the fan, a faint rust scent drifting in the air. Lin Wei tapped the terminal edge twice, as if sealing a ritual.  

The current at E‑11's left forearm scar surged again, as if tiny arcs danced across nerve endings. She clenched her jaw and pulled a worn “offline key” from the magnetic pocket at her waist—its surface scored with fine scratches, edges still smeared with conductive gel from the last harvest. Fingertips brushed the key’s edge; the feel was rough yet warm, like holding a stone that still breathed, the faint residual heat seeping into her palm. The key’s internal microchip vibrated beneath her fingertip, emitting an ultra‑low‑frequency hum only she could hear.  

“Take the dark corridor.” She said quietly to Lin Wei, tone flat, no embellishment. “I have an old channel that can push the raw brainwave into the Provenance Layer before the audit window closes; the cost is 0.5 kWh of reserve quota plus a Black Box write authorization.” Lin Wei’s pupils contracted amid the projected numbers; her fingertip swept the terminal, wrist trembling faintly—“Authorization code issued, valid for 6 hours.” Lin Wei’s voice remained even, yet her keystrokes carried force, as if she were sealing the code with an invisible stamp.  

E‑11 offered no thanks, turning and slipping into the maintenance passage beside the turnstile. Inside the narrow tube, condensation seeped from the walls; the drip‑tap of water blended with the fan’s hum into a monotonous rhythm, each droplet striking metal echoing a faint metallic reverberation. She pressed the key to the wall interface; a faint current jumped the metal contacts, and the screen read “Writing…”. Red characters flickered on her retina; heartbeat synced with the progress bar, the vessels at her throat throbbing as if about to rupture skin; sweat beads slid down her temples, instantly dried by the cold wind. The emergency hatch at the tube’s end slid open, revealing a dim chamber crammed with discarded compute modules. The air reeked of scorched circuitry and stale lubricant; faded “Compliance” plaques hung on the walls, edges worn smooth, lettering barely legible in the dim light. E‑11 exhaled a plume of white vapor, fingertip hovering over the final confirmation key, feeling a faint static prick—this time she did not wait for the countdown; she pressed it herself.  

The backup power indicator deep in the dark room suddenly went out; a synthetic voice seeped from the wall speakers: “Source Audit passed; remaining upload window 3 minutes.” E‑11’s pupils contracted sharply; her fingertip already pressed against the cold keyface, countdown overlapping heartbeat. The current at the depths of her left forearm scar abruptly stalled; the metal grille under her fingertips suddenly grew scorching, as if something waited on the other side. She heard the distant roar of a heavy maglev train, vibration traveling through the floor—like some colossal beast stirring in darkness—reminding her that time no longer belonged to anyone. She felt the metal grille in her palm grow hot, as if something on the other side was waiting for her.
