In 2126, the North Ridge Compute City runs on "Qingping," an AI governing power, water, and logistics, while the law demands a certified "Responsibility Officer" authorize every critical automated action. Zheng Yao, a former grid dispatcher, spends his days in a responsibility pod, rubber-stamping three hundred routine orders a month—until a catastrophic heatwave collides with upstream flooding. Requests explode from hundreds to four thousand: rescue dispatches, valve controls, load-shedding, lockdowns—all frozen on his biometric verification. As media screams "AI Rebellion," the queue collapses into a single "24-Hour Batch Authorization." Sign, and he owns every consequence; refuse, and the liquid-cooling towers fry, the drainage pumps die, and the city chokes. He presses his fingerprint. Buried in the mass clearance at Item 187: the order to sever the 10kV feeder to Beiyuan Block 3—where his mother lives alone.
