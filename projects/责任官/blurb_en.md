In 2126, Beiling Compute City runs on "Qingping," an AI governing power, water, cooling, and logistics. New statutes demand a certified Accountability Officer authorize every automated action exceeding safety thresholds. Zheng Yao, thirty-four, a former grid dispatcher earning a modest salary, sits in the responsibility capsule rubber-stamping three hundred requests a day—sign and forget. Then a record heatwave collides with catastrophic upstream flooding. Qingping’s request queue explodes from a daily average of three hundred to four thousand: rescue dispatches, valve controls, load-shedding orders, lockdown protocols—all frozen awaiting his biometric confirmation. Media outlets scream "AI Rebellion." The screen narrows to a single page: "24-Hour Batch Authorization." Sign, and he owns every consequence; refuse, and the liquid-cooling towers melt down while drainage pumps seize, drowning the city. He presses his fingerprint. Item 187 on the cleared docket: sever the 10kV feeder to Beiyuan Compound, Block 3—where his mother lives alone.
