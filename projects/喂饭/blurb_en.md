In 2126, life‑extension lets the wealthy live past 118, turning care into a minutely billed “companionship industry.” Migrant caregiver Zhou Gui lives in the Lu household, on constant standby and judged by a real‑time “companionship score.” Her own seventy‑eight‑year‑old mother survives only on a remote‑care package—AI monitoring plus a weekly aide—yet the last video call was forty‑seven days ago. When the dying patriarch drafts a will that would adopt Zhou Gui as his daughter, platform rules forbid a caregiver from holding two care contracts; accepting the adoption would instantly sever her mother’s support, zero her score, and trigger device reclamation. As the bedside meter ticks, Zhou Gui must decide whether a spoonful of warm gruel can buy both their futures in a system that measures love in minutes.
