In 2126, life‑extension technology has pushed the wealthy past a century, turning caregiving into a minute‑by‑minute “companionship” market. Migrant worker Zhou Gui lives in the Lu household, on constant call and scored on every interaction, while her seventy‑eight‑year‑old mother survives on a remote‑care package — AI monitoring plus a weekly aide — whose last video call was forty‑seven days ago. When the dying patriarch attempts to adopt Zhou Gui as his legal daughter, platform rules force an immediate severance of her mother’s care contract: loss of subsidies, a zeroed rating, and possible reclamation of the monitoring hardware. Trapped beside the bedside, with the billing meter ticking, Zhou Gui must decide whether a spoonful of warm gruel can buy both their futures.
