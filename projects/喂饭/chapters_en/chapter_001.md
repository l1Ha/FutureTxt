# Chapter 1: Feeding

When Zhou Gui slipped the first spoonful of nutrient paste into the old man’s mouth, her wrist terminal buzzed against the bone: billing started, 05:12, rating 4.62, 0.02 from downgrade.  

She gauged the paste’s temperature with the old scar on her right wrist — thirty‑eight degrees, half a degree below standard; she had to finish the remaining eleven spoonfuls before the next rating refresh and change the sheets before six.  

“Work Order 20260714‑001, Feeding, First Meal.” The synthetic voice emanated from the wrist terminal, edged with a metallic tremor. “Rating 4.62, 0.02 from downgrade.”  

She worked a full sixteen‑hour shift, billed at ¥1.80 per minute; after the Platform’s 22 % cut she took home roughly ¥1,350, while the Remote Care Package (RCP‑V3) back home deducted ¥2,100 a month. The first year she could manage both ends; the second year she reduced Mother’s “weekly hourly caregiver” to “bi‑weekly” — the four hours saved on the bill were never mentioned by Mother on the phone.  

Zhou Gui did not turn; the spoon extended toward the bedside old man. The micro physiological monitoring node behind Lu Lao’s left ear protruded faintly beneath the skin, like a faded pearl. His lips were cracked, the nasal‑tube strap leaving a red groove; his skin shone waxy under the lamp. She fed him quickly and gently, as if lubricating a precision instrument. His throat moved, the swallow drowned by the ventilator’s rhythm; each piston stroke shuddered the bed frame, a micro‑tremor reaching her fingertips.  

The last video call with Mother was forty‑seven days ago. That day her rating fell from 4.64 to 4.62, and the Platform switched her shift from live‑in to day shift, giving her an extra free afternoon. She dialed; Mother waved the phone in the yard hunting signal, the camera showing only eaves and sky — neither face was clear. The call lasted four minutes eleven seconds; the system auto‑hung up, its tone as calm as a checkout receipt.  

“Count one more minute.” She whispered internally; the wrist terminal buzzed once, deducting ¥0.12. The rating flickered: 4.61.  

After the seventh spoonful the old man closed his eyes and a low, rasping command rose from his throat: “Read the letter.”  

Zhou Gui set the bowl back on the bedside table, the chip still facing left. She pulled the yellowed stack of letters from the drawer; the edges were brittle as dry bark, each turn releasing a faint crackle, dust motes drifting in the shaft of light. The first was written thirty years ago to his late wife; the ink had seeped into the fibers, yet the script remained neat, the pen strokes leaving shallow impressions on the paper. She cleared her throat, lowering her voice just enough for the microphone to capture it without triggering a “tone anomaly” penalty in the rating.  

“Line… three… ‘The spring wind does not cross the Jade Gate Pass’…”  

“Work Order 20260714‑002, Companion Reading, duration 12 minutes.” The wrist terminal chimed again; rating slid to 4.60.  

Lu Lao’s fingers clawed at the blanket, nails scraping the cotton with a faint rustle, like dry branches rubbing in the wind. Zhou Gui knew he was waiting for the next line, and for the “Kinship Recognition” moment. Platform rules: a Temporary Grade caregiver may not hold two Care Relationships simultaneously. Once the old man signed the Digital Will, her Wrist Terminal would pop up “Identity Upgrade·Temporary→Permanent,” while Mother’s Remote Care Package back home would simultaneously display “Care Relationship Terminated·Device Reclamation Countdown 48 h.”  

She stole a glance at the tiny red dot in the upper‑right corner of her Wrist Terminal — Mother’s account was 47 days overdue, the Utilities‑Medical Linkage severed, the Device Reclamation Countdown still ticking. Last week when hourly caregiver Chen Zhongdian visited, Mother lay in bed, the fan droning, a rooster crowing in the yard; the video left only an unanswered voice message: “Gui’er, don’t burn the bowl.” The message carried the fan’s electrical hum and the rooster’s sharp crow, a thin thread stitching the two dawns together.  

“Which line are you on?” Lu Lao’s voice sounded like dry branches rubbing, edged with a hoarse tremor.  

“Twelfth line.” Zhou Gui turned the page; the paper crackled, its corner curling slightly at her fingertips.  

“Read it again.”  

She repeated, slowing her cadence, breathing in sync with the ventilator. The Wrist Terminal buzzed once per minute — deduction, rating, billing, countdown — all numbers flowing beneath her fingers. Rating 4.59.  

“Work Order 20260714‑003, Will Signature Confirmation — please check ‘All Care Obligations Fulfilled’ before the old man’s final breath.” The synthetic voice carried no affect, only its fixed prefix and the trailing “beep—beep.”  

Lu Lao’s fingers trembled toward the tablet; green clauses scrolled on screen: Article 4, 5, 7 of the Prohibition of Dual Care Relationships. His fingertip hovered over “Confirm,” nail blanched, the ridge pattern clear in the screen’s glare.  

The white scar on Zhou Gui’s right wrist left a fine pale line on the spoon handle. Cold sweat prickled her palm; the Wrist Terminal kept its one‑per‑minute pulse, rating frozen at 4.58.  

“Sign it.” Lu Lao’s mouth twitched, half smile, half cough, a short burst of air from his throat.  

The tablet lit up; the blockchain write‑complete tone pierced sharper than the Wrist Terminal: “Will recorded on Blockchain Ledger, Identity Upgrade effective.” Almost simultaneously the Wrist Terminal flashed two red lines:  

**Identity Upgrade·Temporary→Permanent**  

**Care Relationship Terminated·Device Reclamation Countdown 48 h**  

Timestamp synced: 2026‑07‑14 05:27:03.  

Zhou Gui stared at the two lines, fingertip hovering over “Confirm.” The Wrist Terminal’s beep‑beep remained metronomic, rating stuck at 4.58. Her breath became distinct within the ventilator rhythm; the scar on her right wrist warmed again, as if an invisible current lightly seared it.  

She did not press it.  

The Chipped Enamel Bowl remained on the bedside table, chip still leftward; the nutrient paste had cooled into a thin skin, a faint oil sheen on its surface. Zhou Gui set the spoon down slowly; metal met porcelain with a soft click, like the end of a ritual.  

She fished the old phone from her pocket — screen spider‑webbed with scratches, corners worn smooth, fingerprint smudges leaving a hazy glow. The dial screen held a single number — the Remote Care Package’s sole service hotline. Her fingertip hovered over the green call button; the display read “Calling,” backed by the fan’s whir and the rooster’s crow from Mother’s voice note, the fan blades slicing air and the rooster’s piercing cry weaving a rough duet.  

Beep—beep—  

The Wrist Terminal kept billing; rating unchanged. “Calling” blinked, no answer. Zhou Gui’s fingertip did not descend, frozen above the call key like an untuned string stretched tight in the pale morning light.

The fan’s whir was suddenly cut by a short busy tone, then a mechanical female voice: “The number you dialed is temporarily unavailable; please try again later.” The sound waves refracted in the cramped ward, mixed with the sharp scent of disinfectant and the sour tang of stale nutrient paste, drilling into Zhou Gui’s nostrils. Her fingertip still hovered over the green call button, the ridge pattern clear in the screen’s glare; the Wrist Terminal’s vibration persisted once per minute, rating immobile at 4.58.  

Lu Lao’s ventilator hissed rhythmically, like an old bellows pulling night air. His eyelids lifted slightly, pupils contracting in the weak dawn light; a low guttural “hm” rose from his throat, followed by a series of broken coughs, phlegm rattling wetly in his airway. He did not look at the tablet; his gaze settled on Zhou Gui’s right wrist, the scar’s white imprint on the spoon handle still faintly luminous.  

“Don’t wait.” Lu Lao’s voice was dry branches rubbing, edged with impatient rasp. “The system won’t wait for anyone; the countdown won’t wait either.”  

Zhou Gui’s throat tightened; her breath locked to the ventilator’s cadence. The Wrist Terminal’s beep‑beep tapped under her fingertips, each pulse striking like a heartbeat. She slowly withdrew her finger; the screen dimmed, the old phone slid back into her pocket, the worn plastic shell rasping against fabric. The nutrient‑paste skin in the Chipped Enamel Bowl gleamed with oil in the morning light; Zhou Gui lifted the thin film, cold air stabbing her fingertips, numbing them.  

“If I sign, I can’t leave.” She spoke slowly, each word weighed. “Device Reclamation Countdown forty‑eight hours; the retrieval truck arrives at 03:00.”  

Lu Lao’s fingers trembled on the tablet, nails blanched, ridge pattern sharp in the screen’s glare. He stared at “Confirm,” fingertip suspended, not descending. The tablet lit up; the blockchain write‑complete tone shrieked again: “Will recorded on Blockchain Ledger, Identity Upgrade effective.” Almost simultaneously the Wrist Terminal flashed two red lines:  

**Identity Upgrade·Temporary→Permanent**  

**Care Relationship Terminated·Device Reclamation Countdown 48 h**  

Timestamp synced: 2026‑07‑14 05:27:03.  

Zhou Gui stared at the two lines, fingertip hovering over “Confirm.” The Wrist Terminal’s beep‑beep remained metronomic, rating stuck at 4.58. Her breath became distinct within the ventilator rhythm; the scar on her right wrist warmed again, as if an invisible current lightly seared it.  

She did not press it.  

The Chipped Enamel Bowl remained on the bedside table, chip still leftward; the nutrient paste had cooled into a thin skin, a faint oil sheen on its surface. Zhou Gui set the spoon down slowly; metal met porcelain with a soft click, like the end of a ritual.  

She fished the old phone from her pocket — screen spider‑webbed with scratches, corners worn smooth, fingerprint smudges leaving a hazy glow. The dial screen held a single number — the Remote Care Package’s sole service hotline. Her fingertip hovered over the green call button; the display read “Calling,” backed by the fan’s whir and the rooster’s crow from Mother’s voice note, the fan blades slicing air and the rooster’s piercing cry weaving a rough duet.  

Beep—beep—  

The Wrist Terminal kept billing; rating unchanged. “Calling” blinked, no answer. Zhou Gui’s fingertip did not descend, frozen above the call key like an untuned string stretched tight in the pale morning light.  

A busy tone sounded again, followed by a brief electronic chirp, then a metallic male voice: “Zhou Gui, Care Package ID 20260714‑003, confirm Identity Upgrade; press ‘1’ within ten seconds to complete device unbinding.” The tone was cold, devoid of temperature, as if transmitted straight from a distant server into the eardrum.  

Zhou Gui’s heart missed a beat; her fingertip tightened, nails digging into her palm, a bead of sweat tracing the lines toward her wrist. She heard Lu Lao’s ventilator accelerate, the hiss now laced with a frantic gasp, like a signal about to snap.  

“Press ‘1’.” Lu Lao’s voice rolled in his throat, edged with an unyielding rasp. “Don’t let the system decide for you.”  

Zhou Gui’s thumb hovered over the screen, fingertip touching cold glass, fingerprint smudges shimmering in the glow. She closed her eyes; fan, rooster, ventilator hiss, wrist‑terminal buzz merged into a low roar that made her eardrums ache. Ten seconds ticked silently on the Wrist Terminal; the red “48 h” burned on her retina.  

Her thumb finally fell; the screen flashed a green “1” then dimmed. Synthetic voice: “Device unbinding confirmed; reclamation procedure initiated.” The Wrist Terminal’s vibration frequency spiked to every ten seconds; rating remained frozen at 4.58.  

Lu Lao’s finger finally pressed “Confirm” on the tablet; a green ripple spread, the blockchain write‑complete tone slicing the air again. His fingertip released, the blanched nail imprint lingering faintly on the screen.  

Zhou Gui pocketed the phone, fingers brushing the coarse fabric. She looked up; morning light slipped through the curtain gap, catching the oil sheen on the Chipped Enamel Bowl and the veins mapping Lu Lao’s withered hand, blue rivers about to dry.  

“Go.” Lu Lao said low, voice stripped of sorrow or joy, only the calm of wind‑dried wood. “The retrieval truck arrives at three; remember the charger.”  

Zhou Gui nodded, silent. She bent, retrieved the charger from the bedside, cable coiling around her fingers; the cold metal plug pricked her palm. She turned, footsteps whispering on the gray‑white tiles, each step stitching the two dawns together once more.  

Stepping out of the ward, the corridor’s antiseptic scent clung like a thin film inside her nose, mingling with stale diaper odor and the distant sour tang of pickles from the cafeteria. Her steps on the tiles produced a soft “sh‑sh” — each one driving the Wrist Terminal’s vibration deeper into bone. The terminal’s red countdown still ticked silently; vibration shifted from ten‑second to five‑second intervals, a faint current crawling up her forearm like a fine needle threading a vein.  

The elevator doors slid open; a gust of cold air carried a hint of machine oil. She entered, pressed “1” — the metal button still warm from the previous passenger’s fingertip. Fluorescent tubes buzzed, their ends flickering a weak violet halo that deepened the shadows under her eyes. Only a cleaner in blue overalls occupied the car, clutching a black trash bag, the knot tightening white knuckles. He glanced at her, eyes pausing on the Wrist Terminal for less than a second, then resumed polishing the handrail with mechanical precision.  

“Zhou Gui, retrieval truck at three, don’t forget the charger.” Lu Lao’s voice came from behind, sandpaper on metal. She did not turn; only as the doors closed she heard his cane thudding dully on the floor, each “thud‑thud” tightening her chest.  

The elevator stopped at ground level; doors opened onto a blast of hot wind laden with dust, mixed with the acrid bite of exhaust from the elevated highway. Zhou Gui exited the lobby; infrared sensors on the automatic doors chirped, a green sweep crossing her Wrist Terminal — the screen still blazed “48 h,” rating still pinned at 4.58, as if time had been nailed to a single sheet.  

A self‑service kiosk in the lobby pulsed a dim blue interface, scrolling “Device Reclamation Confirmation,” “Data Upload in Progress,” “Credit Settlement.” She inserted the charger into the USB‑C port; a soft “click” echoed, coolness creeping up her hand along the cable. The kiosk flashed green: “Charger identified; reclamation phase two entered; proceed to designated collection point within ten minutes.”  

Ten minutes. She glanced at her wrist; vibration now every three seconds, sweat beads sliding beneath the strap. The lobby PA intoned a smooth synthetic female: “Respected user, your Care Package has been unbound; retrieval vehicle will arrive at 15:00 sharp; please bring all accessories to Gate 1.” The voice carried no warmth, as if piped straight from server to eardrum, bypassing the brain to settle on skin.  

Zhou Gui clenched the charger cable, knuckles white. She walked toward Gate 1; each tile underfoot emitted a faint “squeak‑squeak,” a low‑grade heartbeat. The automatic rolling shutter was half‑open, revealing a gray sky and a white box‑van parked outside, “智护回收” in red on its side; the roof antenna swayed, emitting a soft “rustle‑rustle” of metal on wind.  

The van’s side door slid; a young man in gray overalls jumped down, handheld scanner in hand. The red beam swept her Wrist Terminal; green code scrolled, then “Reclamation Confirmed.” He said nothing, merely handed her a black plastic crate, its lid barcoded, edges worn to expose white polymer fibers.  

“Put the charger in, sign.” His voice was flat, tinged with impatient nasal tone. Zhou Gui placed the charger inside; fingertips touched the cold metal base, sweat instantly absorbed. She scrawled her name on the electronic pen; a pale blue trace flared then vanished.  

“Done.” He reclaimed the crate, hit the door‑close button; the van shuddered, engine a low thunder rolling away. Zhou Gui stood still, wind lifting stray strands to her damp forehead. The Wrist Terminal’s vibration finally ceased; the screen displayed only a static “4.58,” the red countdown zeroed, a green “Reclamation Complete” icon flashing once then fading.  

She turned; footsteps echoed across the empty plaza, each step treading the ritual into dust. A distant rooster crowed again, high and fractured, piercing the highway roar, slipping into her ear like a fine needle lodged in the pale morning light.  

Zhou Gui paused at the bus stop, pulled out the old phone, scrolled to the top contact, pressed call.  

The screen lit, a line of gray text rotating at the bottom: **Calling**.  

She held the phone to her ear, the other hand in her pocket, fingertip pressing the rim of the Chipped Enamel Bowl — its cool base climbing through the nail gaps, reminding her the bowl no longer belonged to any meal.  

She did not redial. The Wrist Terminal resumed its steady beep‑beep inside the cuff — fifteen hundred hours, retrieval truck at Gate 1; before that, she still had four hours of Caregiver work orders to fulfill.  

Far off, the retrieval van had already started; exhaust curled into white ribbons in the cold air, dispersing on the wind. She quickened her pace; the Wrist Terminal’s countdown jumped from 4:58 to 4:57, urging her toward the next mandatory “Care.”
